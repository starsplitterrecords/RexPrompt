ACT TWO

INT. PLATFORM SUB-LEVEL — CONTINUOUS

Rae hangs from a service ladder beneath the platform.

Wind tears through the open structure.

The refugee transport is visible through gaps in the metal, trying again to approach Vael-Tor.

Rae reaches a second manual release.

Her hand closes around it.

MERO
Don't.

Rae freezes.

General Mero stands on the platform below her.

No light announces him.

No machine produces him.

He is simply part of the place for as long as the memory holds.

RAE
You're not here.

MERO
You used to know better than to waste time on that distinction.

Rae climbs down to the platform.

MERO
The barrier is doing what it was built to do.

RAE
There are families on that ship.

MERO
There were families everywhere.

Rae looks at him.

MERO
That did not make the choices disappear.

RAE
It should have made them harder.

MERO
They were hard.

RAE
You don't sound like it.

Mero looks out at the transport.

MERO
If the platform falls, everyone below it dies with the ship.

RAE
So you sacrifice the ship.

MERO
You know this.

RAE
No.

MERO
You did.

Something in Rae's face tightens.

A memory arrives.

Not an image.

A feeling.

Standing beside someone in a chamber while voices argued about a city.

Knowing exactly which people would not be evacuated.

Rae grips the rail.

RAE
Who was I?

Mero looks at her with recognition.

MERO
You were the one who asked how many could still be moved.

RAE
That's not a name.

MERO
No.

RAE
Did you know me?

MERO
Yes.

Rae closes her eyes.

MERO
Open them.

She does.

MERO
The ship is still coming.

The refugee transport drops through the storm.

RAE
You wrote this place.

MERO
I wrote what survived.

RAE
There's a difference.

MERO
Not to the dead.

Rae turns back to the release.

MERO
Leave it.

RAE
No.

MERO
You remember what happens when you choose one ship over a city.

Rae takes hold of the release.

RAE
Then this time I get to remember choosing differently.

She pulls.

The old mechanism groans.

Mero disappears.

Rae keeps pulling.

EXT. SKIFF LOADING DOCK — LATER

Theo places his travel papers on RYN'S counter.

Ryn checks the papers.

Then the satchel.

RYN
Open it.

THEO
No.

RYN
That's a very short trip.

THEO
I'm told brevity is a virtue.

RYN
Not at my dock.

Ryn reaches for the satchel.

Theo pulls it back.

RYN
You want passage, I see the bag.

Theo hesitates.

Then opens the flap enough for Ryn to see the wrapped shard.

Ryn's expression changes.

RYN
Absolutely not.

THEO
You don't even know what it is.

RYN
Correct. That is why it is not getting on my ship.

THEO
I'm already on your platform.

RYN
A problem I am correcting.

Ryn reaches for his comm.

The glass counter between them goes dark.

Not broken.

Dark like a window at night.

A reflection appears in it.

A colossal armored prow rising through sand.

Ryn looks over his shoulder.

Nothing behind him.

He looks back at the glass.

The warship is closer.

For a moment, Theo sees himself reflected in front of it wearing a uniform he has never owned.

Ryn drops the comm.

The reflection disappears.

RYN
Take it and leave.

THEO
I was trying to.

RYN
No. I mean leave Vael-Tor.

He opens the departure gate.

THEO
You don't want to report this?

RYN
I don't want to remember it tomorrow.

Theo stops.

RYN
Go.

Theo picks up the satchel.

RYN
And if that thing makes my walls do anything else, I'm charging you for repairs.

Theo almost laughs.

THEO
Fair.

He leaves through the open gate.

INT. OBSERVATION DECK — CONTINUOUS

The refugee transport is in trouble.

Everyone in the observation room can see that without a display explaining it.

Its hull is damaged. It cannot climb away from Vael-Tor. The old defense barrier glows faintly in the storm ahead of it.

VYN stands at the command console.

His hand is on a red authorization plate.

Rae enters.

RAE
Take your hand off that.

VYN
No.

RAE
There are people on that ship.

VYN
I know.

RAE
Then don't kill them.

VYN
If that hull breaks against the barrier, it comes down on the lower platforms.

RAE
Open the barrier.

VYN
It isn't answering us.

RAE
Then stop treating murder like the only control that still works.

Vyn turns toward her.

VYN
You think I want to do this?

RAE
I think wanting has nothing to do with what your hand is about to do.

The transport rolls in the storm.

Vyn looks at it.

VYN
If I wait too long, I don't get to choose anymore.

RAE
Good.

VYN
What?

RAE
Maybe you shouldn't get to choose for everyone aboard.

Vyn presses down.

Rae grabs the authorization unit and rips it free from the console.

A shower of sparks.

The red light dies.

Vyn stares at her.

VYN
What did you do?

RAE
Made your decision harder.

VYN
The barrier is still there.

RAE
I know.

VYN
And now we can't destroy the ship before it hits.

Rae looks out at the transport.

RAE
Then I have to open the barrier.

VYN
You can't.

RAE
I've heard that a lot this week.

She runs for the exit.

Vyn watches her go.

Then looks at the dead authorization plate in his hand.

EXT. STATION PROMENADE — NIGHT

Rain sweeps across the Vael-Tor promenade.

Theo moves with the crowd toward the outbound gates.

VORKEN steps into his path.

Theo stops.

VORKEN
Bag.

THEO
No.

VORKEN
That wasn't a negotiation.

THEO
I noticed.

Vorken grabs the satchel strap.

Theo grabs it back.

Vorken pulls Theo close.

VORKEN
Let go.

Theo looks at the man's hand.

Then his elbow.

Then his throat.

His body moves.

Fast.

Too fast for a junior attaché who has spent his adult life drawing maps.

Theo turns under Vorken's arm, traps the wrist, drives the man against a pillar and puts his forearm across Vorken's throat.

Vorken's feet lose the ground.

Theo holds him there.

Vorken claws at Theo's sleeve.

Theo's face is empty.

MERO (V.O.)
Finish it.

Theo blinks.

He sees what he is doing.

He drops Vorken.

The man collapses into the rain, coughing.

THEO
I'm sorry.

VORKEN
What the hell are you?

Theo backs away.

The satchel has opened during the struggle.

The shard lies exposed.

The rain stops touching the ground around it.

Not because it evaporates.

Because for a moment the promenade is somewhere else.

A shadow moves across the crowd.

Everyone looks up.

A gigantic warship passes overhead.

Silent.

Close enough to blot out the city.

There is nothing in the real sky.

People scream anyway.

The shadow passes.

The present returns.

Rain hits the pavement again.

No one moves.

Theo kneels and covers the shard.

When he looks up, hundreds of strangers are staring at him.

Not at a fugitive.

At proof.

Theo shoulders the bag and walks away.

No one tries to stop him.

END OF ACT TWO
