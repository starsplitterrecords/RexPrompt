ACT TWO

INT. CARGO BAY DOORS — CONTINUOUS

Theo emerges from the maintenance shaft into a freight bay.

He gets three steps.

TARN steps into the only open exit.

Heavy municipal armor. Baton in one hand. No theatrics.

TARN
Hands where I can see them.

Theo raises one hand.

The other stays near his coat.

TARN
Both.

THEO
I am trying.

TARN
Try harder.

Theo slowly brings out the wrapped shard.

Tarn sees it.

TARN
Put it down.

Theo looks at the floor.

His thumb finds his index finger.

The missing stylus.

His shoulders change.

Not dramatically.

Enough.

When he looks back at Tarn, something in his face is older.

THEO
Stand aside.

Tarn actually laughs.

TARN
Excuse me?

Theo hears boots behind him.

The bay stretches.

The freight stacks are gone.

For a heartbeat, he is standing in a smoke-filled corridor lined with wounded civilians.

A red emergency banner hangs from a ceiling that no longer exists.

People rush toward him.

Through him.

Theo's voice is not louder when he speaks again.

It does not need to be.

THEO
The living move first.

Tarn's face changes.

He hears it too.

Hundreds of running feet.

A woman crying somewhere behind him.

A command bell.

The modern bay returns.

Tarn looks over his shoulder.

Nothing there.

TARN
Who taught you to say that?

Theo's borrowed composure cracks.

THEO
I don't know.

Tarn studies him.

TARN
What are you?

Theo looks at the wrapped shard in his hand.

Then at the officer.

THEO
Right now? Scared.

That honesty does what the command voice could not.

Tarn steps aside.

Theo passes him.

TARN
I'm supposed to stop you.

Theo keeps walking.

THEO
I know.

TARN
If I see you again, I will.

THEO
I know that too.

Theo reaches the corridor.

His posture collapses as soon as Tarn can no longer see him.

He braces one hand against the wall.

THEO
The living move first.

He says it again, quietly.

This time it sounds foreign.

INT. CENTRAL PROCESSING CORE — LATER

A sealed archive door fills the wall.

Tallis stands in front of it.

Adrian stands several steps away.

The young archivist from before watches from the edge of the room, wishing he were anywhere else.

On a small ledge beside the door rests an old ceremonial blade.

TALLIS
You said you wouldn't touch me.

ADRIAN
I haven't.

TALLIS
You don't have to.

Adrian looks at the blade.

Tallis follows his eyes.

TALLIS
No.

ADRIAN
Open it.

TALLIS
No.

ADRIAN
Tallis.

TALLIS
The seal exists because people before us decided remembering was worse.

ADRIAN
They decided for everyone who came after them.

TALLIS
So will you.

Adrian looks at him.

The answer takes longer than it should.

ADRIAN
Probably.

Tallis lets out a disbelieving breath.

TALLIS
At least you know what you're doing.

ADRIAN
No.

Adrian looks toward the sealed door.

ADRIAN
I know what I am repeating.

Tallis picks up the blade.

His hand shakes.

The ARCHIVIST takes a step forward.

ARCHIVIST
You don't have to do this.

TALLIS
Yes, I do.

He looks directly at Adrian.

TALLIS
That's the problem.

Adrian almost tells him to stop.

Doesn't.

Tallis draws the blade across his palm.

A shallow cut. Human. Ugly.

He presses his hand against the old seal.

The archive door opens.

Behind it are not glowing machines.

Shelves.

Boxes.

Books wrapped in black cloth.

Names written by hand.

Adrian stares.

Tallis cradles his cut hand.

TALLIS
Was it worth it?

ADRIAN
I haven't looked yet.

TALLIS
You already did the part I'm asking about.

Adrian looks at him.

No answer.

He enters the archive.

INT. SUBSPACE COMM ARRAY — CONTINUOUS

Rae is lower in the relay station now, beside an open communications pit.

Kyn holds a work lamp while she keeps the radio line patched through an old manual board.

A maintenance drone floats beside them.

DRONE
Evacuation required.

RAE
Noted.

DRONE
Evacuation required.

KYN
It's very persuasive.

RAE
I'm considering its argument.

DRONE
Evacuation required.

Rae reaches up, turns the drone around and sends it gliding gently toward the corridor.

RAE
Considered.

Kyn almost smiles.

Then the speaker fills with voices.

Not static.

Voices.

A crowd running through a place neither of them can see.

WOMAN'S VOICE (V.O.)
North gate is gone. Take the children below.

MAN'S VOICE (V.O.)
There is no below.

WOMAN'S VOICE (V.O.)
Then make one.

Rae looks at Kyn.

He hears every word.

Another voice rises over them.

WOMAN'S VOICE (V.O.)
The living move first.

Rae freezes.

KYN
That isn't Theo.

RAE
I know.

KYN
Who is it?

RAE
Listen.

The forgotten voices keep moving through the room.

Some are giving names.

Some are praying.

Some are arguing about who gets through a gate.

Then one voice speaks a word Rae has never heard awake.

The instant she hears it, she knows what it means.

REFUGE.

Not the modern word.

The older one.

Rae whispers it back.

RAE
Avar.

The entire channel falls silent.

Kyn stares at her.

KYN
What did you say?

RAE
The name of the mark.

KYN
What mark?

Rae traces the bent line inside the broken circle against her thigh.

KYN
You know that phrase too?

RAE
I know the symbol that goes with it.

KYN
From the archive?

RAE
From before I ever saw the archive.

The radio clicks.

Theo's voice returns.

THEO (V.O.)
Rae?

Rae grabs the handset.

RAE
I'm here.

THEO (V.O.)
I heard something.

Rae and Kyn look at each other.

RAE
So did we.

INT. ABANDONED MAG-LEV CARRIAGE — LATER

Theo slides shut the door of an abandoned transit car.

Darkness.

He sits on the floor and unwraps the shard.

It gives off almost no light.

Theo stares at it.

THEO
The living move first.

The carriage changes.

No flash.

No projection.

The torn seats become a long command table.

Rain on the windows becomes distant bombardment.

The carriage is still here.

So is another room.

GENERAL MERO stands at the far end of the table.

Theo knows his name before the man speaks.

MERO
Report.

Theo is already standing.

He hates that.

THEO
I don't report to you.

MERO
You did.

Theo looks around.

Officers stand along the walls.

Some are bleeding.

None of them look at Theo directly.

THEO
Who are you?

MERO
You know.

Theo's mouth moves before he decides to answer.

THEO
Mero.

The General's expression softens.

That is worse than anger.

MERO
There you are.

Theo looks down.

The absent stylus is in his hand.

Real enough to feel.

A map lies across the table.

A city divided by fire.

MERO
The lower gates are lost.

Theo knows which gates before he looks.

MERO
Seal them.

THEO
There are people below.

MERO
Yes.

THEO
How many?

Mero looks at him.

Not because the number is hard.

Because the question is irrelevant.

MERO
Enough.

Theo looks at the map.

His hand moves toward the stylus mark that will close the lower district.

He stops himself.

THEO
Did I do this before?

MERO
You knew what the choice cost.

THEO
I don't.

MERO
You remember enough.

Theo looks toward the phantom officers.

They are waiting for him.

Not Mero.

Him.

THEO
They're not my people to spend.

MERO
They were.

Theo's eyes lift.

THEO
Not now.

Mero steps closer.

MERO
You think the dead become less yours because time passed?

THEO
I think the living don't belong to you.

MERO
And if refusing costs more lives?

Theo looks at the map.

For one terrible moment, he can feel why the old decision was made.

Not cruelty.

Necessity.

Or what people call necessity when they cannot bear the alternatives.

Theo drops the stylus.

THEO
Then I live with refusing.

Mero looks at him with something like recognition.

MERO
You said that once too.

The command room disappears.

Theo is back inside the dead carriage.

Alone.

The shard lies on the floor.

Theo backs away from it.

Then he sees the mark beside his knee.

A black handprint burned into the carriage floor.

Theo touches it.

Cold.

No fire.

No explanation.

He looks at his own hand.

THEO
Mero.

Nothing answers.

A municipal searchlight passes over the carriage window.

Theo keeps one hand on the impossible mark until the light is gone.

He is not calming himself with proof anymore.

He is trying to understand whether the past just remembered a conversation differently because he refused it.

END OF ACT TWO
