# Stardust Station — Dialogue Development, Issues 08–10
**Working character-first scenes | Unreleased dialogue exploration | 2026-10-10**

The Liaison is a credible professional, not a bureaucratic villain. Institutional pressures remain real and technical consequences remain bounded. These are **voice-first story scenes** awaiting placement in fixed page art, not a replacement for visual canon or approved lettering.

---

## Issue 08 — The Corporate Visit

### Scene A: The tour they actually take
*Docking vestibule. A StarTrust Operations Liaison arrives with one small case. Jax is the designated guide. Astra is doing her best to look casual about it.*

**ASTRA:** We're very pleased to welcome you.

**LIAISON:** Thank you.

**ASTRA:** Jax has the route. It's brief, but you'll see how we operate.

**JAX:** The route is brief. The station is less brief.

**LIAISON:** I brought comfortable shoes.

**JAX:** You're already ahead of most of our visitors.

**ASTRA:** I can take over at any time.

**LIAISON:** I'm happy with Jax.

*Astra smiles a fraction too hard and lets them leave.*

**LIAISON:** Do you get visitors often?

**JAX:** It depends what you count as a visitor. Last month a transport driver came in looking for a charging cable and stayed for lunch. People still mention him.

**LIAISON:** Why?

**JAX:** He said the vending-machine curry was good.

**LIAISON:** Is it?

**JAX:** The vending machine has a lot of confidence.

*They pass a work area. Glorp has laid out a neat set of records on a counter; Kreeb is attempting to straighten the nearby chairs.*

**LIAISON:** Who's that?

**JAX:** Glorp. Knows where every physical record is, including things that probably don't exist anymore.

**LIAISON:** And the one moving the chairs?

**JAX:** Kreeb. They're good friends. There's a chair arrangement incident I wouldn't bring up unless you have an hour.

**LIAISON:** Do they work well together?

**JAX:** Yes. It's the way they live together that concerns the furniture.

*Glorp looks up as they pass.*

**GLORP:** He's exaggerating.

**JAX:** I said nothing about the dinner party.

**GLORP:** That's what I meant.

**LIAISON:** I feel I'm missing a very good story.

**JAX:** That's most of our operations.

*They turn into the service corridor. An ordinary panel is open and Zib is looking at a manifold gauge.*

**LIAISON:** Is this on the tour?

**JAX:** It is now.

**ZIB:** You couldn't have picked tomorrow?

**JAX:** Tomorrow we'd have to see something else.

*Jax helps Zib set a barrier at the actual working clearance without pausing to explain his competence.*

### Scene B: A visitor who asks the wrong interesting question
*After the service-corridor repair. Mira is packing the test samples in the lab while Astra and the Liaison review the work record. The bounded residue observation is visible but not magically understood.*

**LIAISON:** I was expecting a tour of your material work.

**MIRA:** You got one.

**LIAISON:** Briefly.

**MIRA:** I prepared a much longer one.

**ASTRA:** She had photographs.

**MIRA:** Photographs are useful.

**LIAISON:** I noticed Jax knew the service corridor was about to be inaccessible before the alert reached this display.

**MIRA:** He knows Zib. They make the same argument every Tuesday.

**LIAISON:** That isn't what I asked.

**MIRA:** I know. I'm resisting the urge to give you my longer presentation.

**ASTRA:** I spent three days preparing it for you.

**LIAISON:** I would like to read it. But the repair I watched was more persuasive than a rehearsed demonstration.

**MIRA:** There was a material sample in that repair, too.

**LIAISON:** Yes. You tested it separately, and you told me where it failed.

**MIRA:** You're not disappointed?

**LIAISON:** About the failure data? No. A scientist who tells me only what succeeded usually expects me to pay for a surprise.

*Mira looks at Astra. It isn't often someone says exactly the right thing about her work.*

**MIRA:** You'd get on well with Zib.

**ASTRA:** He'd spend the whole visit showing you the broken things.

**LIAISON:** I like broken things that people know how to fix.

**MIRA:** We have quite a selection.

**ASTRA:** It's a very well-maintained selection.

*The Liaison nearly laughs. Astra realizes they have finally stopped performing a tour.*

## Issue 09 — Output Target

### Scene A: The calendar belongs to someone
*The output target has arrived with a tempting promise of expedited parts. Astra suggests moving a legitimate service check forward. Zib has the maintenance calendar open. Jax is in and out of the room collecting tools.*

**ASTRA:** We'd be doing the same work, only sooner.

**ZIB:** I understand how sooner works.

**ASTRA:** Then what's the problem?

**ZIB:** The day we were planning to do it was open. Tomorrow isn't.

**ASTRA:** We have people.

**ZIB:** They're people with other jobs. Jax has the Dock Two handoff. Mira is testing a component. I'm meeting a ship with a bracket that took six weeks to get here.

**ASTRA:** The bracket that's actually nineteen days late?

**ZIB:** It managed to be both. Procurement is talented.

**ASTRA:** This target could keep that from happening.

**ZIB:** Or it could make us unavailable when the part finally arrives.

*Jax comes in with a tool tote and pauses at hearing his name.*

**JAX:** What am I unavailable for?

**ZIB:** Your own job.

**JAX:** That's usually my favorite kind of unavailable.

**ASTRA:** We need to bring a service check forward.

**JAX:** Which one?

**ASTRA:** The vibration check on the loading conveyor.

**JAX:** Tomorrow? No. Tomorrow that conveyor has a ship at the other end of it.

**ASTRA:** It would be finished before the ship arrives.

**JAX:** According to which clock?

**ASTRA:** According to the schedule.

**JAX:** Oh, good. The schedule's a lot more confident than I am.

**ZIB:** The job is legitimate. I'm not telling you it can't be done. I'm telling you why we put it where we put it.

**ASTRA:** You think I'm doing this for the number.

**ZIB:** I think you want the parts. I want them, too.

*They look at the replacement-part backlog. Nobody gets to be an idiot.*

**JAX:** If it helps, I won't complain when the crate shows up.

**ZIB:** You'll complain about the crate.

**JAX:** I'll complain about the packaging. There's a difference.

**ASTRA:** Do the check tomorrow. I'll take responsibility for shifting the other work.

**ZIB:** You can take responsibility for the decision. We still have to carry it.

*That line should sit; it isn't a slogan. They leave with jobs still to perform.*

### Scene B: Dock Two doesn't care about targets
*The early service has occupied the cargo lane. A real arrival is now waiting. Everyone is hurried but capable; the work of clearing the lane continues throughout the conversation.*

**JAX:** They're waiting at the outer latch.

**ZIB:** I know. I can hear the pilot asking about the delay.

**ASTRA:** How long to clear the conveyor?

**ZIB:** If we don't stop to discuss it, not very.

**JAX:** The receiving crew are asking whether this is an emergency.

**ASTRA:** Tell them no.

**JAX:** I told them we're conducting an efficiency exercise.

**ZIB:** You didn't.

**JAX:** I didn't. They were angry enough already.

*Astra looks at the equipment occupying the cargo lane.*

**ASTRA:** You warned me.

**ZIB:** That's not useful information at the moment.

**ASTRA:** I know. You don't need to be polite about it.

**ZIB:** This is me being polite. I've kept my thoughts about the calendar to myself.

**JAX:** He's been muttering them into the toolbox.

**ZIB:** The toolbox is a good listener.

**ASTRA:** We finish the isolation safely, clear the lane, and take the shipment. I'll explain to central why the output number drops.

**JAX:** Do we have to explain it to the ship too?

**ASTRA:** No.

**JAX:** Lovely. They're my least favorite audience.

*Pixa calls with a revised arrival window; Zib checks an interlock rather than shortcutting it.*

**ZIB:** Jax. Confirm Dock Two is isolated before you pull that panel.

**JAX:** It is. I have it right here.

**ASTRA:** Anything I can actually do?

**JAX:** Yes. Tell the pilot five minutes. Not "soon." Not "as quickly as possible." Five minutes.

**ASTRA:** And if it takes six?

**JAX:** He'll be annoyed for one minute. That's manageable.

*For once, Astra performs the smallest, most useful job in the room.*

## Issue 10 — The Station Stays Open

### Scene A: A very ungrand proposal
*Operations hub. Central review has offered institutional choices that do not describe how Stardust actually works. Astra, Mira, Zib, Glorp and Kreeb have been looking at the proposal in fragments while doing their ordinary jobs.*

**ASTRA:** I need a description of what this station does that they will actually fund.

**ZIB:** It works.

**ASTRA:** I suspect they want more than that.

**ZIB:** It works *most days.*

**MIRA:** That's not making it better.

**ZIB:** It's more accurate.

**GLORP:** If you're looking for a more accurate term, "operationally adaptive" has been used before.

**ASTRA:** By whom?

**GLORP:** Us. Two months ago. You asked me to remove it because it sounded like a brochure.

**ASTRA:** I remember the brochure. There were seven fonts.

**KREEB:** And a drawing of everyone smiling in front of the station.

**ZIB:** I had four hands in that drawing. Two were holding wrenches.

**MIRA:** I quite liked it.

**ZIB:** You were floating over the lab with a sample that doesn't float.

**MIRA:** Artistic license.

**ASTRA:** That is precisely what we cannot afford on this report.

*Pixa enters with an unembellished operating log, placing it beside the sealed physical samples.*

**PIXA:** I can supply what actually happened.

**GLORP:** We have many records of what actually happened.

**PIXA:** Then we can choose a shorter set that tells the truth.

**ASTRA:** That's the first proposal today I can use.

**KREEB:** What about the people? I don't want them to think we replaced them with a good log.

**ASTRA:** They aren't going to think that.

**KREEB:** Do you know that?

*Astra begins to answer. She stops.*

**ASTRA:** No. I don't. I'll tell them what the crew actually does. We have the work history to support it.

**MIRA:** And the material?

**ASTRA:** We describe what we can reproduce. Not what we'd like to reproduce.

**MIRA:** I'm going to complain about the wording.

**ASTRA:** I fully expect you to.

**ZIB:** That's reassuring. At least one part of the station is working as designed.

*Glorp adjusts the position of the cup that has been sitting near the records. Kreeb moves it back to where its owner can reach. Neither speaks. They are still Glorp and Kreeb.*

### Scene B: The invitation after the decision
*After central approves a truthful operating model and the station stays open. Break room; nobody is staging a celebration. Astra has stopped by for tea. A vending machine has inconveniently swallowed a purchase. Kreeb is setting out a game box for an evening gathering.*

**JAX:** I've put in a request for the vending machine.

**ZIB:** You told me five minutes ago.

**JAX:** It's been a productive five minutes.

**ZIB:** You told me. You've told Pixa. Now you've written it down. That's three reports for one sandwich.

**JAX:** I paid for it.

**GLORP:** You also paid for the previous three.

**JAX:** I was testing whether it was a pattern.

**MIRA:** You could've bought a meal with that money.

**JAX:** I was trying to.

*Kreeb sets the game box on the table. Glorp sees it and pulls a face.*

**GLORP:** Oh, no. Not that game.

**KREEB:** You said you'd play.

**GLORP:** I said I would come.

**KREEB:** Nobody wants to sit there while you explain the scoring.

**GLORP:** If the scoring were self-explanatory, I wouldn't have to.

**PIXA:** I've learned the rules.

**MIRA:** All of them?

**PIXA:** Yes. Several contradict one another.

**GLORP:** Thank you.

**KREEB:** That's not an endorsement.

*The vending machine dispenses a sandwich at last. Jax retrieves it triumphantly. A second sandwich drops behind it.*

**JAX:** Oh.

**ZIB:** There. It knew you were coming.

**JAX:** Well, now I'm in profit.

**ASTRA:** Are you all playing tonight?

**KREEB:** Yes. You're welcome to come.

*Astra considers whether the invitation has been offered out of obligation.*

**ASTRA:** Are you sure? I don't want it to feel like a staff event.

**KREEB:** It won't. I'm not going to make you fill out anything.

**GLORP:** You might want to bring your own chair.

**KREEB:** Glorp.

**GLORP:** What? Some of them are claimed.

*Kreeb remembers the label underneath the bright chair from Issue 02. Glorp notices that Kreeb remembers. They share the tiniest conspiratorial look.*

**ASTRA:** I'm terrible at card games.

**MIRA:** Perfect. So is Jax.

**JAX:** I play beautifully. I just like to eat while I'm doing it.

**PIXA:** I would prefer if everybody stopped talking about the scores before we've dealt the cards.

**GLORP:** Finally, someone with principles.

*They settle around an unremarkable table. The station stays open outside the window. Nobody announces a lesson. Jax reaches for the extra sandwich and Glorp slaps his hand away.*

### Reader-facing audit for 08–10
- The Liaison has independent opinions and can appreciate an ordinary repaired system and a messy human team.
- The season's material discovery does not become an emotional oracle or an automatically reproducible business model.
- Astra's bad Issue 09 decision is credible because the station urgently needs parts and central review can actually fund them.
- Issue 10's proposal is not the emotional climax; the relationships are. The ending returns to the social life planted in Issue 02.
- Running threads (Glorp's invitation, Jax's selective generosity, Astra's distance, Pixa's belonging, Kreeb's chair) pay off through new circumstances, not compulsory catchphrases.
- Approved artwork stays fixed until separately examined. These dialogue scenes must be compressed and mapped only where visible action supports them.
