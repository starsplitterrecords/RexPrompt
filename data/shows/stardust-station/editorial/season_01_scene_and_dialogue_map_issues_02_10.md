# Stardust Station — Season One Scene and Dialogue Map
**Issues 02–10 | Working, not released | 2026-10-10**

This is a **scene-level conversational reconstruction** of the nine unreleased stories using the established issue premises, page counts, continuity, and character identities. It sits alongside the voice-written scene packets and does not overwrite approved artwork or final production lettering. Page ranges are editorial targets for natural conversations; adapt speech to the visible fixed panels before committing exact balloons. **Issue 01 remains released canon.**

The change from the current production scripts: each episode has more than a problem, a process response, and a correct managerial conclusion. It has an active social story with consequences independent of the weekly crisis. Nobody starts a scene to deliver the theme. Not all coworkers are present just because they have a recognizable quirk.

---

## Issue 02 — The Break Room Is Not a Department (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** Glorp missed an evening at Kreeb's; it hurts more than Glorp wants to admit. Kreeb feels unfairly rebuffed after inviting them. Jax's contradictory off-hours generosity becomes evidence in a separate dispute. The microwave is merely inconvenient, and Astra cannot solve anybody's feelings by tidying the room.

- **P01–03 | A peculiar morning.** Jax wants to heat soup. Kreeb wants the room to be welcoming; Glorp wants people to notice it was made usable *before* Kreeb re-arranged it. Noola wants the chair returned to her quiet window. Let the first barbs be about material things; the reader has to see the stakes before hearing about the dinner.
- **P04–06 | The first social crack.** Talk turns to who stayed last night and why. Kreeb mentions the gathering naturally in defense of having asked Glorp to leave the cleanup. Glorp gets one sharp, unexpected piece of news: Jax washed Kreeb's dishes. Jax, present for his soup, is delighted to learn someone finally respects his talents. Mira's dinner food is mentioned by the friends who ate it, not a narrator.
- **P07–09 | The appliance stops being an excuse.** Zib finds a mundane blocked vent and loose ground and makes a competent repair. Glorp sees the notices come down and hears a second dismissal of yesterday's work; Zib handles the physical job without staging a tribunal. Jax is frustrated because he is watching adults move objects around the only working microwave.
- **P10–12 | What people were really doing.** Pixa and Brick are in the shared room for their own reasons, not to deliver a score and a noir verdict. Kreeb realizes Glorp missed more than dinner; Mira's sample of residue stays small and physical. The reader hears that Glorp came to Kreeb's door at midnight but did not knock. Glorp can laugh at themselves without ceasing to be irritated.
- **P13–16 | Social friction without a trial.** Glorp and Kreeb keep getting in one another's way around mugs, chairs and trays because each thinks the other is denying something. Noola is amused until they block her kettle. Jax warms the soup because he is hungry, not because he has learned a reporting lesson.
- **P17–19 | Accidental cooperation.** Kreeb sees Glorp wanted company but did not want to demand it. Glorp sees Kreeb saved them food. They don't have to confess this in neat symmetrical speeches: a mug repositioned without comment or a saved plate can do the work. First normal microwave cycle triggers the absurd obsolete Form 8C printer; Glorp gets sentimental about the paper, Kreeb unexpectedly protects it.
- **P20–22 | Still themselves.** Ordinary lunch continues; Astra thanks Glorp for a job she missed without grandly resolving the group. The private chair-labeling tag reveals Kreeb has territorial habits of his own. Glorp offers to laminate the label; Kreeb is mortified. This is the comic button, not a moral.

**Dialogue danger:** If Kreeb repeatedly says "I'm trying to help" and Glorp repeatedly says "you didn't ask," those are still empty positions. Give each the dinner they remember differently, the late-night knock Glorp didn't make, and jokes about Jax's spectacularly selective helpfulness.

## Issue 03 — The Meeting That Wouldn't End (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** Astra wants a short, reassuring professional follow-up, but an undertone of envy slips out when staff refer to off-hours friendships. Zib and Jax each have somewhere more useful to be. Kreeb and Glorp have a promised social rematch that becomes one more thing the meeting postpones.

- **P01–04 | The promised ten minutes.** Astra chairs the meeting briskly. A casual "I heard you all had a nice evening" exposes that she wasn't part of it; her self-consciousness is invisible to herself but visible in how she prolongs conversational transitions. Jax offers opinions before anyone asks.
- **P05–08 | People with other lives.** Zib has a fixed pickup appointment, not a generic dislike of meetings. Kreeb promised Glorp a rematch after dinner; Glorp says it wasn't a *rematch* because the original game wasn't properly scored. Pixa makes a perfectly reasonable social suggestion and inadvertently lengthens the meeting.
- **P09–12 | The official subjects are smaller.** Old Form 8C mapping, access label and a specific follow-up are legitimate. They are not complex enough for a room full of coworkers. Jax agrees to the concrete job but resists accepting a grand "owner" identity; Glorp is genuinely knowledgeable about the legacy trigger.
- **P13–16 | Work proceeds elsewhere.** While the talk continues, Zib and Jax act. Let the others discuss an actual unrelated subject—an awful cafe or a contentious card rule—and then be interrupted by a repaired part or updated label. This should make the meeting laughable without someone pronouncing a productivity lesson.
- **P17–20 | Astra's actual mistake.** She finally realizes that managing the meeting and becoming part of the crew's private conversation are different desires. It does not turn into a confession. Let her end the meeting too abruptly and briefly regret it.
- **P21–22 | An invitation with uncertain status.** A colleague asks Astra about attending something genuinely optional; she considers whether the invitation is politeness or interest. She can remain professionally capable and socially off balance. The series need not resolve her insecurity here.

**Dialogue danger:** Do not turn this into another checklist, metric joke or speech about meeting efficiency. A short meeting is funny when time, appetite, personal habits and private commitments accumulate while nobody notices the room has become the obstruction.

## Issue 04 — Temporary Material (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** The first narrow, accidental usefulness of residue matters, but the emotional story is whether Mira will be treated as an exciting scientist or merely a convenient source of parts—and whether Astra can celebrate her without inviting an institutional takeover.

- **P01–04 | A real need and a delighted curiosity.** Zib needs a temporary conventional fix to a coolant-balancing part with a nineteen-day replacement delay. Mira discovers samples with different physical behavior. Zib knows her excitement well enough to make fun of the number of photographs, not her competence.
- **P05–08 | A test worthy of adults.** Approved clamp hardware carries the load; material fills one limited gap. Jax is good at the job and uses the discovery to joke about the remarkably different reputation he has at Kreeb's home. Nobody calls the residue sentient or makes people perform emotions.
- **P09–12 | When success becomes credit.** The repair holds within defined conditions. Mira wants the work recognized as more than a funny accident. Astra wants precise claims; Glorp wants the right report because the consequences of vague language are real, not because a form is intrinsically funny.
- **P13–16 | Astra and Mira are not ideological representatives.** Their friendship allows bluntness. Mira reminds Astra of her own excitement when arriving at Stardust. Astra admits the practical fear: someone could turn a one-off patch into the station's new mission. Give Mira the pleasure of being right about *one* thing without pretending she won the whole argument.
- **P17–19 | Work that continues after the discovery.** Jax monitors an unglamorous repair interval; Zib trusts him to catch drift. Mira may be talking about dinner while doing careful science: both are part of her life. Astra writes the limited, truthful report and sends it herself.
- **P20–22 | The report travels.** Corporate acknowledgement redirects the station into an unfamiliar institutional category. Let characters respond personally—excitement, dread, "I told you so," resentment of being right—rather than narrating the next storyline. The report reaches the wrong level of attention, but it is not false.

**Dialogue danger:** The discovery must remain understandable through visible apparatus and action. Avoid "science explains feelings" and "scientist explains science in six uninterrupted balloons."

## Issue 05 — Resource Efficiency Review (21 pages)

**Story we watch:** Astra accepts a bounded collection pilot because the station needs a replacement part. Jax takes unusual pride in route work because it has real edges. Pixa experiences the difference between being asked to count a gathering and being invited to one.

- **P01–04 | Incentives have consequences.** Category labels appear, but the real hook is a part delay that matters to Zib. Astra agrees to a pilot and admits to Mira she's hoping for practical help, not corporate praise. Mira can be annoyed while still understanding why.
- **P05–07 | The person who knows the corridors.** Jax volunteers for actual custody and route work. Pixa's optimal map conflicts with lived use and Noola's access rig. Jax isn't transformed into a model employee; he simply likes work he can complete without being watched for meaning.
- **P08–11 | Familiar faces under a new vocabulary.** Brick observes a surprising act of voluntary helpfulness and half jokingly thinks Jax has an alibi. Astra catches herself treating Jax's competence as an exception when it is part of him. One social conversation about an upcoming gathering gives everyone something else to care about.
- **P12–14 | Real repair, incidental finding.** A damper is fixed before sample handling. Mira becomes excited again by a stronger specimen but refuses commercial adjectives. The procurement path shows a benefit but not a guaranteed shipment; nobody celebrates an imaginary part.
- **P15–18 | The additional ask.** Corporate wants wider conditions and contextual data. Characters don't deliver alternating five-point positions; they talk about what would happen to their actual tasks, schedules, friends and usable space. Astra refuses the expanded ask while understanding the cost.
- **P19–21 | After work belongs to people.** Pixa asks to join the next gathering rather than help track it. Mira and Kreeb are surprised to realize they'd never actually said "come." Corporate Wellness Week arrives over the last good moment, interrupting a plan without defeating it.

**Dialogue danger:** A refusal form is not a climax. The action is whose judgment Astra trusts, what costs them, and how the staff speak about that when there is no meeting.

## Issue 06 — Wellness Week (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** A nominally voluntary program occupies a room that has a real damper problem. Kreeb hopes a quiet-room provision will be useful; Noola wants privacy and stable suit conditions, not a mascot role as the person who "knows wellness."

- **P01–04 | Everyone has made plans.** Kreeb genuinely wants some quiet time. Zib wants access to a damper for ten minutes. Jax sees an approved chance to nap. Astra is trying to keep the schedule and the station functional. The system reads Jax's nap as exemplary participation.
- **P05–08 | A bad physical environment, not an abstract moral.** Noola's water rig is too warm; she says so. Kreeb initially hears rejection of something he was excited about. Mira can observe the environmental data without turning Noola into a case study. An exit is a choice, not a lesson everyone claps for.
- **P09–12 | The fastest fix took hours to authorize.** Zib and Jax clear the room and repair the damper. They are legitimately irritated, but their exchange can also include some unrelated point of rivalry or humor. Noola returns because the room now suits her, not because corporate approves her return.
- **P13–15 | The quiet room becomes personal.** Glorp handles the practical paperwork narrowly; Kreeb discovers that inviting people in may make his own quiet room less quiet. Noola admits a specific frustration to Kreeb and lets him admit his too, privately.
- **P16–19 | What happened versus what was recorded.** Mira and Pixa separate physical temperature traces from participation entries. Astra faces one inconvenient corporate interpretation. Kreeb's disappointment doesn't vanish because the form is corrected.
- **P20–22 | Nobody gets a certificate for friendship.** Jax is mildly proud of the relaxation certificate he earned by sleeping and then denies caring about it. Zib retrieves a room for its intended use. A new disclosure initiative arrives; the characters have already established that private speech to friends is more valuable than public performance.

**Dialogue danger:** Noola cannot be everybody's therapist and Kreeb cannot spend every appearance saying "participation." Give them personal reasons to be glad, upset, impatient and funny.

## Issue 07 — Bring Your Feelings to Work Day (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** Anonymous submissions provoke recognizably bad guesses about coworkers. One complaint identifies a real mechanical noise. Kreeb is tired of people assuming every invitation from him is pressure; Glorp is tired of being asked to state feelings on demand.

- **P01–04 | An anonymous box isn't anonymous to colleagues who know one another.** Coworkers recognize words, habits and supposed handwriting, often incorrectly. Jax is accused by association with any mechanical whine. Zib goes to check a genuine sound, not to investigate who has feelings.
- **P05–08 | Competing knowledge.** Mira tests the noise under the correct conditions while Jax helps find it. Glorp sees that a specific complaint can be useful without becoming a worker's personality record. Brick's noir speculation over an anonymous card should be entertainingly wrong at least once.
- **P09–12 | A friend's disclosure.** Noola invites Kreeb into the quiet room and tells *him*, not the institution, why constant comment on her water rig is exhausting. Kreeb admits his own frustrations; both can have a laugh. Repair evidence follows the machine, not a confession.
- **P13–16 | The central system wants categories.** Corporate is dissatisfied with low disclosure; Pixa and Mira recognize that the material observations don't support an emotional interpretation. The crew's ordinary work record is more informative than a box of private statements.
- **P17–20 | Preparing for a real visitor.** A competent liaison is coming. Astra wants to be prepared; Zib dislikes dressing up a working station as a presentation. The crew share genuine anecdotes about previous visitors—good ones and bad ones—without turning one person into the spokesman for a policy.
- **P21–22 | The station is a place, not a performance.** Jax checks the real route and Brick invents an absurd hypothesis about the observer; Pixa corrects him socially, not via a giant dashboard. A human visit creates a fresh kind of awkwardness.

**Dialogue danger:** No colleague must deliver "privacy is important" to the reader. Show the difference between what someone chooses to say to a friend and what the company wants in a file.

## Issue 08 — The Corporate Visit (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** A sensible Liaison comes to see a small physical material pilot and instead learns what it takes to make Stardust function. Astra wants to give the right impression; Jax is a better guide than he has any right to be.

- **P01–04 | A credible outsider.** Jax takes the Liaison through working areas, describing coworkers in ways that reveal his actual affection and long experience. Astra is proud and a little mortified. Mira wants to show a much longer sample presentation; the Liaison respects the limitations.
- **P05–08 | Where the real station lives.** Glorp shows retained records, Kreeb points out what isn't recorded, and the Liaison has actual questions. A manifold fault interrupts the tour: practical access and ordinary crew knowledge matter more than staging a perfect visit.
- **P09–12 | The crew act before explaining themselves.** Zib isolates and repairs; Jax finds a route, Mira makes one careful test, Pixa coordinates without assigning personalities. The Liaison watches people disagree efficiently and keep helping one another. Let human dialogue occur amid tasks.
- **P13–16 | A surprising professional respect.** The residue specimen is bounded, inconsistent and not an exportable recipe. The Liaison is more persuaded by repeatable operations than a polished claim. Mira feels heard, Astra surprised, and nobody needs to say "adaptive model" as a punchline.
- **P17–20 | A visit ends as a visit.** The Liaison offers a defensible recommendation; they exchange sincere, sometimes funny judgments about what visitors usually misunderstand. Jax sends the visitor out through the correct hatch with one unromantic reminder about the shoe-cleaning mat.
- **P21–22 | Central sees a different opportunity.** The site has done well, but remote reviewers add a measurable material target. Astra reacts as someone who thinks she just gained ground and discovers a new bargaining problem.

**Dialogue danger:** Don't make the Liaison a teacher explaining the show's correct thesis. Let them be a professional with their own sense of humor who sees something the cast has learned to take for granted.

## Issue 09 — Output Target (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** Astra makes a risky decision for a plausible reason: she wants the overdue parts, reliable funding and fewer last-minute compromises for people she cares about. She temporarily distorts legitimate work around a number; the cost arrives as a real ship at Dock Two.

- **P01–04 | A target wrapped around a need.** Astra initially resists the benchmark, then sees its connection to expedited supply. Zib and Mira don't simply represent ethics: they have their own work and deadlines. The argument should be specific enough that a reader could believe both sides.
- **P05–08 | The first accommodation looks harmless.** One maintenance check is moved earlier and completed. Jax notices the calendar changed but doesn't yet object. Mira refuses to claim a tiny sample supports a production forecast. Astra sees numerical success and thinks perhaps this is workable.
- **P09–11 | The second favor costs someone else.** Zib's original schedule collides with an arriving part and Jax's cargo handoff. Noola can object because a colleague made promises that affect her day, not because she is a spokesperson for personal boundaries.
- **P12–16 | The part of the station that can't wait.** Early service blocks cargo access; a real ship arrives. Zib, Jax and Astra move through the practical repair without abandoning safety. Humor comes from the cargo pilot's entirely reasonable impatience, Zib's restrained annoyance and Jax's habit of saying exactly the wrong *true* thing.
- **P17–19 | The number rewards the wrong thing.** Pixa shows that the target still looks good despite the delay, but the human embarrassment is the scene: Astra knows she caused it, Jax knows she heard his warning, Zib won't let anyone pretend the schedule was somebody else's idea.
- **P20–22 | An honest, unpopular record.** Astra takes responsibility without staging a public apology ceremony. The report describes actual work and the missed benchmark; the institutional question remains open. The cost continues into the finale.

**Dialogue danger:** A character saying "metrics can distort priorities" does not create drama. A crew member missing an actual delivery while arguing with someone they respect does.

## Issue 10 — The Station Stays Open (22 pages)

**Story we watch:** When offered false choices for the station's future, the crew build a truthful fourth option. The final emotional payoff is that these people have become more interesting to one another, not that they now share an approved management philosophy.

- **P01–04 | False choices and old work.** Central's options arrive. Zib brings a physical need, Mira brings bounded evidence, and Astra refuses to describe colleagues as a material-extraction machine. The humor of drafting together emerges from things these people remember saying during earlier issues.
- **P05–08 | Things they can prove.** Actual repair logs, prior unexpected failures and the cargo incident matter. Jax can be honest about his own decisions without proving a character arc. Mira remains proud of the discovery and can tolerate its limitations.
- **P09–11 | Glorp and Kreeb write together, differently.** Both remember whose objects, spaces and invitations have been misunderstood. They know their way around one another's habits now. The proposed model separates actual service from incidental material observations; their argument over wording is sharp because the relationship is lived-in, not ideological.
- **P12–15 | One genuine outsider and a real budget.** The Liaison raises hard questions rather than delivering approval on cue. Astra explains the practical reasons for the fourth choice. The station may remain in trouble; the crew's sincerity doesn't guarantee a good decision.
- **P16–19 | While awaiting a verdict, work continues.** People go back to actual jobs and ordinary conversations. Someone complains about the vending machine, someone's evening plans fall through, and a tool is found where it wasn't supposed to be. This continuation shows the station's worth more convincingly than a pledge.
- **P20–21 | A conditional, believable win.** Approval enables real procurement and service continuity, not sudden riches. Everyone reacts idiosyncratically—relief, suspicion, pride, jokes about a part that's finally coming. No unanimous speech.
- **P22 | Back where people live.** A casual invitation and the old card game recur; Glorp attends this time, Pixa comes as company, Astra isn't forced to become a best friend, and Kreeb's secretly labeled chair still exists. A mundane shared-space argument provides the laugh. The workplace never stops being a workplace.

**Dialogue danger:** The corporate decision is plot closure. **The last laugh belongs to the coworkers**, and it should depend on a character fact or relationship the reader discovered by spending time with them.
