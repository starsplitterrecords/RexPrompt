# Stardust Station — Dialogue Development, Issues 05–07
**Working character-first scenes | Unreleased dialogue exploration | 2026-10-10**

Source premises remain: Resource Efficiency Review, Wellness Week, Bring Your Feelings to Work Day. The scenes put specific friendships, private plans and contradictory desires underneath corporate interruptions. These are **dialogue explorations**, not approved page lettering or instructions to change finished art.

## Issue 05 — Resource Efficiency Review

### Scene A: The job Jax actually wants
*Admin hub, midday. A limited physical-sample collection pilot is being set up while ordinary work continues. Jax arrives carrying an awkwardly shaped container. Astra needs someone to maintain the collection route.*

**ASTRA:** Set that down by the wall, please.

**JAX:** Which wall? We've become a four-wall organization since this morning.

**PIXA:** The suggested site is near the central junction.

**JAX:** The central junction is where everyone leaves spare ladders.

**ASTRA:** And you know where the ladders are because—

**JAX:** Because I'm the one who has to move them. This station has some very inventive people who still believe a ladder disappears when you turn your back.

**PIXA:** It's the shortest collection route.

**JAX:** From the loading bay, yes. From the actual places you're collecting from, no. You've walked us through the tool shed to get to the kitchen.

**PIXA:** I thought the tool shed might be useful.

**JAX:** It is. For tools.

*Astra opens the pilot assignment list.*

**ASTRA:** We need one person to own the route.

**JAX:** I'll do it.

*Both Astra and Pixa look up.*

**ASTRA:** Really?

**JAX:** Don't make that face. You asked.

**ASTRA:** I'm not making a face.

**JAX:** That's the same face you made when I told you I could cook.

**ASTRA:** You told me you could *warm* a sandwich.

**JAX:** Which is how you know I've done it successfully.

**PIXA:** Why would you prefer custody routing over the other pilot roles?

**JAX:** Because nobody gets to tell me the fastest way through the station unless they've carried a bin up that stairwell.

**ASTRA:** So it's territorial.

**JAX:** It's an actual job. There's a place to start and a place to finish, and nobody has to rate how I felt carrying it.

**PIXA:** I can remove the staff-condition prompts.

**JAX:** Excellent. Also, you'll need to move the second container. It's blocking Noola's access rig.

**PIXA:** I didn't realize.

**JAX:** That's what happens when the route lives in a picture.

*Pixa takes the container and begins moving it. Jax joins instead of leaving the lifting to Pixa.*

**ASTRA:** You could have volunteered for a route assignment months ago.

**JAX:** Months ago you didn't ask me to do it in front of an audience.

*Astra looks around. Pixa, already occupied moving the container, hasn't been watching.*

### Scene B: An invitation mistaken for a task
*End of shift. Mira enters the operations hub with a container of leftovers from Kreeb's gathering. Pixa is checking the end of the pilot's first-day figures. Kreeb stops to pick up a cup.*

**MIRA:** Is that my container?

**PIXA:** This one contains residue samples.

**MIRA:** No, the one behind it.

**PIXA:** It contains pastry.

**MIRA:** Yes. It's mine.

**PIXA:** I separated it from the sample storage as soon as I noticed.

**MIRA:** Good. Jax ate the label off the lid last time.

**KREEB:** It was icing. He didn't eat the label.

**MIRA:** He ate *around* the label. Somehow more irritating.

*Pixa gives Mira the container.*

**PIXA:** Are you preparing another gathering?

**MIRA:** I was thinking about it. Kreeb's place gets so crowded you have to stand in a different room to change your mind.

**KREEB:** You can have it at mine. I've moved the little table.

**MIRA:** Where?

**KREEB:** Near the window.

**MIRA:** Where Glorp likes to sit?

**KREEB:** Oh, that's going to be a conversation.

**PIXA:** Would a seating plan be helpful?

**MIRA:** Not unless you want a civil war.

**PIXA:** I meant for myself. I don't need a seat, but I'd like to come.

*For a beat, neither Mira nor Kreeb realizes Pixa means the invitation.*

**KREEB:** Of course you can.

**MIRA:** I thought you knew that.

**PIXA:** You generally ask me when you need the schedule.

**MIRA:** Right. Well, this time you can come to eat nothing and tell us our game rules are bad.

**PIXA:** I have several observations ready.

**KREEB:** Please save them for after dessert.

**MIRA:** That's what Glorp says about dessert.

*They leave together. Pixa switches off the work tablet before following.*

## Issue 06 — Wellness Week

### Scene A: A perfectly good quiet room
*The official wellness session occupies the multipurpose room, delaying a small damper repair. Noola's water-dome rig makes the resulting temperature particularly uncomfortable. Kreeb is sincere about the program's one useful feature: a room where nobody is required to perform for anyone else.*

**KREEB:** I checked the quiet room. Nobody is using it.

**NOOLA:** That's why I like it.

**KREEB:** I thought you might.

**NOOLA:** You're giving me a tour of an empty room.

**KREEB:** I wanted you to know it was there.

**NOOLA:** I've worked here longer than that table.

**KREEB:** They repurposed it last month. It used to be storage.

**NOOLA:** I've also worked here longer than storage.

*Zib passes with a tool case, still waiting for access to the multipurpose damper.*

**ZIB:** Don't get too comfortable. I'm supposed to fix the vent in the room they're using for deep breathing.

**NOOLA:** My suit temperature is climbing every time they use the exercise lighting.

**KREEB:** You could skip the session.

**NOOLA:** I was going to skip it. Then someone told me I couldn't, which made attending briefly attractive.

**ZIB:** Why?

**NOOLA:** Spite is excellent motivation. Until you have to sit through the session.

**KREEB:** I actually wanted to try it.

*Noola turns from the empty room.*

**NOOLA:** Why?

**KREEB:** Because I never stop. If I'm not looking after something, I find something that needs looking after.

**NOOLA:** There are many people here who would confirm that.

**KREEB:** I know. I was hoping an hour of sitting quietly might help.

**NOOLA:** It would probably help *us*.

**KREEB:** I was expecting you to say that.

*They sit. No facilitator, no data, no quiet-room lesson.*

**KREEB:** You think it's silly?

**NOOLA:** I think getting an hour alone is wonderful. I think making everybody attend a group event to earn it is hilarious.

**KREEB:** I didn't ask you to be here.

**NOOLA:** No. You just told me the room existed.

**KREEB:** And now I'm in it.

**NOOLA:** You'll get the hang of it.

*Pause.*

**KREEB:** I am already thinking of six people who should come here.

**NOOLA:** Then you're not getting the hang of it.

### Scene B: The nap that became a session
*Elsewhere the same day, the official tracker has flagged Jax as an enthusiastic participant because he took a break in an approved chair. Astra and Pixa are near the crowded schedule screen.*

**ASTRA:** According to this, Jax completed the relaxation block.

**PIXA:** Twenty-two uninterrupted minutes.

**ASTRA:** Jax? Uninterrupted?

**JAX:** I was having a nap.

**PIXA:** Your posture and heart rate were compatible with the relaxation exercise.

**JAX:** My entire life has been compatible with sitting down.

**ASTRA:** You know the exercise was supposed to be voluntary.

**JAX:** I volunteered for the chair. Nobody asked me for the seminar.

**PIXA:** The system has issued a certificate.

**JAX:** Can I use it to get tomorrow afternoon off?

**ASTRA:** No.

**JAX:** Then it has no commercial value.

*Zib walks in, slightly sweaty, carrying a replacement damper component.*

**ZIB:** Can I go into the multipurpose room now? I have a part in my hand and a ten-minute job I could've done three hours ago.

**ASTRA:** There's one group left.

**ZIB:** Are they still breathing?

**PIXA:** Yes.

**ZIB:** Good. They won't need me to demonstrate it.

**JAX:** I can help carry that if we're done with my certificate.

**ASTRA:** You have a certificate?

**JAX:** Apparently I'm very well.

*Pixa holds out the certificate without pride or sarcasm; Pixa is genuinely pleased.*

**PIXA:** It's your first.

**JAX:** Please don't say that as though we're collecting them.

**ZIB:** I got a warning for standing near the sign.

**JAX:** That's because you looked stressed.

**ZIB:** I was looking at my job, through a locked door.

*The human exchange continues; nobody explains "participation data" to the reader.*

## Issue 07 — Bring Your Feelings to Work Day

### Scene A: Everybody thinks they recognize the handwriting
*A box invites anonymous feelings. One card describes a recurring mechanical noise in a service corridor. Another says only: "I am tired of being asked if I'm all right." Glorp, Kreeb, Noola and Brick are near the submissions.*

**KREEB:** I think that's a fair complaint.

**GLORP:** Which one?

**KREEB:** The one about being asked how someone feels.

**GLORP:** Of course you would.

**KREEB:** What's that supposed to mean?

**GLORP:** You ask me how I am before I've had coffee. Sometimes while I'm getting the coffee.

**KREEB:** I say good morning.

**GLORP:** And then you wait.

**KREEB:** That's how conversations work.

**GLORP:** It feels like a follow-up question.

**BRICK:** It's not your handwriting.

**GLORP:** I didn't say it was.

**BRICK:** You reached for your pen when you read it.

**GLORP:** I'm allowed to have a pen near paper.

**KREEB:** Maybe Noola wrote it.

**NOOLA:** I can hear you.

**KREEB:** Then you're probably not the author.

**NOOLA:** That's an astonishing conclusion.

*Brick lifts the second card, the one about the noise.*

**BRICK:** I know who wrote this.

**GLORP:** Why?

**BRICK:** The use of the word "whine."

**KREEB:** A lot of us say whine.

**BRICK:** Not about the west corridor. Usually about Jax.

*Jax enters at exactly the wrong moment.*

**JAX:** Why is everybody looking at me?

**NOOLA:** We're discussing a noise.

**JAX:** That narrows it down to nearly everyone.

**BRICK:** I was careful not to accuse you.

**JAX:** Very kind of you. I don't know how I'll recover from the restraint.

*The reader knows these colleagues well enough to enjoy the wrong guesses without learning the anonymous authors.*

### Scene B: A feeling told to a friend, not an employer
*After the noisy damper is found, Noola has withdrawn to the plain quiet room. Kreeb finds her there, alone; he doesn't bring a clipboard.*

**KREEB:** Do you want company?

**NOOLA:** For a minute.

*Kreeb sits. He doesn't ask how she is.*

**NOOLA:** That was considerate.

**KREEB:** What was?

**NOOLA:** You didn't start by asking for a feeling.

**KREEB:** I've learned something this week.

**NOOLA:** Don't make it a landmark.

*Quiet.*

**NOOLA:** I'm tired of people explaining what my rig must feel like.

**KREEB:** Have I done that?

**NOOLA:** Quite often.

**KREEB:** I'm sorry.

**NOOLA:** You don't have to apologize every time. Sometimes I just want you to agree that the room is too warm.

**KREEB:** It was too warm.

**NOOLA:** Thank you.

**KREEB:** I was hot too, actually.

**NOOLA:** Nobody ever asks about your temperature.

**KREEB:** No. I could put it on the card.

**NOOLA:** You could put it on a postcard and send it to yourself.

*Kreeb laughs, then ventures something more personal.*

**KREEB:** The thing I was upset about wasn't the program. It was Glorp assuming I'd make people come.

**NOOLA:** Would you?

**KREEB:** I don't know. I want people to enjoy things with me. Sometimes I think that's the same as wanting people to enjoy things.

**NOOLA:** That's something you could tell Glorp.

**KREEB:** I was going to tell *you.*

**NOOLA:** Then I have heard it.

*Pixa appears in the doorway, careful not to enter without asking.*

**PIXA:** Astra needs the room in fifteen minutes. I told her to find another one.

**NOOLA:** See, Kreeb? That's a wellness program I can support.

### Reader-facing audit for 05–07
- What Jax chooses in Issue 05 has a material route consequence; the jokes reveal pride and reputation without writing a competence lesson.
- Pixa is invited as a person; the realization is funny because of how the others accidentally framed earlier invitations.
- Wellness is not presented as a simple malicious company plot: Kreeb actually wants respite and Noola actually wants autonomy.
- Anonymous disclosures produce funny **misidentifications** and a real equipment complaint. The private Noola/Kreeb conversation is not automatically public evidence.
- Comic beats need setup and observable reactions. Adapt to approved panel geography rather than adding unscripted screen copy or explanatory captions.
