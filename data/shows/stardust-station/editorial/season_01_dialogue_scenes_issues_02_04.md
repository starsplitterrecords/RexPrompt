# Stardust Station — Dialogue Development, Issues 02–04
**Working character-first scenes | Unreleased dialogue exploration | 2026-10-10**

These scenes are written for voice and relationships before balloon compression. They are **new proposed dialogue**, not released story facts. Approved artwork and final page framing remain unchanged. Scene labels point to appropriate story neighborhoods rather than authorizing new panels.

## Issue 02 — The Break Room Is Not a Department

### Scene A: The dinner that Glorp missed
*Break-room morning. Glorp has finished collecting yesterday's cleaning cloths. Kreeb is putting out chairs. Astra has brought a fresh waste bin. The repaired-microwave work is happening elsewhere in the room.*

**ASTRA:** I got you another bin.

**GLORP:** We have six.

**ASTRA:** Is that too many?

**GLORP:** It's seven now.

**ASTRA:** I thought there was a problem with the old one.

**GLORP:** There was a problem with what was in it. I believe you were here for that part.

**KREEB:** You were still here when I left?

**GLORP:** You watched me cleaning.

**KREEB:** I watched you start cleaning. There's a difference.

**GLORP:** Apparently there's an hour and a half of difference.

**KREEB:** You should've come by after.

**GLORP:** At what time?

**KREEB:** Whenever you finished.

**GLORP:** At eleven-thirty I thought you would be asleep.

**KREEB:** We were playing cards.

**GLORP:** Of course you were.

**KREEB:** Jax stayed until two. He helped me wash everything up.

**GLORP:** Jax washed your dishes?

**KREEB:** Yes.

**GLORP:** Our Jax?

**KREEB:** Do you know another?

**GLORP:** The same Jax who used a broken machine as an excuse not to rinse his lunch bowl last week?

**KREEB:** He was lovely. Did the dishes, wiped the table, put away the chairs. Wouldn't let me do a thing.

**GLORP:** I've seen that man step around a spilled drink for three days.

**ASTRA:** Perhaps he's more considerate socially than professionally.

**GLORP:** Is that an approved distinction?

**ASTRA:** I said perhaps.

**KREEB:** He brought dessert, too.

**GLORP:** Now I know you're making this up.

**KREEB:** He brought half a dessert.

**GLORP:** Ah.

**KREEB:** The other half had been eaten on the walk over.

**GLORP:** There he is.

*Jax comes in with his soup. He is surprised to find everybody looking at him.*

**JAX:** I don't like how that felt.

**KREEB:** I was telling them what a good guest you were.

**JAX:** Oh. Carry on.

**GLORP:** I understand you know how a dishcloth works.

**JAX:** I'm very versatile.

**GLORP:** Good. There's a bin by the door.

**JAX:** Oh, it was almost a compliment.

*Jax departs with his soup as Glorp lets the moment sit.*

**ASTRA:** You all had dinner last night?

**KREEB:** Yes.

**ASTRA:** That sounds nice.

**GLORP:** I wouldn't know.

**KREEB:** I invited you.

**GLORP:** I know you did. That's the worst part.

*Astra is still holding the seventh bin.*

### Scene B: The knock that never happened
*Later, near the clean counter, the workday is almost normal again. Kreeb sees Glorp returning the cracked mug to its original spot.*

**KREEB:** I saved you a plate.

**GLORP:** I heard.

**KREEB:** You did? From whom?

**GLORP:** You. Just now.

**KREEB:** Right. Well, I did.

**GLORP:** I actually went by your quarters.

*Kreeb turns fully toward them.*

**KREEB:** When?

**GLORP:** Around midnight. Your door was closed.

**KREEB:** Doors are often closed, Glorp.

**GLORP:** I didn't want to wake you.

**KREEB:** We were awake.

**GLORP:** I know that *now.*

**KREEB:** You could've knocked.

**GLORP:** I wasn't going to announce myself at midnight like a tax collector.

**KREEB:** You spent half your night at the door and walked away?

**GLORP:** It wasn't half the night. It was perhaps forty seconds.

**KREEB:** Forty seconds?

**GLORP:** I was deciding whether the lights were on.

**KREEB:** You can see our lights from the corridor?

**GLORP:** No, which is why it took so long.

*Kreeb almost smiles, then doesn't.*

**KREEB:** You could have sent a message.

**GLORP:** I did. I wrote one. I deleted it.

**KREEB:** Why?

**GLORP:** Because it said, "Are you still entertaining?" and at midnight that sounded as though I were reporting you.

**KREEB:** We *were* still entertaining.

**GLORP:** Yes, you've made that painfully clear.

*An uncomfortable but companionable pause.*

**KREEB:** I still have the food.

**GLORP:** What was it?

**KREEB:** Mira's cooking. Some kind of pastry.

**GLORP:** The purple one?

**KREEB:** No, that was dessert. This was dinner.

**GLORP:** I'm not eating either without speaking to her first.

**KREEB:** Come by tonight.

**GLORP:** If you're awake.

**KREEB:** I'll leave the door open.

**GLORP:** That's a terrible security practice.

**KREEB:** Glorp.

**GLORP:** I'll knock.

*That is as close as they come to settling anything.*

## Issue 03 — The Meeting That Wouldn't End

### Scene A: Ten minutes, with the door open
*Admin hub conference nook. Astra arranged a ten-minute follow-up. She wants to discuss the microwave, but she's overheard two coworkers laughing about an evening she wasn't present for.*

**ASTRA:** This is going to take ten minutes.

**ZIB:** Is that what the timer is for?

**ASTRA:** The timer is a reminder.

**ZIB:** Of what?

**ASTRA:** Of ten minutes.

**JAX:** It seems to have started before everybody sat down.

**ASTRA:** You were all here.

**PIXA:** I was not. I was holding the door.

**ASTRA:** That's being here.

**PIXA:** That's good to know.

*Kreeb tries to fit a chair between Glorp and the portable screen. Glorp refuses to move the binder.*

**ASTRA:** Before we start, I heard the little gathering went well.

**KREEB:** It was lovely.

**ASTRA:** I'm glad. It seems healthy that everyone is spending time together.

**JAX:** We mostly ate snacks and insulted a game.

**GLORP:** I wasn't there.

**JAX:** We've established that, Glorp.

**GLORP:** I'm merely specifying the limits of "everyone."

**ASTRA:** No, you're right. I didn't mean *everyone.* I meant—never mind. Does anybody still have a microwave problem?

**JAX:** Yes, but mine's with the soup container and that's private.

**ZIB:** Your container's fine. The hinge on the side rack is loose. I told you.

**JAX:** I'm choosing to preserve the mystery.

**ASTRA:** Can we assign the hinge? We're six minutes in.

**ZIB:** I have the replacement part in my pocket. I was going to do it while you were talking.

**ASTRA:** You can do it when we're done.

**ZIB:** I could have done it twice by then.

**ASTRA:** Sit down, Zib.

*Zib sits. Pixa closes the door. It clicks shut.*

**PIXA:** Everyone is present.

**JAX:** Oh, that's going to be a problem.

### Scene B: Advice from people who aren't attending
*The meeting has outlasted its ten-minute limit. The maintenance problem outside the door still exists. Zib wants to leave; Astra refuses to lose the thread.*

**ASTRA:** Jax, can you take ownership of the access label?

**JAX:** I can certainly advise whoever takes ownership.

**ZIB:** You'll have a great time advising yourself, then.

**JAX:** I've always respected my own judgment.

**ASTRA:** The reason I'm asking is that you actually know this equipment.

**JAX:** I do. We can end the meeting right there.

**ASTRA:** Not until we know who's doing it.

**ZIB:** He's doing it. He said the equipment was his.

**JAX:** I said I *know* the equipment. I also know a delightful cafe near Dock Two. That doesn't mean I'm opening a cafe.

**PIXA:** Is the cafe good?

**JAX:** Surprisingly. Ask Kreeb.

**KREEB:** The coffee's terrible.

**JAX:** The food is excellent.

**GLORP:** How can a place have terrible coffee and excellent food?

**KREEB:** You'd enjoy the food. They put labels on everything.

**GLORP:** That isn't funny. Where is it?

**ASTRA:** We're not reviewing restaurants.

**ZIB:** Why not? We're certainly not fixing the rack.

*Jax takes the tiny access label and stands.*

**JAX:** I'll go fix it, on one condition.

**ASTRA:** Name it.

**JAX:** When I get back, nobody is still talking about the label.

**ASTRA:** Agreed.

*Later, Jax returns holding the old label. Astra and the others are debating whether the meeting ended.*

**JAX:** You all look very proud of yourselves.

**ASTRA:** We did finish three items.

**JAX:** Which three?

*Silence. Zib's smile is unusually unhelpful.*

**PIXA:** We did establish where Glorp is going for lunch.

**GLORP:** And I'm going.

## Issue 04 — Temporary Material

### Scene A: A scientist has friends, too
*Maintenance bay and adjoining lab. The bracket's replacement is nineteen days away. Mira and Zib have completed several ordinary tests of a physical residue fragment. It's useful within a limited range and fails other tests.*

**ZIB:** We could use it to fill the gap. We are not using it to hold the machine together.

**MIRA:** I know. I was there for the test.

**ZIB:** You keep looking at it like it should get a medal.

**MIRA:** It survived a load I didn't expect.

**ZIB:** So did that washer. Nobody's asking for a portrait of the washer.

**MIRA:** I'm not asking for a portrait.

**ZIB:** You were taking pictures.

**MIRA:** For the records.

**ZIB:** Sixteen pictures.

**MIRA:** Thirteen. Three were blurry.

**ZIB:** That sounds like a portrait session.

*Jax brings the ordinary clamp assembly from the workbench.*

**JAX:** Do we need the special stuff or can I do this with things we understand?

**MIRA:** Both. The clamp does the work. The sample fills one gap we haven't been able to reach.

**JAX:** That's better. Yesterday you told me you were "watching a new world unfold."

**MIRA:** I said "a whole new line of work."

**JAX:** Those aren't the same thing?

**MIRA:** No. One means I get an assistant.

**ZIB:** Keep dreaming.

**JAX:** I've been told I'm very helpful at other people's houses.

*Mira glances toward Glorp, who is passing the doorway with a supply box.*

**MIRA:** Oh, I've heard about your legendary dishes.

**JAX:** I've never been appreciated more than during a crisis with plates.

*Zib slides the approved clamp into place.*

**ZIB:** Then hold this. You can be appreciated with your hands full.

*Jax does. Mira watches the readings change.*

**MIRA:** It works.

**ZIB:** It does one thing. For now.

**MIRA:** For now is enough to be excited about.

**ZIB:** I'm excited about the coolant staying where it's supposed to.

**JAX:** I'm excited about being able to put my arm down.

**MIRA:** Fine. Three different forms of excitement.

### Scene B: The person who sends the report
*Later. Astra and Mira are in the maintenance bay after the temporary repair has held within its test limits. The report is drafted but unsent. No one is shouting.*

**ASTRA:** This is good work.

**MIRA:** You sound unhappy about that.

**ASTRA:** I'd sound less unhappy if there weren't a field here asking for commercial implications.

**MIRA:** That's because somebody else will eventually read it.

**ASTRA:** Yes. That's exactly the concern.

**MIRA:** They should read it. We found something.

**ASTRA:** We found a gap filler that helps a particular repair, with safeguards.

**MIRA:** You know that isn't the part I'm excited about.

**ASTRA:** I do.

**MIRA:** Then stop introducing it as a gap filler.

**ASTRA:** That's what it did.

**MIRA:** It's the first thing it did.

*Astra looks up at her.*

**ASTRA:** You sound like I did when they offered me this station.

**MIRA:** What did you think it would be?

**ASTRA:** Bigger. Not physically. I thought there'd be people who understood what we were doing.

**MIRA:** There are.

**ASTRA:** I thought there'd be more of them.

**MIRA:** So now I have the same ridiculous idea and you're protecting me from it?

**ASTRA:** I'm protecting all of us from someone who reads "promising material" and decides we're a factory.

**MIRA:** I'm not asking you to write promising material. I'm asking you not to write "curious dust."

**ASTRA:** Did I write curious dust?

*Mira points at the draft. Astra reads her own wording.*

**ASTRA:** Oh, that's dreadful.

**MIRA:** I'd like a little scientific dignity. I already work next to a vending machine that dispenses soup in spheres.

**ASTRA:** They improved the packaging.

**MIRA:** I'm still not calling it soup.

*They return to the document. Astra crosses out the adjective.*

**ASTRA:** We can both be right. The test is real, and the limits are real.

**MIRA:** Fine. But you send it.

**ASTRA:** I always send it.

**MIRA:** I know. That's why you'll be the one they call.

*Astra's hand pauses over SEND. The audience now understands what each stands to lose without needing a speech about institutional incentives.*

### Reader-facing audit for 02–04
- New off-duty details (Kreeb's gathering, Jax's dishes, Mira's cooking) enter *through characters who have firsthand knowledge*, not narration.
- The dinner story creates a running comic contradiction and exposes Astra's position as supervisor.
- Issue 03's meeting is funny because it traps people whose real jobs, outside errands, and friendships continue.
- Issue 04 allows scientific discovery to delight Mira **as a person**, while Zib and Astra have legitimate practical counterpoints.
- A final panel must show the action being discussed; speech balloons referring to invisible reactions require a production flag rather than a silent art rewrite.
