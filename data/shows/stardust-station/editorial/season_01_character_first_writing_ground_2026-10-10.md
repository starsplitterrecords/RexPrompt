# Stardust Station — Season One Character-First Writing Ground
**Working dialogue and story redevelopment | 2026-10-10 | not approved/released canon**

## Authority and scope
Released Issue 01, *Mandatory Proximity*, remains the source of story and visual canon. Keep approved character designs, established station geometry, cast identity and already-approved artwork. Issues 02–10 retain their durable premises and broad material/institutional escalation, but dialogue and scene transactions are editable. **This document proposes new off-duty social history, not retroactive established canon.** Do not silently put these proposed facts into Issue 01. Do not alter finished images as a shortcut to improving words. Where a comic panel cannot carry the new conversation, flag the line for a later production choice.

## The show
*The Office if it were Futurama.* These are coworkers with a life outside any given incident. The station is competent and appealing; it is not an endless corporate training simulator. A scene starts because someone is trying to do or enjoy something, not because an issue needs its theme explained.

**Writing test:** Remove the procedural and sci-fi vocabulary. If the dialogue has no relationship, history, opinion, personal surprise or comic choice left, the scene has failed. Remove the punchlines. If nobody still has an intelligible reason to speak, the scene has failed again.

## What makes the dialogue specific
- **Conversations have subjects.** A dinner missed, someone bringing an awful gift, whose shift was traded, a game nobody admits to liking, whether an invitation was genuine, a visit that somebody has been dreading. The official problem can interrupt the conversation without becoming the conversation.
- **People disagree about facts as well as feelings.** They misremember, omit embarrassing details, defend absent friends, exaggerate, change subjects, and know which detail will bother a particular colleague. They do not always diagnose their own emotions on cue.
- **One joke can take a while.** Establish a believable claim; complicate it with specific new information; let someone change the meaning accidentally; land the payoff later. Not every line is a reversal or an objection.
- **Give coworkers mixed loyalties.** Jax can be generous at Kreeb's apartment but evasive on station. Kreeb may be generous with invitations and territorial about a chair. Glorp can be fussy and sentimental. Astra can be an excellent manager and be embarrassed that people are happier when she's gone. Mira can be rigorous at work and scatterbrained at dinner.
- **Relationships are different in different rooms.** Kreeb and Glorp may fight over a table in Issue 02, enjoy a shared joke in Issue 04, say something cruel in Issue 07, and collaborate without speaking in Issue 10. No fixed "Kreeb helps, Glorp obstructs" transaction.
- **Other characters exist even when absent.** Talking about an absent coworker can be funnier than making the coworker perform a routine bit.
- **Don't make every problem about privacy or management.** Sometimes the stakes are pride, embarrassment, curiosity, envy, appetite, not wanting to look foolish, or being annoyed that somebody else's terrible restaurant recommendation was right.
- **Narrator not required.** The artwork, timing, and natural speech must let a first-time reader understand who wants what and why a reaction matters. Script notes are not visible story information. No emotional subtitles or lectures about themes.
- **Let the station intrude.** A delayed part, wrong door, working microwave, visitor, shared tool, or residue observation can interrupt an actual conversation. Physical reality stays specific and functional.
- **Reader laughter before moral resolution.** Give small personal revelations and funny changes in status; avoid a neat final lesson in better procedures.

## Proposed recurring social history (first introduced in unreleased Issue 02)
1. **Kreeb's evening gathering.** Kreeb invited Glorp; Glorp stayed late cleaning the break room and missed it. Jax stayed to help Kreeb clean up, despite dodging cleanup requests at work. Mira brought unexpectedly excellent food. Kreeb did save Glorp a portion. Glorp went to Kreeb's door late, but did not knock. Nobody knows this entire story until the pieces emerge through conversation. Do not reduce it to a single confession.
2. **The card game.** The group sometimes plays a game Glorp claims to dislike because of the scoring. This isn't a hardcoded running gag; it gives the cast a concrete history to recall, argue about, and gradually change.
3. **Astra's uncertain social place.** Astra is respected and sometimes genuinely liked, but the crew behaves differently when management is in the room. Astra notices this. Do not solve it by having the crew declare they love her or by having Astra intrude on their private events.
4. **Jax's contradiction.** He is capable of being an exceptionally thoughtful friend and an aggravating colleague on the same day. When someone praises him, somebody else can possess genuine counterevidence. Preserve his exact established augmented forearm visually; never turn it into an easy disability joke.
5. **Noola's elective solitude.** Her quiet preferences are choices, not symptoms to be treated by communal coworkers. She can also be one of the funniest people at an evening gathering by choosing when to join.
6. **Pixa as a friend and colleague.** Pixa may help plan an actual gathering, have preferences, and recognize the difference between being asked for company and being asked to count participants. No obligatory stats line.
7. **Mira's actual private interests.** She cooks, plays games, has enough competitiveness to be insufferable about some things and enough curiosity to lose track of time. These are proposed characterization opportunities, not a compulsory hobby list.
8. **Zib outside repair.** Zib has people to see, appointments to keep, opinions about food and coworkers and social debts. Zib's competence isn't the sum of Zib's personality.
9. **Brick off duty.** The detective performance is sometimes a joke other characters quietly enable. Brick can be generous, lonely, intensely observant, or unexpectedly practical without revealing a secret tragic backstory.

## Ten-issue shape — grounded premises, new character-first emphasis

### 01 — Mandatory Proximity [RELEASED CANON; NO REWRITE]
A morale-and-safety inspection establishes the ensemble and physical residue as backdrop, not a speaking mystery. Use visual/personality authority as released. Never retcon page events to manufacture new relationships.

### 02 — The Break Room Is Not a Department [22 pages; dialogue redevelopment]
Keep the microwave, signs, chair semicircle, maintenance fix and obsolete Form 8C printer. Real story: Glorp missed Kreeb's gathering and discovers that Jax was unexpectedly generous there. Kreeb thought they had been kind; Glorp feels both excluded and irritated at caring. Astra is embarrassed to discover the private life she didn't know the station had. Make room for Noola's solitary seat, Zib's repair, Mira's curiosity, Pixa and Brick's social observations. End on Kreeb discreetly claiming his preferred chair, Glorp catching him and offering the laminator. **Do not make the resolution a three-rule management victory.**

### 03 — The Meeting That Wouldn't End [22 pages]
Keep the absurd ten-minute follow-up and its unending owner assignments. Social spine: Astra wants to be included in the informal conversation and accidentally traps everyone in the official one. Jax and Zib are maneuvering around an outside-of-work obligation; Glorp and Kreeb continue to disagree about what happened at dinner. The funniest realization is that the work gets done by people who stop attending the meeting. Astra shouldn't announce this as a productivity principle: somebody should return mid-discussion with the actual repaired object. Final social beat: Astra gets an invitation she realizes isn't meant as a management directive and has to decide whether to accept.

### 04 — Temporary Material [22 pages]
Keep accidental, limited usefulness of residue in an ordinary temporary repair and the report that attracts institutional attention. Personal spine: Mira, usually the expert, is tired of scientific work being treated like a cute aside; Astra knows that attention changes budgets *and* lives. Zib distrusts an unproven material but trusts Mira enough to test it properly. Jax can be dependable in the repair while letting coworkers argue about whether he is dependable at dinner. Their personal history makes the question of who gets credit awkward and funny. Do not make Mira an exposition terminal or the material an answer to human feelings.

### 05 — Resource Efficiency Review [21 pages in current working script]
Keep the procurement incentive, limited collection pilot and its creeping demands. Personal spine: Astra chooses a compromise because Zib genuinely needs a part; Jax finds himself trusted with a task he cannot charm his way out of, and is secretly pleased. Glorp is less worried about forms than about a company rewriting everybody's memory of who did the work. Mira worries about scientific credit, not just abstract ethics. Pixa wants to attend a social invitation but is initially approached as if a spreadsheet were a person. End with corporate Wellness Week intruding into ordinary plans.

### 06 — Wellness Week [22 pages]
Keep the multipurpose-room damper/temperature fault, Noola's physical limits and the nominally voluntary session. Do not turn the whole issue into a privacy position paper. Kreeb actually hopes the week will help a person he cares about; Noola wants one useful quiet room but refuses to be anybody's wellness success story. Jax tries to get an uninterrupted break in an absurd program. Zib's real ten-minute repair keeps being deferred. Humor is the mismatch between how each person intends to use the day and how the schedule uses them. Keep one meaningful disagreement that isn't settled by reporting language.

### 07 — Bring Your Feelings to Work Day [22 pages]
Keep the complaint that points to an actual service noise, the anonymous-disclosure trap and the coming outside observer. Personal spine: colleagues know each other's voices well enough to guess authors of unsigned cards, and are wrong in ways that expose their prejudices. Glorp remembers how their personal feelings were mishandled at lunch; Kreeb feels the sting of being treated as if every invitation is pressure. Noola may choose to tell a friend something private but not to tell a central database. Brick's anonymous case note has a mundane explanation. End with the arrival of a visitor who has no idea what social situation they are walking into.

### 08 — The Corporate Visit [22 pages]
Keep the credible StarTrust Liaison, actual working tour, galley-manifold repair, narrow residue observation and operations-focused recommendation. Social spine: Astra wants the visitor to see the station at its best and worries the crew will treat the visit like the group dinner she wasn't invited to. Jax gives the truest tour because he actually knows how colleagues work together, including how they irritate one another. The Liaison has opinions, patience and a sense of humor, not villainous bureaucratic menace. Let the unexpected breakdown interrupt a socially awkward but believable visit, not a lecture about adaptive operations. Central review still imposes an output target.

### 09 — Output Target [22 pages]
Keep benchmark/procurement temptation, early maintenance, the blocked cargo lane and Astra's consequential reversal. Character spine: Astra makes a bad call because she wants everyone to keep their jobs and part supply, not because she forgot management principles. Zib is irritated that the promise of real parts made them rearrange real work. Jax remembers being treated as the cheerful unaccountable guy and has specific proof of what Astra's decision cost the shift. Mira is excited by data but refuses fake certainty. Noola is allowed to be angry for a personal reason unrelated to representing privacy. Humor continues through what people say while trying to get an actual shipment through Dock 2.

### 10 — The Station Stays Open [22 pages]
Keep the attempted fourth institutional option, truthful distinction between operational service and incidental material, unresolved central review and eventual approval. The climax is *not* the crew giving a presentation on good management. It is nine people who have spent a season knowing one another better, disagreeing, and occasionally showing up. Some choose Astra's recommendation because it keeps doing their jobs possible, not because they have become a corporate family. The Liaison advocates as a credible professional. End with the station open, the coworkers very much themselves, and a trivial shared-space argument with a history behind it.

## Continuity and production protections
- Keep released Issue 01 and fixed finished art. The working scripts are **not** approved canon.
- Issue 02 dinner details are **new proposed story**, whose first on-page disclosure must be readable by a new reader. Do not depend on invisible backstory.
- The material stays physical, nonsemantic, inconsistent and only accidentally useful from Issue 04. Do not explain jokes by claiming the residue "knows" emotions or words.
- Corporate interest grows for grounded value and institutional incentives; representatives remain credible human professionals.
- Scene imagery doesn't have to change to produce deeper conversation, but a speech balloon cannot refer to an action not visible or established. Flag any such mismatch.
- Preserve exact cast/character identities, Jax's established prosthetic, Noola's water rig, Glorp/Kreeb/Pixa anatomy, and geographic continuity of all recurring rooms.
- Caption/narrator is not an exposition substitute. The art and character speech deliver causal comprehension.
- Comedy audit: **Why are they talking now? What private or shared history intrudes? What new fact, contradiction, or choice changes the interaction? What is funny as a *scene* rather than a sequence of zingers?**
- Future revision priority: replace dialogue page by page only after its scene's personal subject, structural action, and comic escalation are solved. Do not convert the writing ground into automatic boilerplate for every issue.
