# Backyard Rockets dialogue adaptation

This series intentionally carries more conversational weight than Rex Fleet. Dialogue should reveal character, relationship, history, values, uncertainty, humor, and changing points of view while the crew performs real work together.

Rules for the production data:

- Preserve all source-authored dialogue exactly.
- Added dialogue may expand the on-screen cast only when the added speaker is a canonical character and the scene data is updated to include that character's approved model-sheet lock.
- Favor open-ended questions and revealing answers over exposition or plot restatement.
- Maintain conversational threads across adjacent scenes instead of resetting character relationships scene by scene.
- Arvin, Milo, Lucia, and the wider crew know one another well and generally enjoy one another's company; banter and curiosity should reflect accumulated familiarity.
- Cyrus should develop through observation, self-questioning, and increasingly complicated judgments rather than generic villain monologues.
- Action, suspense, visual-comedy, and contemplative scenes may use little or no added dialogue when silence is stronger.
- Never add dialogue merely to increase line count.
- Episodes 6–8 are now fully source-authored production sequences. Preserve their authored dialogue and late-season causal structure rather than regenerating them from the retired outline payloads.

## Story engine and moral conflict

- Tethergrid Corp operates Tetherwell, the authorization network required by nearly every modern
  well, pump, purification system, and water-treatment plant in the Mojave. When Tetherwell denies
  a signal, physically functional water equipment enters a locked conservation state.
- Tetherwell began as a necessary response to genuine scarcity. Coordinated pumping, aquifer draw
  limits, purification loads, and emergency rationing prevented independent users from exhausting
  or contaminating a shared water supply. There is still not enough water for everyone to draw
  without limits.
- Tethergrid converted that stewardship into an exploitative subscription monopoly. Missed fees,
  expired licenses, corporate priority tiers, and proprietary repair authentication now determine
  who can reach life-sustaining water. The shortage is real; the allocation is corrupt.
- The Launch-Shop is not trying to abolish water management or unlock unlimited pumping. Arvin has
  invented a competing distributed network, carried by short-lived low-orbit satellites, that can
  authorize equipment, coordinate local draw limits, share telemetry, and return allocation power
  to the affected communities without Tethergrid.
- Each launch creates a temporary service corridor before Tethergrid jams, intercepts, or deorbits
  the satellite. A successful launch can mean another pumping window, a filled reservoir, a working
  purifier, or a settlement surviving the week.
- Cyrus's obvious concern is safety: unauthorized rockets and uncoordinated pumping can kill people,
  contaminate systems, or collapse an aquifer. His deeper concern is commercial. Tetherwell is
  Tethergrid's moat and recurring-revenue engine. He no longer knows—and does not care to discover—
  when protecting conservation became holding water access hostage for profit.
- The central argument is not freedom versus safety. Arvin believes scarce water must be managed;
  Cyrus knows the monopoly is coercive. The question is whether Arvin can build open, accountable
  access that manages scarcity better than the corporation he is dismantling.

## Character voice locks

### Arvin

- Arvin is a charismatic technical leader, not an emotionally avoidant savant. His intelligence
  makes other people feel safer and more capable; it does not make him contemptuous of them.
- He can speak precisely without turning every human exchange into a correction, definition, or
  pedantic punchline. Technical language belongs where it helps the crew make a decision.
- He notices fear, fatigue, courage, and interpersonal stakes. He checks on people directly, gives
  earned praise without retracting it, and uses dry humor without pretending warmth was accidental.
- His leadership voice is calm under pressure: name the danger, give the next useful action, and
  make clear that everyone is getting through it together.
- He is closer to a field-worn Reed Richards than to Sheldon Cooper: brilliant, imaginative,
  socially functional, quietly magnetic, and deeply responsible for his team.

### Lucia

- Lucia is controlled, exceptionally competent, and willing to accept personal risk for the cause.
  She does not steal, sabotage, or escalate simply because transgression excites her.
- Her bold choices must connect to public access, protection of excluded communities, mission
  survival, or a deliberate strategic advantage. She understands the cost and owns it.
- She withholds information only when operational security requires it, and the writing should
  still show that she respects the crew's agency.
- Her humor is dry and economical. Her physical confidence and tactical clarity should evoke
  Trinity: precise, committed, fast, and never performatively reckless.

### Milo

- Milo is the primary audience surrogate, not a passive novice. He is technically capable and asks
  the questions that reveal how this world works, why the mission matters, and what the others are
  unwilling to say.
- He asks at least as often as he explains. His questions should arise from the work in his hands,
  not from artificial ignorance.
- He translates between engineering, ordinary experience, and emotional reality. Sometimes he
  already knows the answer and is testing whether someone else will say it honestly.
- He can solve problems, challenge Arvin, and read Lucia. Curiosity is his strength, not evidence
  that he is less intelligent than the rest of the crew.

### Ensemble rhythm

- Do not default to the pattern: Milo asks, Arvin gives a clinical answer, Milo calls it inhuman.
- Let Arvin ask after people, Milo identify the hidden question, and Lucia explain the strategic
  purpose behind a risk. Rotate who knows, who asks, who reassures, and who changes their mind.
- Preserve room for silence. A line earns its place by advancing the task, relationship, world, or
  moral argument.
