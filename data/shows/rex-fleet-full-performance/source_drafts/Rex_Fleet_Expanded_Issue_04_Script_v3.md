### SCENE 1 — THE KETTLE (P01–P03)

*TAL CORVUS — WARRENS — BEFORE DAWN. A queue bends around working pipes, sleeping families, portable heaters, and skiffs being checked for the third evacuation window. A child stands on a crate to draw the ships she expects to see. Two neighbors are arguing over a dented kettle.*

**MECHANIC:** You've had that since the water rationing. I want it back.

**NEIGHBOR:** I borrowed it. I didn't steal it.

**MECHANIC:** I said you could have it for a week.

**NEIGHBOR:** It was a particularly long week.

**MOTHER:** They've been doing this since before my daughter was born.

**CHILD:** Then why doesn't he take it?

**MOTHER:** Because he's very fond of complaining about it. He'd miss it terribly.

*The mechanic makes a wounded gesture. The mother tightens a heater strap and immediately has to undo it when her daughter complains it pinches.*

**CHILD:** Which one's Gravefire?

**MOTHER:** The one at the civilian berth. That captain's been arguing with the departure office since midnight.

**CHILD:** Is she losing?

**MOTHER:** The departure office is losing.

*The child adds a small ship to her drawing. On the far side of the station, Thunderbreak and its Fleet screen come into view. The queue quiets. Some people move toward the viewing rail. Others move away.*

**MECHANIC:** Didn't we ask them to come?

**NEIGHBOR:** We asked someone to protect us. They're not always the same thing.

*The mother folds the drawing and gives it back to her daughter. The child draws Fleet's cruiser as large as the paper will allow.*

### SCENE 2 — THE SHIP IS YOURS (P04–P05)

*THUNDERBREAK COMMAND. Lieutenant Erin Deka surveys the third-window approach. The patched cruiser still carries the scars of earlier rescues. Its weapons are not released for offensive fire.*

**TACTICAL:** Third-window orders received. Offensive batteries remain under Admiralty release.

**DEKA:** And the Commodore?

**TACTICAL:** Her tribunal finding stands. Thunderbreak remains under your command.

**DEKA:** I was beginning to worry they'd take my favorite chair away.

*Deka marks the distant vessels: Gravefire at the departure mouth, Sunlash at the far exit, Fleet in a narrow outer screen.*

**DEKA:** Shift our shields toward the civilian route. Guns stay dark unless we receive release. Keep a reserve where it can actually turn.

*Dominion appears on a secured command link.*

**DOMINION:** Lieutenant, I expect you understand the distinction between guarding a corridor and surrendering it to the first captain who shouts for help.

**DEKA:** Yes, Admiral. Unfortunately, disabled ships are poor listeners. They drift through the lines you've drawn.

**DOMINION:** Protect the crossing without abandoning the blockade.

**DEKA:** I'll do what I can with the cruiser I have. If you know where to find a second one, I'll gladly accept it.

**DOMINION:** Your predecessor was also fond of presenting limitations as invitations.

**DEKA:** She taught us a great deal about limitations.

*The link closes. The tactical officer studies Deka.*

**TACTICAL:** You didn't mention what you'll do if the line breaks.

**DEKA:** I thought I'd wait for it to break. I prefer to be disappointed by the actual disaster.

### NEW SCENE V4-A — THE WRONG KIND OF SIGNATURE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P05; VENN HAS NO COMMAND)

*FLEET DISCIPLINARY TRANSIT — REVIEW CABIN. The cabin has been made comfortable in the efficient way that makes it impossible to forget whose cabin it is. A desk, a narrow sleeping berth, a working comms panel and a door that opens only when the escort officer authorizes it. The ship hasn't left Fleet jurisdiction. Commodore Ella Venn is reading a civilian freight-repair estimate on an ordinary portable screen. She has circled an item and written the word “why” beside it in pencil on paper.*

*Admiral Soren Kerr appears on a supervised connection. He doesn't bother with a greeting.*

**KERR:** Tell me that's not the report from Auric I think it is.

**VENN:** That depends which report you think it is.

**KERR:** The one you asked an Admiralty records clerk to retrieve under your retired access privileges.

**VENN:** I haven't retired.

**KERR:** Precisely. You have no retired access privileges. You also have no active command privileges. You're managing to occupy an impressively small legal space.

**VENN:** It was a publicly tendered estimate for thermal couplings. The clerk sent me the wrong copy. I told him so.

**KERR:** After circling the part number.

**VENN:** He'd already sent it. Was I supposed to close my eyes?

*Kerr considers her. The old irritation between them is familiar enough not to require anger.*

**KERR:** What are you trying to do, Ella?

**VENN:** Auric needs people who can verify an improvised corridor coupling without turning the job into a Fleet installation. I'd like the name of the technician who did the independent surveys before Kaldr reopened.

**KERR:** You know her name.

**VENN:** I know her *former* name. She retired eight years ago. She may not want to speak to me. I'm hoping you know whether she still does that work.

**KERR:** You want me to introduce a suspended officer to somebody who may then be seen arranging independent equipment for Auric while a Fleet screen holds Tal Corvus.

**VENN:** When you put it like that, it sounds needlessly suspicious.

**KERR:** How would you put it?

**VENN:** A woman looking for a mechanic.

**KERR:** A woman who used to move heavy cruisers by saying a sentence and now needs a mechanic rather badly.

*Venn is amused despite herself. Kerr doesn't let the amusement settle.*

**KERR:** I cannot obtain contacts through the Admiralty directory for you. I won't pretend the request came from me. And I certainly won't make it look as if you're still assigning work on behalf of Fleet.

**VENN:** I didn't ask you to pretend.

**KERR:** You didn't have to. That was once a useful thing about serving with you.

*Venn puts the pencil down.*

**VENN:** You're right. I'm sorry. I thought if I gave you the smallest possible request, it wouldn't feel like a request at all.

**KERR:** It's quite a request when you know the person on the other end can lose a career for helping.

**VENN:** Have you lost yours?

**KERR:** Not yet. You're making it a strenuous afternoon.

*From the cabin corridor comes the modest clatter of a meal trolley. Venn looks at the untouched tray waiting at the hatch.*

**VENN:** There's a public registry of civilian survey licenses. I can use that without you.

**KERR:** You can. Whoever answers can refuse you without a Fleet admiral standing behind your shoulder.

**VENN:** And if nobody answers?

**KERR:** Then you discover what other people have to put up with when they aren't you.

*He is about to close the connection, but stops.*

**KERR:** I hear Deka has the third-window screen.

**VENN:** She does.

**KERR:** You could write her a note.

**VENN:** About what?

**KERR:** That's what I wanted to find out.

**VENN:** She knows how to fly that ship. I won't send her a suggestion disguised as encouragement.

**KERR:** Good. That would have been the second wrong kind of signature today.

*He disconnects. Venn turns to the public directory. She composes a message without rank or Fleet insignia. It takes her longer than any order she's issued in years. The recipient is a licensed civilian surveyor, not an Admiralty officer. She leaves the first sentence unwritten for a full minute before beginning.*

### SCENE 3 — THE BOUNDARY (P06–P07)

*DOCK GANTRY. Abby has posted an evacuation protection boundary. Pike has put Broken Oath where it will be unmistakably visible to the people below. Billie has a patrol within sight of the forbidden lane, careful not to cross it yet.*

**PIKE:** A boundary is a splendid thing. Everybody can see exactly where your authority stops.

**ABBY:** It's where my captains agreed to protect civilians. They don't need to answer to me anywhere else.

**BILLIE:** You make it sound like our people volunteered to be janitors for Fleet's traffic.

**ABBY:** They volunteered because the ships carry families from our own warrens.

**BILLIE:** I know who they carry.

**ABBY:** Then stop making them nervous for the pleasure of making Fleet nervous.

**PIKE:** My position is outside the lane.

**ABBY:** Your cameras are outside it. Your hull is half across the reserve's turn.

*Pike gestures toward the waiting crowd, whose members can see his unmistakable silhouette.*

**PIKE:** The people need to see somebody defending them.

**ABBY:** They need the reserve to get past you if a tug fails. You're confusing being seen with being useful.

*Later, Tess joins Abby on the maintenance rail. Below them a loader is testing the same mechanism for the fifth time.*

**TESS:** You know what happens if you keep telling captains they're not useful? They start trying to prove otherwise.

**ABBY:** I told Pike to move. I told Billie not to shoot. That's been my entire diplomatic achievement today.

**TESS:** It's early. You can offend the other half before lunch.

*Abby glances toward Gravefire.*

**ABBY:** You're directly across Billie's approach. I wouldn't put anyone else there.

**TESS:** You mean nobody else would agree.

**ABBY:** I mean I trust you.

**TESS:** That's worse. Now I have to be careful.

*She squeezes Abby's shoulder as she passes. Abby watches her go with an anxiety that never enters her command voice.*

### SCENE 4 — THE WARM BERTH (P08–P10)

*AURIC FREEPORT. Halev has ordered freight cleared from a heated hall to receive Tal Corvus evacuees. Wallace is counting the lost throughput. Naomi is counting the minutes until the transit window. Jex arrives with three hostile screen traces and a private worry he will not explain.*

**WALLACE:** Those pallets were booked through tomorrow. We move them and they'll spend another day on the dock, charging us for the privilege.

**HALEV:** I know. Clear the floor anyway. People can't sleep on pallets no matter how expensive they are.

**NAOMI:** We can get the skiffs to the exit. What we can't do is park them there while your arrivals desk debates whether a family has a berth.

**HALEV:** Then the desk meets them at the dock.

**JEX:** I'd suggest it meet them with better news than last time.

**WALLACE:** What would you suggest, specifically?

**JEX:** Not charging them for a night they haven't lived through. But while I'm being generous, I have something else.

*He shows the three independently positioned hostile interception screens. Naomi recognizes the geometry before anyone else.*

**NAOMI:** One takes Gravefire. One takes Sunlash. The third waits for the reserve to chase either of them.

**JEX:** If I charged you for that observation, could you look a little more impressed?

**NAOMI:** You didn't get this from a public notice.

**JEX:** No.

**HALEV:** What do you want?

**JEX:** Keep a channel listening for Tali. If anybody hears from that corner of the route, don't put it in a council report before you tell me.

*Halev agrees. Naomi moves Sunlash to the threatened exit anyway.*

**NAOMI:** They'll know we've seen them.

**JEX:** I was hoping you'd surprise them.

**NAOMI:** I intend to. The surprise is that I'll be there before they are.

### NEW SCENE V4-B — AN OFFER WITH A HANDLE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P10; BEFORE THE THIRD WINDOW OPENS)

*The supervised civilian link is delayed by the same traffic emergency everyone is trying to navigate. Venn waits while Auric's connection drops twice. When Captain Naomi Sol finally appears, she is in a freight operations passage carrying two handheld instruments. An arriving dockhand asks her for a docking number. She gives him one without looking away from Venn.*

**NAOMI:** I have eight minutes before we bring Sunlash into position. If this is a speech about shared purposes, I can send you the one I made last month. Nobody listened to that either.

**VENN:** I can make it shorter. I found a civilian calibration service familiar with old reciprocal-field couplings. They're willing to quote for your damaged arrivals equipment.

**NAOMI:** Quote to whom?

**VENN:** Auric's maintenance office.

**NAOMI:** Good. I was afraid you were going to say Fleet.

**VENN:** They want access to the original sensor measurements. Not the whole operational picture. Just the physical readings.

**NAOMI:** Will their people see our port registrations? Our cargo contracts? Where we put the refugees afterward?

**VENN:** I asked for measurements. They asked for the full bundle. I haven't answered.

**NAOMI:** Then answer no.

**VENN:** It's more complicated. They need to know which instruments are lying to each other.

**NAOMI:** They can have sensor types, tolerances, and timestamps. They don't need the names of the families sleeping beside them.

**VENN:** I could have asked that.

**NAOMI:** You could have. You know what you sound like, Commodore? Somebody who's never had to persuade a maintenance crew to let her take a look without giving her the keys to the entire building.

**VENN:** For a long time the maintenance crews worked for me.

**NAOMI:** Exactly.

*Naomi shifts the instruments under her arm and steps out of the way of an arriving trolley of blankets. She doesn't ask Venn whether the room on the other side of the screen is a detention cabin. Venn realizes that she has noticed.*

**VENN:** They aren't my people now. If this service comes, Auric will employ them, supervise them, and decide what information to provide. No Fleet installation. No authority over your traffic.

**NAOMI:** Why did you go looking for them?

**VENN:** Because you told me to come back with something useful.

**NAOMI:** I didn't think you'd take that as an assignment.

**VENN:** Old habits.

**NAOMI:** You asked about *useful*. Useful would be three working heat exchangers, seventy new berths, and a freighter that stops arriving missing pieces. What you've found is a person who may be able to tell us why our old equipment fails. That's a beginning, not a gift.

**VENN:** I know the difference.

**NAOMI:** Do you? The last time we spoke, you described every limitation Fleet had. You were very accurate. I spent the next week trying to heat people with your accuracy.

*Venn receives the remark without trying to improve its presentation.*

**VENN:** I can send Auric the contact and the limitations. You can decide whether to call.

**NAOMI:** Send it to Sera. She'll find the person who actually owns the equipment. If they're allowed to say no, I'll believe this is different.

**VENN:** I won't contact Deka about the window.

*Naomi looks up sharply.*

**NAOMI:** Why would you?

**VENN:** I thought you might wonder.

**NAOMI:** Erin doesn't need you to tell her where the shields go. She needs somebody to make sure she won't be punished for keeping them there. Can you do *that*?

**VENN:** No. Not from here.

**NAOMI:** All right. That's a real answer.

*There is a call from Sunlash. Naomi turns toward it.*

**NAOMI:** I've got a departure. Send the contact. If you're tempted to attach a letter explaining your intentions, don't. Just send the number.

**VENN:** Captain Sol?

**NAOMI:** What?

**VENN:** Good crossing.

**NAOMI:** I'll try to make it boring.

*Naomi breaks the connection. Venn sends a single civilian contact and the narrow specifications. The message contains no request for favorable publicity, no demand for acknowledgement, and no Fleet tasking. The civilian contractor has made no commitment and no equipment has moved. Outside the review cabin, the third-window operation proceeds without her.*

### SCENE 5 — A VERY NARROW ROAD (P11–P15)

*The hostile screens hold their positions. Broken Oath's camera-facing stance obstructs the reserve. Billie nudges Ashfang close enough to make civilian tugs tighten their approach. None of the attackers needs to be inside Abby's protective boundary to put civilians in danger.*

**TACTICAL:** They're pulling our escorts in different directions. Pike's sitting where the reserve needs to turn.

**DEKA:** Tell him there's a tug behind his cameras. If he wants a good picture, he can try turning around.

*On Gravefire, Tess studies the cramped departure mouth.*

**TESS:** Thunderbreak, Ashfang is squeezing the lane. I can bring the tugs out, but I can't turn them twice.

**DEKA:** I'll give you our armored side.

**TESS:** Good. And keep the guns away from their noses. One frightened pilot sees a battery turn and we'll all spend the evening catching pieces of tug.

**DEKA:** Batteries stay dark. Get the people moving.

*At the gantry Abby sends Pike a direct warning.*

**ABBY:** Move the ship. You don't have to leave the watch, just stop blocking the reserve.

**PIKE:** If I move, the audience will think I'm backing down.

**ABBY:** There won't be an audience if those skiffs don't launch.

**PIKE:** You don't understand what it means for people to see a captain stand his ground.

**ABBY:** I understand perfectly. I also know a ship can stand its ground in the wrong place.

*Pike does not move in time. The window is opening.*

### SCENE 6 — THE FIRST SHOT (P16–P22)

*The third window opens. Civilian skiffs begin departing under ordinary measured corridor markers. Sunlash takes the far exit. Gravefire walks the line out of the berth, while Deka keeps Thunderbreak between the tugs and the waiting screen.*

**SECOND:** The second hostile screen is shifting to make us follow it.

**NAOMI:** Then let it enjoy the trip. Keep our nose on the exit marker.

*Billie fires a warning shot outside the protected lane. The sound will never reach the civilians, but its consequences will: two skiffs dodge, a tug loses thrust near drifting wreckage, and another crosses toward Fleet's reserve line.*

**TESS:** They panicked on the first flash. I warned you that would happen.

**BILLIE:** I didn't hit the lane.

**TESS:** You didn't have to.

*Deka commits Thunderbreak's armored flank to shield the scattered tugs. Pike sees the first vessel drifting beyond safety. For a moment his camera feed shows the faces of people who cannot wait for him to look heroic. He swings Broken Oath out of its prepared shot.*

**PIKE:** Kill the broadcast angle. Bring us around.

**PILOT:** That puts our stern in the pressure wake.

**PIKE:** I can see that. The tug has children on it.

*He retrieves the first tug, damaging his carefully maintained profile. The second keeps drifting.*

**TACTICAL:** The other tug's outside the reserve. We can't reach it from here without opening the flank.

*A junior Fleet escort officer hears the report from their own bridge. No one has ordered the officer to abandon station.*

**ESCORT OFFICER:** I'm going after it.

**ESCORT SECOND:** Standing orders say hold.

**ESCORT OFFICER:** I've read them.

*The escort turns. An obvious and dangerous gap opens in the screen.*

**TACTICAL:** Deka, the junior escort's left formation. They've exposed our reserve flank.

**DEKA:** I see it. Bring Thunderbreak around. We cover the gap until they're back.

**TACTICAL:** That gives the third hostile screen a clear look at us.

**DEKA:** Then let's give them the old side. The one we can afford to scrape again.

*Thunderbreak turns. The junior escort reaches the stranded tug while Deka absorbs the formation cost. At the Relic Court, Dominion watches the unauthorized break and the covering maneuver.*

**DOMINION:** That escort left its station without authority. If the next ship does the same, who guards the rest?

**SELENE:** Deka held the gap. The tug's crew has been brought aboard.

**DOMINION:** The first fact doesn't vanish because you prefer the second.

**SELENE:** I don't prefer facts, Admiral. I enter them. Both, if you'll allow it.

*He makes her hold the recording. His objection is not theatrical; he has seen formations fail because captains each saved the nearest ship.*

### SCENE 7 — TESS DOESN'T LEAVE (P23–P24)

*Gravefire stays in the departure mouth. Heat wash eats through its limited cooling margin. The port radiator is already damaged and now takes another strain. An adult passenger is shaking so badly he cannot fasten a restraint.*

**PASSENGER:** They've gone. The escorts have gone.

**TESS:** They're at the exit. They're meant to be at the exit. You'd be worried if they were still sitting here.

**PASSENGER:** I can't get the buckle closed.

**TESS:** Here. You pull this. No, the other part. It's a terrible buckle. I designed a better one once and nobody paid me to install it.

*She closes it for him, then returns to the helm while the engine crew reads off increasing temperatures.*

**ENGINEER:** Port radiator won't take much more.

**TESS:** How much is much?

**ENGINEER:** I was hoping you'd ask for something less specific.

**TESS:** Then tell me when we have to get clear. Until then, the last skiffs get their escort.

*The final craft passes into protection. Tess takes Gravefire out on an exhausted and damaged cooling system.*

### SCENE 8 — WHAT BILLIE WON'T BURN (P25)

*ASHFANG MARAUDER — COMMAND. The ship has been out of position since the warning shot and its crew knows everyone is talking about the scattering of civilian tugs. Billie has listened to the aftermath reports twice and asked her officer to stop playing them. A Triarch emissary comes aboard while Ashfang's fuel officer is trying to explain how long the ship can remain on station.*

**EMISSARY:** The independent agricultural yards are to be rendered unusable. They have supplied food outside the Confederacy allocation system.

**BILLIE:** Those people grow vegetables.

**EMISSARY:** They control distribution without authority. The instruction is to disable the harvest equipment and growing decks. No civilian casualties are required.

**BILLIE:** A generous distinction. We won't kill the farmers. We'll just take away the thing they've been using to stay alive.

**EMISSARY:** The Confederacy can supply their needs through recognized routes.

**BILLIE:** When?

**EMISSARY:** Once the yards are secured.

**BILLIE:** They aren't even getting the things we've already promised them. I know, because I've spent the last month trying to get fuel to take those things from somebody else.

**EMISSARY:** You made that difficult when you failed to obey the interdiction order.

**BILLIE:** I fired on the convoy approach. The tugs scattered. You want me to apologize for that or do it again? Because I've had both messages from your office today.

*The emissary waits. Billie's officer remains at his station, deeply uncomfortable.*

**EMISSARY:** The point of the operation was to weaken independent transit, not to make its participants admired. The food yards are another route of independence. We must control them.

**BILLIE:** They're Shards.

**EMISSARY:** So were the districts that refused inspection.

*That answer is too close to Richard Secundo's published evidence. Billie hears it. The officer looks down.*

**BILLIE:** I know those yards. We took a damaged engine coil to one because they had a working shop. They fed us while we waited. The woman running the grow deck made us wash the dirt off our boots before we went near it. She was furious about the mess. I'm not going back there with a cutter and calling her harvest a military target.

**EMISSARY:** Your tanker allotment remains conditional.

**BILLIE:** It's been conditional for so long I've forgotten what an unconditional delivery looks like.

**EMISSARY:** Without fuel you cannot maintain Ashfang's patrol schedule.

**BILLIE:** That's a problem I intend to have.

*The emissary closes his case. Billie does not rise to make the refusal more dramatic.*

**BILLIE:** Tell them no.

*After the emissary departs, the fuel officer returns with his figures. Billie has heard enough figures for one day.*

**OFFICER:** We can remain on station another watch. After that we choose between getting home and keeping pressure on the route.

**BILLIE:** Then we go home.

**OFFICER:** And if the Triarch won't refuel us there?

**BILLIE:** We find someone who will sell to us.

**OFFICER:** You mean ask. After everything we've done to the freight lines.

**BILLIE:** I know what the word means.

**OFFICER:** Some of those growers were on the ship we frightened today. Do you think they'll sell to Ashfang?

*Billie thinks of the panicked tugs and the faces she deliberately avoided watching.*

**BILLIE:** I don't know. And I am not going to make you tell me they will.

**OFFICER:** You could call Abby.

**BILLIE:** She's not taking my calls.

**OFFICER:** Neither are you taking hers.

*Billie gives him a look. He doesn't retract it.*

**BILLIE:** Get the ship home. I'll work out who I owe an apology to when nobody on this bridge is running out of air or fuel.

**OFFICER:** That could be a long list.

**BILLIE:** I have excellent stamina when it comes to being disliked.

*He leaves to prepare the return course. Alone, Billie reopens the incident feed. She watches just enough to see the tug break off toward the danger, and closes it again. Refusing the farms has not undone what she did.*

### SCENE 9 — INVOICES FOR SURVIVAL (P26–P31)

*Auric receives the final skiffs. Families are warm for the night, though some sleep on the freight hall floor. A relief pallet did not survive. Naomi reports that loss to Halev instead of allowing the passage to be remembered as perfect.*

**NAOMI:** All the registered families made the crossing. We lost the relief pallet.

**HALEV:** The heated berths are ready. The food allotment was calculated with the pallet.

**NAOMI:** Then recalculate it. I'm not telling them the story ends when the door opens.

*At the dock Pike returns with the tug he saved. People begin shouting his name. He seems ready to enjoy the acclaim until he sees the crew struggling to unload a stretcher from a narrow hatch.*

**PILOT:** Captain, your broadcast's live again.

**PIKE:** Turn it off. Somebody bring the lower ramp here.

*He steps into the work instead of posing. Abby observes without commenting. The gesture does not absolve the captain's earlier obstruction.*

*In the shadow warrens, the Fear Mask orders selective release of real footage. The records are authentic; their arrangement is calculated to imply that no crossing can be trusted.*

**FEAR MASK:** They will remember the danger better than they remember the arrival. They need only be shown what frightened them.

*At Auric, the girl from the queue finds her mother in a warm berth. She looks for a permanent room on the arrivals board and finds none.*

**CHILD:** Are we staying?

**MOTHER:** Tonight we are.

**CHILD:** Tomorrow?

**MOTHER:** Tomorrow we ask again.

*Outside the berth, Naomi hears it. She takes the lost-pallet accounting to the civic office herself.*

### NEW SCENE V4-C — THE PART OF THE RECORD SHE DOESN'T OWN (AFTER SOURCE P31; BEFORE THE LATER DEKA INQUIRY)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — RECORDS CONFERENCE ROOM. The Tal Corvus skiffs have reached Auric. Their supplies haven't miraculously multiplied. The older incident report on Venn's own rescue remains in the Fleet case files. Admiral Janet Keating has brought that file in person. Kerr is present as an observer. Venn has been granted an interview, not a command consultation.*

**KEATING:** You requested preservation of the escort's live recording, the civilian tug's pressure log, and three non-Fleet observations from the departure mouth.

**VENN:** Yes.

**KEATING:** You'll understand why that looked like an attempt to build Lieutenant Deka a defense before anyone had even questioned her.

**VENN:** It would be a defense if I knew what happened. I don't. We have a Fleet summary with a large blank where the formation changed.

**KEATING:** The blank is because part of the tactical track remains restricted.

**VENN:** Then preserve it under restriction. I'm not asking you to send it to Auric. I'm asking you not to decide what the evidence means before we can put the different witnesses in the same room.

**KEATING:** You want to put a Shard pilot in a Fleet disciplinary proceeding.

**VENN:** I want the pilot who was there to be available to the independent record. Whether the Fleet accepts testimony is your decision, and one you'll have to defend.

**KEATING:** You've become very fond of saying which decisions are mine.

**VENN:** I haven't got many left.

*Kerr almost smiles. Keating hears him thinking about it.*

**KEATING:** Your own tribunal file is still open on appeal. The record shows you chose civilian rescue over a supply convoy, and then refused the segregation order. You've objected to how those decisions were characterized.

**VENN:** I've objected to the suggestion that the supplies weren't real, or that the people weren't. Both mattered.

**KEATING:** Then you'll have no objection to attaching the complete account of the supplies Auric lost. Not only your bridge log, not only the survivors who thanked you. The invoices, the failed heat allotments, the medical shortages.

*Venn is silent for the first time. She glances toward Kerr. He gives her no assistance.*

**VENN:** That record will be read as an argument against me.

**KEATING:** It is an argument against part of what you did. I thought you preferred complete records.

**VENN:** I do. I don't particularly enjoy them.

**KEATING:** Neither do I.

*Keating turns the file around. Venn can see the early estimates, still imperfect, and the names of Auric workers who have had to sign for depleted stock.*

**VENN:** Include them. And the return readings from the passenger hull. Not as competing tallies. As the two consequences of one order.

**KEATING:** If the civilian witnesses consent, they can submit their own records. They don't become Fleet informants by doing so. I'll authorize the preservation notice. I won't promise access or a favorable finding.

**VENN:** I didn't come for a finding.

**KEATING:** You're very difficult to believe when you say that.

**KERR:** She has been consistent about being difficult.

**KEATING:** Consistency is not the same as good judgment, Admiral.

**KERR:** I'm beginning to notice.

*Keating collects the folder. Before she leaves, she stops at the door.*

**KEATING:** One more thing. You will not contact Deka or her junior escort to shape their testimony. If they want counsel, they will have counsel. Not a former commander with a long memory for what her officers owe her.

**VENN:** Agreed.

**KEATING:** You said that quickly.

**VENN:** Because I was tempted to do exactly that.

*Keating studies her, startled by the admission. Then she leaves.*

**KERR:** I thought you might ask me to call Erin.

**VENN:** She deserves a room where nobody is waiting for her to agree with me.

**KERR:** You know Dominion won't thank you for expanding the record.

**VENN:** I wasn't hoping for thanks. I was hoping he would have to read it.

*The signed preservation order goes forward. The later inquiry will take its own course. Venn has influenced what can be examined, but neither Deka's decision nor Deka's testimony belongs to her.*

### SCENE 10 — THE RECORD (LATER INQUIRY; INSERTION POSITION AND LENGTH UNDECIDED)

*ADMIRALTY INQUIRY. The rescue is over. Every civilian tug that made the crossing is accounted for, but the reports disagree on what the Fleet formation was meant to protect. Deka has been giving evidence for hours. Her uniform is clean. Her hair has begun coming loose around the temples. Dominion sits across the table with the independent escort commander's deposition and the actual ship telemetry. Selene Stormwell operates the verified record. No ship has been declared innocent simply because the people aboard survived.*

**SELENE:** The junior escort left the assigned reserve position at 14:06. At 14:08 Thunderbreak altered shield orientation and course to cover the opening. For the interval between those decisions, the reserve line was exposed.

**DEKA:** That's correct.

**DOMINION:** Remarkably comfortable with the admission.

**DEKA:** Would you prefer I got the times wrong?

**DOMINION:** I'd prefer you understood why you're here.

**DEKA:** I've had three hours to form a theory.

**DOMINION:** Let's hear it.

**DEKA:** You want to know why I put a heavy cruiser into a hole somebody else made.

**DOMINION:** I want to know why you treated that hole as an invitation to make another. The escort's unauthorized departure is under separate review. This inquiry concerns the captain who was responsible for the rest of the formation.

**DEKA:** Then you have the right captain.

*Selene changes the display to the instant before the junior escort turned.*

**SELENE:** The drifting tug was beyond the reserve's permitted recovery edge. Its propulsion was failing. The junior escort transmitted an intention to assist, then crossed the formation boundary before receiving permission.

**DOMINION:** Which the officer knew was prohibited.

**SELENE:** Yes.

**DOMINION:** Then there's no mystery about the first decision. Why cover it, Deka? That escort had already decided to be a hero. You could have ordered it back and preserved the screen.

**DEKA:** I did order it back.

*Selene brings up the communications log.*

**SELENE:** Recall sent at 14:06 and acknowledged at 14:07. The escort reported it could not reach the tug and return before its power failed.

**DOMINION:** And you believed that.

**DEKA:** The tug's drive record agreed. You can look at it. The escape attitude was drifting. In another minute the crew might not have been able to orient its pressure hatch toward a rescuing ship.

**DOMINION:** Your job wasn't to decide whether the tug deserved rescue. Your job was to keep the civilian convoy protected while a designated reserve conducted it.

**DEKA:** Our designated reserve was the ship that left its station.

**DOMINION:** Exactly. It made a bad decision. You made a second decision that enlarged the risk.

**DEKA:** Yes.

*Dominion closes the report. Deka's agreement has left him without the argument he prepared.*

**DOMINION:** That is not the defense I expected.

**DEKA:** I'm not sure what you expected, Admiral. I can give you the names of the people who came home or the names of the ships I left exposed. Both lists are on the table. I'm not going to pretend one doesn't exist because it makes the other easier to discuss.

**DOMINION:** A formation is a promise between captains. You hold your place because the captain beside you is counting on you. A ship beyond the boundary is not permitted to take away that promise from everyone behind it.

**DEKA:** I know. That's why I didn't follow the escort out. I moved Thunderbreak across the gap it left. I kept the civilian route covered. It cost us the reserve flank and put our own damaged armor where the third hostile screen could reach it.

**DOMINION:** Had that screen fired, your crew could have died. The other ships might have followed.

**DEKA:** Yes.

**DOMINION:** And you don't appear particularly disturbed by that.

**DEKA:** I'm disturbed enough to have watched the recording every night since we came back. You keep asking me for an explanation that sounds like confidence. I don't have one.

*Selene adjusts her chair. The archive chamber has been warm all morning. Deka looks cold.*

**SELENE:** A question of terminology. Should the record call Thunderbreak's move a second breach of formation or a protective displacement of the reserve?

**DOMINION:** Both, if both are true.

**SELENE:** They are. I wanted to know whether you would permit the same movement to have two descriptions.

*Dominion gives her a tired look.*

**DOMINION:** I had not intended to make you choose adjectives today.

**SELENE:** You usually do, Admiral. Today they happen to matter.

*Deka nearly smiles. The relief lasts an instant.*

**DOMINION:** If the same sequence occurred tomorrow, would you cover the escort again?

*Deka looks down at the record. There is an easy answer available, and she knows exactly how it would sound.*

**DEKA:** I hope not.

**DOMINION:** That's not an answer.

**DEKA:** It's the only one I have.

**DOMINION:** Then give me a useful one. You have a ship and a crew. If you cannot say what you would do with them under the same conditions, perhaps you shouldn't command either.

**DEKA:** If I tell every junior officer I'll always cover them, one of them will eventually break formation expecting me to clean up the result. If I tell them I will never cover them, one of them will eventually sit in a warm cockpit and watch people die because a line on the display told them not to move. Those aren't hypothetical officers, Admiral. I've served with both kinds.

**DOMINION:** And yet you expect your decision to be treated differently from theirs.

**DEKA:** No. I'm asking you not to make mine into a rule that saves anybody from thinking next time.

**DOMINION:** Rules exist because captains cannot consult their consciences while a formation collapses.

**DEKA:** Then I'm a poor advertisement for the profession. I was frightened. I thought the escort would be lost. I thought we'd lose the port armor. I heard the freighter pilots asking whether our shields were still there, and for a moment I couldn't remember whether I'd given the turn before or after I'd told tactical to check the third screen. I've gone over the recording more times than is sensible.

**SELENE:** You requested the third-screen check before the turn. The order is marked at 14:07.

**DEKA:** Yes. I know that now.

*There is a silence in which Dominion's anger no longer has an obvious place to go.*

**DOMINION:** There were children aboard the convoy behind you as well as aboard the tug.

**DEKA:** Yes. That's what frightens me. If the screen had fired, I'd have been the captain who gave up their protection for the crew she could see in trouble. I'd have made the same decision with the same information and caused a different disaster. I understand that very well.

**DOMINION:** And what do you want me to do with that understanding? Reward you for being honest about the gamble?

**DEKA:** No. I'd like you to believe me when I say I knew it was a gamble.

*Dominion sits back. Selene has not stopped recording.*

**DOMINION:** Your predecessor also believed an officer could make a dangerous decision for compelling reasons. Look where it brought her.

**DEKA:** I was on that bridge, Admiral. I know where it brought her. It brought us a ship that still works and a new captain who hasn't had a proper night's sleep since the ceremony. It brought her a tribunal. I don't find that a particularly tempting career plan.

**DOMINION:** You are not being judged on Venn's record.

**DEKA:** Then don't keep bringing her into mine.

*Selene looks up. Even Dominion seems surprised by the sharpness of it. Deka realizes how it sounded and does not withdraw it.*

**DEKA:** Sorry. That was unwise.

**DOMINION:** It was.

**DEKA:** But you were going to say it eventually, weren't you?

*Dominion considers that, more carefully than she expected.*

**DOMINION:** Yes. I was.

*Selene turns to the tug's final recovery recording. The junior escort has brought it to safety. The footage shows workers hauling a jammed hatch open; a parent and child wait inside behind a makeshift pressure barrier. The parent is trying to ask something, but the audio is poor.*

**DEKA:** I didn't go out there. I didn't open the hatch. That escort officer did. I had a cruiser to fly and a formation to keep together. The tug pilot sent me a message afterward thanking me for saving them. I tried to explain who'd actually done the rescue, but they didn't seem interested in the distinction.

**DOMINION:** Does it matter to you?

**DEKA:** Yes. It matters to me that the officer who crossed the line owns that choice. It matters that I own the cover. Otherwise nobody has to explain anything. We can just call it a rescue and wait for everyone to applaud.

**DOMINION:** There will be no commendation from this inquiry.

**DEKA:** I hadn't asked for one. Would it have come with a full night's leave?

**DOMINION:** Lieutenant.

**DEKA:** Right. Not the time.

*But even Dominion's expression softens for a moment. Selene lets the joke pass into the record instead of editing it away.*

**DOMINION:** The finding remains open. The junior escort's testimony must be heard separately. Until then you retain command, subject to the standing operational restrictions. Is that clear?

**DEKA:** Perfectly.

**SELENE:** I'll enter both decisions, the resulting exposure, and the recovery. No final attribution of fault until the other witness is heard.

**DOMINION:** Every word.

*Deka stands, then hesitates. After three hours of sparring, what she asks next is something she seems almost embarrassed to care about.*

**DEKA:** Could somebody tell me whether they repaired the tug? I've asked the service office twice and they keep sending me back to the inquiry desk.

*Selene checks another record.*

**SELENE:** Returned to service yesterday. Civilian berth twelve.

**DEKA:** Oh.

*A small smile escapes before she can stop it.*

**DEKA:** That's good.

*Dominion looks at her, recognizing the question as a kind of evidence he cannot use to settle the case. It neither excuses the maneuver nor makes him want her to have acted differently. He dismisses her without another lecture. Selene stays to finish entering the record, including the small question about the repaired tug.*
