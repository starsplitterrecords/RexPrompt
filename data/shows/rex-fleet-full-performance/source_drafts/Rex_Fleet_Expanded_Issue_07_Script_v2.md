# REX FLEET — FULL PERFORMANCE DEVELOPMENT SCRIPT
## Source Issue 07 — The Calm Corridor

**Revision v2 — dramatic expansion of RF_I07_P01–P19.** Seven continuous scenes. The existing page identifiers describe source beats, not a maximum page count. Dialogue and staging are development proposals. The recurrence is physical and its cause unknown. Venn has limited task-group authority; Deka commands Thunderbreak.

### SCENE 1 — A PERFECTLY GOOD READING (P01–P02)

*THE LATTICE. A survey ship proceeds through the Calm Corridor, a route whose instruments have been boringly reliable. Its captain has arranged her life around boring instruments. A junior survey officer discovers a star duplicated in two contradictory directions. It isn't marked on any of the overlays.*

**SURVEY OFFICER:** Captain, I have a question about the aft camera.

**CAPTAIN:** If it's about the grease on the housing, the technician has already promised to clean it.

**OFFICER:** Why is the navigation star behind us also ahead of us?

*The captain stops writing. She approaches the console, looking first at the officer, then the feed.*

**CAPTAIN:** Which one is a reflection?

**OFFICER:** I hoped you'd tell me.

**CAPTAIN:** Don't do that. Tell me what you measured.

**OFFICER:** Optics, ranging and passive field return agree. Same spectrum. Same drift. It moves when we alter attitude, only less than it should. There are two returns, and every timing beacon says we're exactly where we were supposed to be.

**CAPTAIN:** You've checked the independent clock?

**OFFICER:** Three times.

**CAPTAIN:** Check it again.

**OFFICER:** Do you want a different answer?

**CAPTAIN:** Desperately. But I'll settle for a true one.

*He checks. The ship's clocks agree to the tolerance they had before the departure. A second duplicate appears, a faint reflection of an old probe at the edge of the passage.*

**OFFICER:** That wasn't on the earlier pass.

**CAPTAIN:** How fast is it moving relative to us?

**OFFICER:** That's the problem. The optical position says it's ahead. The range return says it never left the volume behind us.

**CAPTAIN:** And our actual separation from the nearest anchor?

**OFFICER:** Stable.

**CAPTAIN:** Very good. We have one fact that can still be trusted. Reduce speed. I want an independent position check from the next beacon and a complete record on an isolated circuit.

**OFFICER:** Shall I report an unsafe corridor?

**CAPTAIN:** Report an unexplained observation inside a corridor whose verified measurements remain in tolerance. Don't tell people they're safe on the strength of a number that has just made a fool of us. Don't tell them they're doomed either.

*The officer begins the transmission. A long pause passes while they watch the duplicated star. Both know that the most alarming thing is not that their system has failed. It is that the system hasn't failed in any measurable way.*

**OFFICER:** My father travels this route next week.

**CAPTAIN:** Then let's give him something better than a story about a second star.

### SCENE 2 — THE COST OF BEING CERTAIN (P03–P04)

*VEIL MARKET. Naomi is at Jex's broker table with the unedited survey records. His customers have already heard three versions of the anomaly, including one that involves a ship with no crew.*

**JEX:** Do you know what the first man asked me? Whether the duplicated star was worth the cost of the ticket. As though I were selling tickets to a view.

**NAOMI:** You sell guarantees.

**JEX:** Of windows. Timing. Where a freighter can be without being sheared apart. I have never guaranteed the astronomical objects will behave themselves for the passengers.

**NAOMI:** You've guaranteed a safe arrival based on the clocks. The clocks are giving us a reading that doesn't fit what the cameras can see.

**JEX:** The clocks have been independently checked.

**NAOMI:** That's why I'm not sleeping.

*He takes the record from her and scans it. For once, the numbers tell him nothing useful.*

**JEX:** I could sell a warning. Narrow the next window, charge a premium, tell people we're taking special precautions.

**NAOMI:** Are we?

**JEX:** No. I said I could sell one.

**NAOMI:** Then you should be ashamed of yourself.

**JEX:** I am. I'm also trying to understand what happens to this market when every trader decides the roads have gone mad. Both feelings are available to me.

*On THUNDERBREAK, Jia Morgan works at a measured field display. Selene Stormwell has brought old logs. Venn and Deka arrive from separate workstations, neither pretending the ship has gained a second captain.*

**JIA:** The sensor arrays aren't disagreeing. They're reporting phase-repeated returns within the verified volume. I can quantify the offset. I cannot tell you why it happens.

**DEKA:** Can it move a ship?

**JIA:** I have no evidence it has moved any mass at all.

**DEKA:** Good. Then I'm not ordering anyone to shoot a reflection until you've decided whether it can shoot back.

**SELENE:** There are older Academy records of vessels appearing beside themselves near lost anchors. Entire crews wrote accounts. They couldn't all have imagined it.

**JIA:** They may have seen something. They didn't have instruments that could separate an optical return from a real target. If you ask me to use the records as a warning, yes. If you ask me to use them as measurements, no.

**SELENE:** There was a time when people called the Shattering an error in their maps.

**JIA:** And there was a time when people called infection a punishment. Historical descriptions are evidence that someone observed a problem, not evidence that their explanation was right.

*Selene looks wounded, but not because the scientific point is wrong.*

**SELENE:** I'm not asking you to believe the explanation. I'm asking you not to throw away the observation because it comes from frightened people who didn't have your equipment.

*Jia stops, considering that.*

**JIA:** That is fair. Give me their raw descriptions. I'll keep the interpretations separate.

**VENN:** Good. I need a working restriction, not an argument about who deserves to be right. What can our escorts do differently today?

**JIA:** Stop mirroring their course changes until I know whether synchronous inputs alter the effect.

**DEKA:** Fine by me. They were making formation flying far too fashionable.

*Dominion appears on the command channel. He can hear only the professional end of an argument that has been going on for some time.*

**DOMINION:** Commodore, the Admiralty has confirmed your temporary field appointment. You will coordinate Fleet assets in the corridor. Lieutenant Deka remains in command of Thunderbreak. The tribunal finding stands.

**VENN:** Understood.

**DEKA:** I should hope so. I only just got the chair adjusted.

**DOMINION:** Lieutenant, I don't imagine you need reminding what this means.

**DEKA:** Not unless there's something new in it.

**DOMINION:** Very well. Use your judgment. And record it.

*The channel closes. Venn stays beside Deka for a moment.*

**VENN:** I haven't come back for the chair.

**DEKA:** I know. I didn't expect you to. It's the rest of the ship that worries me. When I give an order, half the crew still looks to see whether you approve.

**VENN:** I'll speak to them.

**DEKA:** Don't. Let me. They're my crew now, and I need them to understand that before we have to do something ugly.

*Venn accepts the correction. Deka is pleased, but doesn't turn it into a ceremony. Behind them Jia starts drafting a test.*

### SCENE 3 — WHO GETS TO BE FRIGHTENED (P05–P07)

*The Tal Corvus service passage has been closed because a thin physical seep has formed on a structural seam. Tess has been asked to look at it. A maintenance worker keeps leaning toward the phenomenon, attracted by the way the light seems doubled along its edge.*

**TESS:** If you touch that before the pressure crew gets here, I'm going to tell your supervisor you fell into it trying to impress me.

**WORKER:** I wasn't trying to impress you.

**TESS:** Then you have even less excuse.

**WORKER:** People are saying the corridors are making copies of us.

**TESS:** People said my radiator repair was finished last month. People say all sorts of things.

**WORKER:** Are you frightened?

*Tess crouches to examine the seam, but keeps her hands off it.*

**TESS:** Yes. I'm frightened of the pressure reading changing after I've put people through the next door. I'm frightened of a child getting curious while I stand here talking to you. Those I can do something about. Go get the rope barrier.

*The worker does. Tess waits, watching the seam without claiming it is alive or aware.*

*On ASHFANG, Billie watches the footage in sour silence. Her officer has seen her do something reckless before and is prepared for it.*

**BILLIE:** If there's a second ship out there, what happens if we fire on it?

**OFFICER:** We don't know there is a second ship.

**BILLIE:** That's why I asked.

**OFFICER:** We also don't know whether weapons will affect whatever the return is. We'd be firing through the same volume the civilian craft use.

**BILLIE:** Am I ordering you to load a gun?

**OFFICER:** No.

**BILLIE:** Then I'm permitted to wonder aloud. I know everyone has forgotten it, but I used to be allowed that on my own bridge.

*She switches off the most dramatic piece of footage. It had been cut to repeat the moment of apparent duplication.*

**BILLIE:** Who made this version?

**OFFICER:** It came from the Arena Vault.

**BILLIE:** Naturally. Nothing travels faster than something that might frighten somebody.

*In the ARENA VAULT, the Fear Mask watches the same clip. Its emissary has the original transmission, complete with the survey captain's uncertainty.*

**EMISSARY:** The uncut report says the transit measurements remain within tolerance. If we distribute only the images, anyone who sees the original will know we've removed the context.

**FEAR MASK:** They may. Many won't wait for context before refusing to travel.

**EMISSARY:** You want the corridor shut?

**FEAR MASK:** I want the pilots to ask us whether to enter it. Today the pilots have their own instruments and their own witnesses. Tomorrow they may require reassurance from an authority who knows what to show them.

**EMISSARY:** And if the anomaly is harmless?

**FEAR MASK:** Then they'll be relieved. Relief is a powerful thing to supply to someone you've frightened.

*The emissary doesn't share the Mask's satisfaction. He has relatives traveling through that corridor. He asks for the uncut feed to be retained in the official archive and gets no reply.*

### SCENE 4 — THE OTHER GRAVEFIRE (P08–P10)

*Sunlash leads a mixed civilian convoy into the Calm Corridor. Gravefire escorts the rear. The first echoed ship appears beside Tess's cutter. It has the same damaged radiator housing, the same hull markings and a tiny lag in its attitude change. It looks almost real enough to make a pilot want to salute it.*

**SECOND:** Naomi, there's a second Gravefire on our starboard optical feed.

**NAOMI:** Does it have a second Tess? Because one of them could take a shift.

**SECOND:** One transponder. Two returns. The second return appears to be answering the primary before it receives the signal.

*Naomi stops smiling. She changes attitude slightly and watches the real escort respond.*

**NAOMI:** Tess. Tell me what you see. Don't tell me what you think it is.

**TESS:** My ship. From an angle nobody should be able to see it. And somebody's fixed the radiator on the reflection. Very poor attention to detail.

**NAOMI:** Is your hull pressure steady?

**TESS:** All normal. Cooling's still a problem. We are making no move toward the other ship.

**NAOMI:** Good.

**TESS:** You were going to say that without asking me, weren't you?

**NAOMI:** I was. I know it's rude.

*An inexperienced civilian captain turns toward the bright duplicate, believing it marks the safe escort path. His freighter begins to deviate from the verified volume.*

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** I've got Gravefire crossing my bow. I'll follow its turn.

**TESS:** No. You have a return that looks like Gravefire. The real one is behind you, and I've never steered you into a wall before. Keep your course.

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** The other one looks closer.

**TESS:** Close your forward visual if you have to. Use the instruments we verified at the last anchor. You don't have to like the view.

*His hands tremble at the controls. Naomi can hear breathing over the open circuit.*

**NAOMI:** Your last course correction was good. Give me that exact turn back, slowly. You're not being asked to trust the whole universe. You're being asked to keep one ship in one measured lane.

*The pilot manages the turn. The visual echo drifts across the canopy and becomes more vivid, as though inviting a second correction. He holds course.*

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** I have children aboard.

**NAOMI:** I know. Let's get them home without teaching them to fly by chasing pictures.

*The pilot says nothing further. Tess watches his freighter regain the verified course while the second Gravefire lingers impossibly beside the first.*

### SCENE 5 — THE UNTIDY ANSWER (P11–P15)

*THUNDERBREAK. Jia has grouped every recorded course adjustment, firing impulse and timing input. Venn asks to see only the observations relevant to the convoy currently in the corridor.*

**JIA:** There's a pattern. When the ships repeat one another's corrections at nearly identical intervals, the echoes become more coherent. We can see a relationship. We haven't proved the field's mechanism.

**VENN:** Does that relationship survive different instruments?

**JIA:** Yes. Not all of the measurements have the same precision, but the tendency survives.

**DEKA:** If we stop making corrections together, what happens to the escorts? They're trained to keep that formation.

**JIA:** I believe the echo coherence will decline.

**DEKA:** Believe, or know?

**JIA:** Believe. I would rather be wrong in a way we can measure than certain in a way that gets someone killed.

**VENN:** We can't pause the convoy for a laboratory experiment.

**NAOMI:** We can make the convoy the experiment. I know the spacing margins. I can vary the freighters' turns and arrival intervals without losing the safe volume.

**DEKA:** You're going to ask merchant captains to abandon the escort pattern while a second copy of their ship stares at them.

**NAOMI:** Yes. I'm not delighted about it either.

**DEKA:** Then tell them why. Don't give them the word 'desynchronization' and expect it to keep them calm.

*Venn opens the civilian circuit. She starts formally, then stops. The pilot's breathing can be heard over Naomi's channel.*

**VENN:** Your escorts have a proposed maneuver. It changes the formation you were told to trust. The corridor remains within the measured transit boundary, but we are seeing duplicated returns we cannot yet explain. Captain Sol will give you individual directions. Do not mirror the ship ahead of you unless she specifically tells you to.

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** Is something about to hit us?

*Jia glances up. Venn doesn't answer until she has a useful distinction.*

**VENN:** Nothing we've measured suggests a second physical vessel is approaching you. We are changing the flight pattern because the existing one appears to make the echoes worse.

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** That's not very comforting.

**VENN:** No. It isn't. But it's what we know.

*Deka turns toward her tactical crew.*

**DEKA:** Cancel synchronized fire triggers. Independent defensive authority only. No shooting because the ship beside us shot at a shape. If someone has an actual physical target, I'll hear it from the officer responsible.

*One of the officers hesitates.*

**TACTICAL:** Captain, we may lose a firing opportunity.

**DEKA:** I am prepared to miss a ghost. I am not prepared to put a round through a freighter because a ghost looked convincing.

*Naomi begins splitting the convoy deliberately. Each pilot receives a different speed and attitude instruction. Tess takes the trailing ship in hand while keeping Gravefire within its limited cooling margin. From the outside, it looks disorderly. To people accustomed to elegant formations, it looks like a mistake.*

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** Sunlash, we're losing our escort geometry.

**NAOMI:** I know. That's deliberate. You've still got your safe corridor and you've still got us. You're allowed to hate this maneuver while you're doing it.

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** I already do.

**NAOMI:** Then we're ahead of schedule.

*The echo behind Gravefire begins to blur. The bright duplicate appears to move out of phase, then breaks into ordinary reflections. Jia watches the readings rather than the imagery.*

**JIA:** Coherence is falling. Timing inputs remain valid. This is the response I predicted.

**SELENE:** Does it tell us why the original measurements stayed healthy?

**JIA:** No. It tells us we can influence a phenomenon we still don't understand. That's not a small thing. It's also not an explanation.

*Venn watches the last freighter make the unsynchronized turn.*

**VENN:** Log the procedure and the uncertainty. The next convoy deserves both.

### SCENE 6 — A CUSTOMER WHO WANTS MAGIC (P16–P17)

*VEIL MARKET. Jex has a customer demanding an exclusive alert service. The buyer has enough money to pay more than an entire relief crew earns in a season. Jex knows it, and knows how easy it would be to put a premium label on the incomplete readings.*

**BUYER:** I don't need a lecture about phase. I need a private forecast of which windows to take.

**JEX:** The most honest forecast is the public survey report. It gives the verified measurements and explains what we don't know.

**BUYER:** I can read a public report myself.

**JEX:** Congratulations. You're already receiving better service than half my customers.

**BUYER:** I pay to know before the others.

**JEX:** I can sell you earlier notice of a real change in the window when one has been measured. I can't sell you the power to make a passage behave differently for your ship.

**BUYER:** Other brokers will.

**JEX:** Yes. Some of them will be wrong for less money. I've never understood why that improves the bargain.

*The buyer pushes a payment token back across the table. Jex doesn't take it.*

**BUYER:** I used to come to you because you knew things other people didn't.

**JEX:** I still know quite a few things they don't. It's getting harder to tell which ones are worth keeping secret.

**BUYER:** Is that supposed to make me feel better?

**JEX:** No. I don't offer emotional guarantees. Those are even less reliable than the crossing windows.

*The buyer leaves. Jex looks at the unaccepted token for a moment and then starts helping a courier verify a route correction without charging for it.*

*At Shardfall, the Fear Mask watches the successful convoy footage. Its emissary has prepared a list of the survey crew, pilots, market workers and couriers who have been distributing the original measurements.*

**EMISSARY:** The footage contradicts our warning that the corridor was unusable. It has already been copied beyond the city.

**FEAR MASK:** Then the footage is no longer the only problem.

**EMISSARY:** What do you want done with the people who copied it?

**FEAR MASK:** Learn who relies on them. A courier doesn't have to be believed by everyone. Only by the people who expect them to arrive with the next correction.

*The emissary studies the names. One belongs to someone who used to carry medicine for his family. He doesn't say so. The mask doesn't ask.*

### SCENE 7 — THE LAST LIGHT (P18–P19)

*The convoy reaches the far berth in a ragged pattern, safe and unmistakably unheroic. One freighter has to wait while a service tug clears the approach. Tess watches the docking sequence from Gravefire, standing beside the chair she has refused for the past several hours.*

**ENGINEER:** Every ship made it. You can stop staring at that display.

**TESS:** One hasn't docked.

**ENGINEER:** It's clear of the corridor.

**TESS:** Which is not the same as being docked.

*He sits in the chair she was saving for herself.*

**ENGINEER:** This chair tilts to the left. Did you know?

**TESS:** I know everything on this ship that tilts to the left. There's a short list of things that don't.

**ENGINEER:** You're exhausted.

**TESS:** I know. Thank you for the diagnosis.

*The last freighter receives its docking lock. A light steadies.*

**ENGINEER:** There.

*Tess finally sits, deliberately choosing the less comfortable chair because it is closer. The engineer has already stood to offer his. She takes the cup of water he hands her and drinks before speaking.*

**TESS:** The little freighter. Did someone check the pilot after he went after the reflection?

**ENGINEER:** Naomi did. He's shaken. Everybody aboard is all right.

**TESS:** Good. Tell the repair crew I'm not going anywhere for an hour.

**ENGINEER:** They'll celebrate.

**TESS:** Tell them not to. They'll wake me.

*She closes her eyes. Outside, the corridor looks perfectly ordinary. Somewhere inside the measured volume, an unknown effect has behaved differently because people changed their behavior. Nobody has explained it, and nobody has to pretend they have in order to bring the ships home.*
