A HOME IS NOT A BERTH

## Issue 4 — From the Editor — The Third Window (RF_I04_EDITOR_ENDMATTER)

FROM THE EDITOR  
THE THIRD WINDOW  
At Tal Corvus, families do not get an abstract debate about freedom. They get one departure window, a child drawing the ships she hopes will come, and a choice about who will stand between them and the blockade.  
Abby tries to set a boundary without claiming command of every Shard captain. Tess holds the departure mouth in Gravefire while Deka brings Thunderbreak's shields alongside; neither commands the other's ship. Naomi reads the danger in Jex’s warning and keeps the civilian ships moving. Even Pike, drawn first to the camera, crosses the line to save a stranded tug. The families make it through—but the cost follows them home.  
A NOTE FROM THE FRONTIER  
A corridor window is brief and shared. Civilian pilots, Fleet escorts, and independent Shard crews must agree on timing long enough to get people through, even when none agrees who should lead.

## Issue 5 — From the Editor — The Cost of a Signal (RF_I05_EDITOR_ENDMATTER)

FROM THE EDITOR  
THE COST OF A SIGNAL  
Jex makes route information public because people cannot cross safely on rumors. That choice helps civilian pilots find an open window—and gives raiders the same advantage. When Billie turns desperation into an attack on a relief freighter, the price is counted in lost crates, damaged ships, and families waiting at the ration door.  
Naomi trusts a hand-drawn correction because it gets people to food. Tess puts Gravefire between the freighter and Ashfang, though her ship is already hurt. Billie withdraws, but the harm remains. This is a frontier where doing the useful thing can expose someone else to danger—and where people still choose to share what little arrives.  
A NOTE FROM THE FRONTIER  
A route bulletin is more than a line on a map. Its timing can save a freighter, feed a neighborhood, expose a convoy, or give a captain leverage over people with no other way through.

## Issue 12 — From the Editor — End of the First Run (RF_I12_EDITOR_CLOSE)

FROM THE EDITOR  
When we started building Rex Fleet, the idea was much smaller than the universe it eventually demanded.  
The starting point was the corridors: if civilization depends on a network of routes through dangerous space, somebody has to maintain them, defend them, map them and decide who gets to use them. From there came the Fleet, the Verge and the Shards—not simply as opposing factions, but as people with very different relationships to the same roads.  
That gave us the world. The characters changed the book.  
Tess, Abby, Naomi, Venn, Jex, Billie and the rest gradually stopped feeling like pieces positioned around a political conflict and started pulling the story in their own directions. Some relationships became more important than we expected. Characters who seemed destined for one role refused to stay there. A story that began with fleets and borders increasingly became about what people do when the institution they belong to no longer gives them an adequate answer.  
The Rift changed too. Early on, it was primarily the environment through which these people traveled. By the time we reached the Calm Corridor and the Mirror Star, it had become something much stranger. We still know more about what the Rift can do than the characters do—but not nearly enough to make it comfortable.  
Making these twelve issues also taught us what Rex Fleet itself wants to be. It can hold battleships and refugee skiffs, political arguments and engineering problems, strange physics, marketplaces, old grudges, bad jokes and people trying to get dinner through a checkpoint. The enormous things work better when the ordinary things remain visible underneath them.  
That is the part we want to keep exploring.  
This issue closes the first major Rex Fleet story, but it does not close the universe. There are ships we have barely visited, regions we have only named, histories that have only been hinted at, and parts of the Lattice that nobody in this book has seen.  
Most importantly, there are still stories we want to tell with these people.  
Thank you for reading all the way to the end of the first run—and for giving us the chance to find out what Rex Fleet could become.  
We’re not finished with it.  
— The Editor
