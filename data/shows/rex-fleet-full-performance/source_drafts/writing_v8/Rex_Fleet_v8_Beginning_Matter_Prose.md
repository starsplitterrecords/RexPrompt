# Issue 1 — A World Before the Story: Before the Crossing

Distance used to be a fact of life. Then, for a long time, it became the fact that determined nearly everything else.

Humanity had spread among the stars along a network of engineered passages known as the corridors. A ship entering one of these carefully measured volumes could travel at speeds impossible in ordinary space. The passages were not shortcuts granted by a generous universe. They relied on reciprocal fields, precise timing, stable reference points, and people who knew the difference between a safe crossing and a reading that only appeared safe. Keeping them open was work. So was keeping the ships that used them alive.

Then came the Shattering.

The corridors did not simply grow inconvenient. Reliable fast travel broke down across the settled regions, and places that had depended on each other abruptly found themselves separated by journeys that could take months or years. Some routes remained possible at punishing cost. Others were lost, damaged, or too uncertain to trust. News traveled poorly. A shipment could arrive after the people who ordered it had moved, died, or learned to live without it. An emergency somewhere far away could become a historical footnote before anyone capable of helping heard about it.

Life went on because it had to.

At the center of the old network, the Core held on to much of its technical and military inheritance. Its Fleet protected what could still be reached, maintained vessels built for a larger civilization, and taught its officers a doctrine with two obligations: Reach and Resolve. Reach meant finding the way back to people. Resolve meant helping them endure when the way was gone. The officers who kept that promise through generations of isolation had reason to take pride in it. The people who waited for help that never arrived had reasons of their own.

Beyond the Core, the Verge developed a different understanding of survival. Its farms, markets, ports, local governments, and working escorts had to make decisions without expecting a central authority to solve them. A route belonged, in practice, to the people who knew how to fly it. A merchant's timetable could matter as much as a warship's orders. The Verge was not waiting to be civilized when the old connections returned. It had been governing, trading, repairing, and raising families all along.

Farther out, in places shaped by wreckage and scarcity, the Shards built communities from what the Shattering left behind. Their settlements have engineers and farmers, repair crews and children, captains and political authorities. They have also learned to take supplies from traffic they cannot always persuade to stop. Some call that piracy. Others can point to the empty holds and histories that made it seem necessary. Neither word can tell the entire story of the people living there.

Between all of them are independent traders, brokers, pilots, and the crews who keep routes moving when nobody agrees who ought to control them.

Now the corridors are beginning to reopen.

A passage that once consumed a generation's patience can suddenly become a practical journey again. For a family, it might mean a new home. For a farmer, a market that pays enough to keep next season's crop growing. For a settlement short of food, the sight of a freighter on the approach. For a navy, a chance to restore a service it believes civilization cannot survive without.

But reconnection does not return people to the moment when they were separated. The governments have changed. The borders have changed. People have learned what to fear, whom to trust, and what it costs to depend on someone else. Faster travel gives every old argument less distance in which to cool.

Even the corridors themselves remain demanding. No ship can bargain with shear, drift, or a failed timing reference. The mathematics can be checked. A hull can still strike debris. An arrival window can still close. And after the last course correction, someone must decide which vessel goes first and who will stand between it and danger.

On Luminara, a grain freighter is preparing to depart. Cargo crews are securing food grown by people who need the shipment to arrive. A mother and her son are taking their belongings aboard because somewhere beyond the crossing, they believe, there may be a better place to live.

The route is open again.

That is the beginning of the story, not the end of anyone's problems.

# Issue 2 — Previously in Rex Fleet: The Price of an Open Route

The Green Corridor has made a journey between Luminara and Auric possible in a fraction of the time it once required. It has not made the journey safe.

Captain Naomi Sol led a Verge grain freighter through Kaldr's hazardous approach with Fleet cruiser Thunderbreak guarding its flank. Shard cutters reached the cargo despite Commodore Ella Venn's attempt to break their approach. Three containers of grain were stolen; a fourth ruptured during the withdrawal. The passengers survived, but Auric received less food than it had counted on.

The stolen grain did not vanish into somebody's private fortune. It reached hungry households at Black Drift. Abby Saville, Tessa Banks, and Billie Rusk know exactly what the supplies mean to those families. They disagree about what continued raiding will do to the merchants they need to keep sailing. Meanwhile, route broker Jex Marrin knows more about the interception than the people who suffered it.

Auric's governor, Chris Halev, has accepted a trial arrangement: Fleet protection and technical support, with Verge civilian traffic and freight still under Verge control. The faster route promises prosperity, but arriving families are already finding the celebrated freeport crowded and short of heat.

The next ship needing refuge will not arrive at a convenient time.

# Issue 3 — Previously in Rex Fleet: What the Rescue Left Behind

The evacuation freighter Mercy of Dawn came to Auric under attack, carrying Verge and Shard families who needed a safe berth more urgently than they needed permission to approach one. Commodore Ella Venn abandoned her assigned supply screen to answer their distress. The decision saved people and put Auric's heating and medical supplies at risk. She later refused a Fleet detention order that would have separated patients from their caregivers.

The rescue was hardly the work of one uniform. Naomi Sol led civilian contact. Richard Secundo and his son Paul kept people alive aboard a ship coming apart. Tessa Banks helped protect passengers from raiders wearing her own faction's colors. Abby Saville opposed Billie's use of civilians as leverage, and independent Shard captains began making different choices about whom they would follow.

Some freight reached Auric. Some was lost or damaged. Every survivor needed somewhere to sleep. Governor Halev opened disputed rooms in the Crown Ring, but those apartments still had owners, the building still had faults, and the city's heaters still had limits.

Venn has handed Thunderbreak to Lieutenant Erin Deka and must answer to the Admiralty. Auric has only a provisional civilian rescue arrangement, not a settlement between the powers.

A ship has been saved. The people aboard it still need a home.

# Issue 4 — Previously in Rex Fleet: An Open Road, an Unfinished Reckoning

Auric has survived an attack on its residential ring, but the city has not escaped the questions that brought the attack to its doors. The rooms are still crowded. Heat and medicine remain scarce. Families who lived there before the refugee arrivals have claims as real as the claims of those who fled.

Dr. Richard Secundo has made public the original records from Tal Corvus. Some of the shortages imposed on Shard districts were not mechanical failures or unavoidable consequences of isolation: supplies were deliberately diverted after political protests. The evidence spread despite an attempt to silence him. Its publication has strained the authority of the Silent Triarch and divided people who once fought alongside one another.

Abby Saville and six independent Shard captains helped Naomi Sol defend civilian transports. Billie Rusk's attack failed, but the damage to her friendships and to Auric remains. Jex Marrin helped open hidden approaches for evacuees; his sister Tali has since disappeared from contact, and he does not know whether she escaped.

Fleet's tribunal has removed Commodore Ella Venn from command. Deka holds Thunderbreak. Venn still has her experience, her reputation, and relationships she cannot use as orders. Auric has chosen to offer refugees a chance to remain under its own civilian authority.

On Tal Corvus, other families are preparing to leave. They have a departure window. Whether anyone can protect it is another matter.

# Issue 5 — Previously in Rex Fleet: After the Third Window

Tal Corvus's evacuation passed through a corridor that everyone knew would be contested. The plan nearly failed when armed ships pressed the departure lane and two civilian tugs drifted out of safe formation.

Tessa Banks held Gravefire at the departure mouth, damaging the ship's already vulnerable cooling systems while guiding the final craft through. Naomi Sol used warning from Jex Marrin to keep another hostile screen from isolating the convoy. Oren Pike abandoned his carefully staged position long enough to recover a stranded tug. A junior Fleet escort officer left formation without permission to reach another; Thunderbreak's commander, Erin Deka, chose to cover the gap that officer opened. Those were two decisions by two different officers, and Fleet is keeping a record of both.

The families reached safety. Their arrival did not replenish Auric's stores, repair Gravefire, or settle the competing explanations each faction offered for the rescue. Nor did it erase the pressure on Billie Rusk, whose refusal to destroy independent Shard food production has complicated her position with the Triarch.

Off her former bridge, Ella Venn has begun discovering that an old acquaintance may answer her calls without being free to act on them. She cannot relieve Deka of responsibility, and she cannot promise Auric help the Fleet has not authorized.

The rescue brought people through. Feeding them will require another crossing.

# Issue 6 — Previously in Rex Fleet: The Public Clock

In Veil Market, people have been learning what a route is worth when a ration door is shut. Jex Marrin, who once earned his living selling privileged crossing information, opened a verified public timing bulletin so civilian pilots could find the next safe passage. It helped freighters move. It also gave raiders the same information.

Billie Rusk, short of fuel and supply after defying the Triarch, attacked a Verge relief shipment. Twenty-eight of the promised hundred crates were lost. Seventy-two reached Auric, where the arithmetic became smaller portions on actual plates. Gravefire, still damaged from Tal Corvus, placed itself between Billie's guns and the freighter. Billie withdrew rather than fire through Tess Banks's ship.

Deka kept Thunderbreak's limited defensive protection available without taking command of the Shard escort. Abby restricted the intelligence shared with Billie's crew, though she could not issue orders to that independent captain. The convoy that followed showed how much work cooperation required even when nobody fired.

Meanwhile, copies of Jex's route information have begun moving through working crews faster than any single authority can suppress them. The Triarch has noticed. So has Venn, who is learning to secure voluntary technical help rather than issue orders she no longer has the right to give.

The next crossings will carry both food and medicine. Someone has decided they should not be allowed through.

# Issue 7 — Previously in Rex Fleet: Seven Decisions

At Auric Gate, the Silent Triarch attempted to stop civilian traffic by placing Shard pickets across the available crossing windows. Auric had supplies waiting on the other side and two brief openings, half an hour apart. Naomi Sol led the exposed food convoy through the first. Tessa Banks brought medical freight toward the second.

The blockade did not end because one commander won a battle. Abby Saville sent a dangerous message to captains whose crews had their own loyalties and families. Seven of them independently turned their weapons away from the civilian lane. Billie Rusk used Ashfang to shield Gravefire from a loyalist attack without joining Abby's rebellion. Deka held Thunderbreak's defensive overlap and refused to let Fleet claim credit for the captains' decisions. Food came through with damage to one freighter; the medical crossing survived without a firefight.

Those decisions have consequences inside the Shards, where obedience and standing cannot be replaced by a single public statement. Around Tal Corvus, crews have also observed an unfamiliar physical disturbance in the built environment, with no reliable explanation for it yet.

Venn's influence beyond the chain of command has grown, but she has not recovered Thunderbreak. The coming problems will test whether cooperation can be built around evidence instead of authority.

For the first time in a while, the danger ahead may not be an enemy ship.

# Issue 8 — Previously in Rex Fleet: The Trouble with a Perfect Reading

The Calm Corridor appeared stable by every ordinary measurement. Then a survey ship saw a second image of itself, following its actual maneuver after a measurable delay. The anomaly was real enough to endanger navigation, but neither the instruments nor the physicists could yet explain it completely.

Jia Morgan found that the delayed returns grew more dangerous when ships fired their maneuvering systems in unison. Formation guidance began treating the echoes as real hulls while actual civilian vessels lost safe separation. Naomi Sol broke the synchronized pattern with staggered manual instructions. Tess Banks kept Gravefire and its passengers on the real course rather than chasing an image of her own ship. The convoy came through because different captains trusted a shared measurement without making identical movements.

The Fear Mask used frightening recordings to pronounce certainty where researchers could only report observations. The attempt to turn uncertainty into obedience met witnesses who had seen the crossing for themselves.

But the Shards have other ways of contesting authority. Abby Saville's independent captains have already challenged what the Triarch can demand of them, and Tess's standing carries weight beyond her own vessel. Their problems at home have not disappeared while the corridor has been misbehaving.

# Issue 9 — Previously in Rex Fleet: What a Victory Doesn't Feed

At Shardfall, Oren Pike challenged Tessa Banks before witnesses, turning the shortages and unfinished repair debts of the Shards into an argument about who deserved to lead. Pike proved that he could fight. Tess won, with a real injury to her left forearm, and then refused to kill him. The outcome changed what the witnesses were willing to say in public. It did not change how much food was available.

Two more Shard captains withdrew from the Raid Mask's muster. The Triarch responded by freezing their berths, placing costs on crews who had made their own political choices. Abby Saville must live with the consequences of offering those captains an alternative she cannot guarantee will be easy.

While the challenge drew attention, Naomi Sol found a safer way to move an overcrowded ration line through Veil Market. Jex Marrin and the market workers are still dealing with diverted supplies, incomplete deliveries, and the difference between food that exists on a ledger and food a family can actually reach.

A watched duel can end in an instant. A hungry market cannot.

# Issue 10 — Previously in Rex Fleet: What the Ledger Cost

Someone tried to kill Jex Marrin while Veil Market was already close to breaking under real shortages. The attempt helped provoke panic, a fire, and a struggle over stored food. Jex survived, injured, and opened reserves that had once been part of his private commercial advantage. When the fire threatened the passage used by rescuers, he surrendered more access and allowed workers to cut through goods he had expected to sell.

That decision saved routes for food and medicine. It also left honest buyers without purchases they had already paid for. Jex's records began linking the attacker, paid agitators, and diverted cargo, but those connections do not mean all of Veil Market's hunger was manufactured. Naomi Sol followed the evidence without confusing sabotage with scarcity itself. Venn and Sera Dain helped obtain medical access without handing Fleet an invitation to occupy the market.

Jex has released enough of his transactions for others to verify the pattern while holding back private refuge routes that might still matter to his missing sister, Tali. Copies are beyond his control now.

What happens next will depend on what those payments purchased—and on the people who have to live with the answer.

# Issue 11 — Previously in Rex Fleet: The Last Skiff

The emergency at Shatterforge began with equipment that should have protected its people. Industrial service vents were deliberately locked, forcing families toward departures that could be controlled. Tess Banks reached the manual relief system. Abby Saville brought Scythe of Saville over the exhaust, using her ship's shields to keep the discharge away from occupied terraces while the evacuation proceeded.

The last skiffs took longer than the shield system could safely promise. Venn, directing Fleet resources, chose to protect the final civilian craft rather than draw that protection toward Abby's increasingly exposed ship. Scythe stayed over the vent. After the last craft cleared, the ship vanished behind the furnace bloom, its fate unconfirmed. Tess got the rescued people docked before turning to the question she could hardly bear to ask.

Abby had secured authentic evidence of the deliberate vent lock before contact was lost. Jex made the payment trail public, including his own part in the network that carried it. Billie refused orders to obstruct independent departures. Pike's attempt to control the image of events left Broken Oath damaged.

Shatterforge's families reached shelter. The evidence reached beyond it. Abby and her ship remain unaccounted for.

At Auric Gate, the next confrontation is already gathering ships.

# Issue 12 — Previously in Rex Fleet: A Road Still Open

Auric Gate was carrying civilians before the fleets began fighting over it. Fleet, Verge, and independent Shard ships faced one another across a passage none could afford to lose. Pike sought a battle the cameras would remember. Venn declined a shot that would have put civilians behind the target at risk. Naomi, Halev, and Tess kept traffic moving even as the formation broke apart.

Then the Gate's physical readings began to echo the battle. Jia Morgan found that synchronized weapons fire was coupling to the same anomalous returns previously measured in the Calm Corridor. The harder the ships fought in step, the less reliable their picture of the real traffic became. Stopping synchronized volleys was a technical necessity, not a sudden end to the fighting.

Abby Saville returned aboard a damaged Scythe with survivors and captains who had chosen her. She directed them toward the civilian lane. After Verge escorts relinquished their offensive locks, Venn asked Thunderbreak to power down its offensive batteries and cover civilians. Deka, still commanding the cruiser and responsible for its exposed flank, made the ship-handling decision. Other captains chose their own restraint. Billie Rusk prevented Pike from turning the remaining confusion into a massacre.

The firing stopped. The echoes diminished. The road remained open, scarred and difficult, for the ships that had waited through the battle.

No faction surrendered its principles, command, or claim to have acted correctly. The next civilian convoy still needs an agreed time to cross.
