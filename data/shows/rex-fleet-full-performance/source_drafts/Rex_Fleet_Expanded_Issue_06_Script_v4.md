### SCENE 1 — SUPPER (P01)

*SHARDFALL CITADEL — ARENA VAULT. The Trade Mask sits with a captain whose vessel has been held at the Gate picket. He has come wearing a work jacket with one sleeve freshly patched. A young clerk beside Trade turns the pages of a practical freight manifest. Nothing in the room is ceremonial except the fact that the people making the decision do not have to stand in the queue.*

**TRADE:** The manifests. Inspection access. Then the grain moves.

**CAPTAIN:** It was inspected when it was loaded.

**TRADE:** By whom?

**CAPTAIN:** The growers. The carrier. The men and women whose names are on the freight bond.

**TRADE:** Verge names.

**CAPTAIN:** Most of the grain is for Auric, yes. That was the arrangement before we stationed guns across their route. It hasn't become a different arrangement because you've become angry with it.

*The clerk glances up. Trade does not.*

**TRADE:** You think I enjoy preventing food from reaching anyone?

**CAPTAIN:** I think you have eaten recently.

*A movement among the guards. The captain doesn't look at them.*

**TRADE:** Mind your manner.

**CAPTAIN:** My crew's families live under the same roof your allocation officers use. We were supposed to receive a share from the return trade. Instead the cargo's sitting in a vacuum and I'm here asking permission to bring home a ship full of people who haven't been paid.

**TRADE:** That is precisely why the inspection must be recognized. If we surrender the registry now, we will be asking for every future shipment as a favor.

**CAPTAIN:** The registry won't be much comfort to my youngest. She wants to know what we're having for supper.

**TRADE:** An effective negotiating position must last longer than a single meal.

**CAPTAIN:** She's six. I'll try explaining long-term leverage. Perhaps she'll stop asking for bread.

*Trade turns to the clerk.*

**TRADE:** The captain's berth remains registered. His crew will receive an allocation after inspection.

**CAPTAIN:** What allocation?

**CLERK:** The amount hasn't been calculated.

**CAPTAIN:** You see? Even your clerk won't make up a number for you. I'd hire her, if my ship were allowed to leave.

*Trade's face remains concealed. The clerk lowers her head to the manifest.*

**TRADE:** You can return to the picket. Or relinquish your berth to a captain prepared to carry out its purpose.

**CAPTAIN:** If you take the berth, you take the crew with it. That's what you keep forgetting. Those people were there when we welded it together. It isn't a chair you can give someone else.

**TRADE:** This audience is concluded.

*The captain takes back his empty travel case. Outside, a worker who has waited the entire hearing asks if the freighter will come through tonight.*

**WORKER:** Did you get it?

**CAPTAIN:** No.

**WORKER:** What did you tell them?

**CAPTAIN:** The truth, mostly.

**WORKER:** That was stupid.

**CAPTAIN:** Evidently.

*The worker goes back to the waiting crowd, furious. The captain stays for a moment. He knows the worker's family will go hungry before his own.*

### SCENE 2 — THE HALF HOUR (P02–P03)

*AURIC — GOVERNOR'S WORK ROOM. The air in the room is warm, though the adjoining hall is crowded with people who are not. Naomi has two independently verified reciprocal-field windows on the tactical plot, thirty minutes apart. Jex has brought his own crossing figures. Halev keeps rubbing his eyes and pretending he hasn't.*

**NAOMI:** The first opening is narrower. Two food freighters can take it, provided neither captain gets adventurous. The second opens thirty minutes later. Medical transports have the better approach geometry there.

**HALEV:** Food first. Medicine second.

**JEX:** Would you like a moment to make that sound less dreadful?

**HALEV:** The first ships can survive the narrow approach. The medical craft can't. We lose both loads if we swap them. I thought I might begin with the part that's true.

**JEX:** Very brave. The families outside won't ask for a field calculation. They'll ask why the sick are waiting while a freighter full of grain goes first.

**NAOMI:** Then give them the calculation, Chris. Don't dress it up. The medical ships have less shear tolerance. It's not a judgment about which people you like better.

**HALEV:** I'll have the arrival teams explain it. Where do we berth the food ships?

**NAOMI:** Where you told me you had room last week.

**HALEV:** We did have room last week.

**NAOMI:** I've learned to treasure that expression. Usually it means somebody's turned a cargo floor into a bedroom.

**HALEV:** Somebody did. Me.

*Jex tilts his head toward the closed door.*

**JEX:** The merchants say you've been giving their docking reservations to people who don't pay.

**HALEV:** The people are paying. Just not with money I can put against the freight budget.

**JEX:** A complicated way to run a city.

**HALEV:** You don't run cities.

**JEX:** I have the sense not to.

*Naomi taps her two timing marks.*

**NAOMI:** Neither window is a guarantee. We still have pickets across the approach. I can lead the first crossing. Tess will need a shield overlap for the second.

**HALEV:** Will Fleet provide it?

**NAOMI:** Deka can provide defensive cover within her order. I've spoken to her. She'll tell us exactly how much. I'm not asking her to promise the other ships will behave.

**JEX:** I'll send out the current timing corrections. Publicly. Every pilot sees the same figures.

**HALEV:** Including Billie.

**JEX:** Including Billie. If I could arrange for information to be read only by people with charming personalities, I'd be a richer man than I already am.

**NAOMI:** We wouldn't let you have the information.

**JEX:** See? That's why I like you.

*Halev turns back to the arrival roster. A transport liaison enters. Her mother is on one of the medical ships.*

**LIAISON:** They heard they're second.

**HALEV:** Tell them why. You can use my name.

**LIAISON:** They already are.

**HALEV:** Fair enough. Tell them I know thirty minutes is a very long time when it's somebody you love who's waiting.

*She nods, not satisfied. Naomi heads for Sunlash. Jex leaves to publish updates rather than sell them. Halev remains to face the questions neither of them can answer from a flight deck.*

### NEW SCENE V6-A — THE PART YOU CAN MEASURE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P03; NO CHANGE TO THE TWO-WINDOW PLAN)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — DOCUMENT ROOM. Two civil Gate windows have already been publicly announced by Jex and Auric. The Shard pickets and each captain's choices remain entirely outside Venn's control. Keating has agreed to let Venn attend a review of what Fleet's verification instruments are permitted to disclose to a civilian crossing authority. Kerr is there because he was willing to sign his name to the request. A technician has brought the relevant technical records, not a tactical display.*

**KEATING:** Before we begin, nobody in this room is reopening the order for the Gate screen. Deka has the assigned vessel. The civilian authorities have the windows they intend to use.

**VENN:** I wasn't going to challenge the crossing plan.

**KEATING:** You challenged two orders in one afternoon. My confidence in your intentions is not quite as robust as it once was.

**KERR:** We might save time by treating every sentence here as a proposal, not an order.

**KEATING:** That is precisely what I was going to suggest, Admiral. Without the air of discovery.

*The technician opens a recorded test. Two successive field readings disagree within a tolerable range; the verified transit boundary remains unchanged.*

**TECHNICIAN:** What the civilians asked for is the instrument uncertainty and the test method. What the division asked me to release is a single certification mark. If I give them only the mark, they cannot tell when their older equipment has drifted.

**KEATING:** If you release the raw Fleet recordings, you may reveal where our survey vessels stand and what their instruments can observe.

**VENN:** Then remove the ship positions. Publish the tolerance band and the method used to arrive at it. Auric can compare against its own independent sensors.

**TECHNICIAN:** We can do that. It won't tell them where tonight's pickets are, and it won't tell anyone the full Fleet field track.

**KERR:** Would it let a raider improve their timing?

**TECHNICIAN:** A raider can already read Jex's public updates. They might learn how little faith to put in an old sensor. The same is true of a freighter captain. The information has no conscience.

**KEATING:** A cheerful way to describe what you're proposing.

**TECHNICIAN:** I repair instruments, Admiral. The people who buy them supply the cheer.

*Venn studies the tolerance band. The technician has marked a discrepancy in an instrument that was tested under load, not while a ship was in transit.*

**VENN:** How long to validate this for civilian release?

**TECHNICIAN:** Long enough that the first crossing will be over. Probably the second too, if nobody asks me to rewrite every sentence for a committee.

**VENN:** Then don't pretend we're helping tonight's pilots. We're helping the next convoy determine whether the numbers they receive deserve to be believed.

**KEATING:** You are suggesting that Fleet admit its instruments have limits.

**VENN:** They've always had limits. We've been unusually fortunate that civilians haven't had the authority to read them.

**KERR:** That is not entirely fair.

**VENN:** No. Neither is a certificate without a test method.

*Keating turns to the technician.*

**KEATING:** Draft the stripped technical method and the uncertainty limits. Release it after validation. No vessel positions, no tactical observations, no names of informants or private crossings. Route the draft through the ordinary safety review, not Venn's correspondence file.

**TECHNICIAN:** Understood.

**VENN:** Thank you.

**KEATING:** I haven't given you a favor. I've authorized a defensible public standard.

**VENN:** I think I'm beginning to understand why those are different.

**KEATING:** Good. Then perhaps we won't have to repeat this conversation when you find something else you want.

*On the way out, Kerr pauses beside Venn.*

**KERR:** You could have asked her to release the whole recording.

**VENN:** And spent another month arguing about why she couldn't. I used to have a ship to fill that month with work. I need to be more selective now.

**KERR:** That sounds dangerously like patience.

**VENN:** Don't tell anyone. They'll think I'm ill.

*The after-action technical release proceeds through a valid review. It cannot alter tonight's two crossings, which still depend on Halev's decision, Naomi's flying, Tess's ship, Deka's shields, and the independent Shard captains.*

### SCENE 3 — THE NAMES DISAPPEAR (P04)

*THUNDERBREAK — COMMAND. Deka is in the captain's station. Her crew has been tracking real hulls, not trusting icons to substitute for sensor returns. Beyond the cruiser, Shard pickets are stationary across both Gate approaches.*

**TACTICAL:** Shard transponders are going dark. The hulls haven't moved.

**DEKA:** How many?

**TACTICAL:** All the picket identifiers on the first screen. Second screen is following.

**DEKA:** Keep ours identified. Civilian pilots need to know where we are.

**TACTICAL:** Fleet control will call it a hostile concealment maneuver.

**DEKA:** It may be. It hasn't moved a ship one meter. Use the measured positions. Don't allow anyone to invent a fleet in an empty gap because a transponder stopped saying good morning.

*An engineering officer approaches, irritated by the shield schedule.*

**ENGINEERING:** Our port bus can cover the overlap, but I'd prefer to have it back before the second window. It's running warmer than I like.

**DEKA:** What do you like?

**ENGINEERING:** A functioning port bus.

**DEKA:** A person of limited imagination. So am I.

**TACTICAL:** Gravefire wants yesterday's overlap.

**DEKA:** Hold it open for the skiffs. Their captain keeps the lead.

**TACTICAL:** She's still classified hostile in our registry.

**DEKA:** Then the registry can learn something from the viewports. She's carrying medical skiffs. The classification doesn't entitle us to drive the patients into a blockade.

*She studies the two openings. The first could be reached now. The second is barely more than a promise on the clocks.*

**DEKA:** Ask engineering what the port bus can sustain for thirty minutes. Tell me the number they believe, not the number they'd give the Admiralty.

**ENGINEERING:** I heard that.

**DEKA:** Good. Saves a second message.

### SCENE 4 — THE POSTING (P05–P07)

*SHARDFALL — ARENA VAULT. Abby and Tess stand before Trade and Raid. A courier has brought the blocked medical manifest. The medicine is needed on Auric and in Shard settlements, including the place where one of Gravefire's mechanics lives.*

**TRADE:** Without recognized inspection, the medical convoy remains at the Gate.

**TESS:** The pharmacists have inspected the cases. I've seen the seal numbers.

**TRADE:** We require the household lists. Recipient names. The shipborne custody sequence.

**TESS:** You need to know whether the medicine is what the label says. You're asking to know where every person who needs it sleeps.

**TRADE:** Only those with something to conceal fear inspection.

**TESS:** That's a wonderful system. The hungry produce a list of their children and the frightened tell you their addresses. Anyone who objects confesses to being guilty. Very efficient.

**RAID:** Gravefire will enforce the medical blockade.

**TESS:** No.

*Abby turns slightly. Tess keeps looking at the masks.*

**RAID:** You have a Gate-line posting, Captain. You may not choose which parts of the line you hold.

**TESS:** I can choose what I do with my ship.

**RAID:** Then surrender the posting.

**TESS:** That's done. Gravefire leaves with me.

**RAID:** The Confederacy made your command possible.

**TESS:** My crew made it possible. The Confederacy helped us find a berth when the old yard broke apart. I haven't forgotten that. But you aren't taking the ship because I won't point her at medicine bound for our own families.

*Trade speaks more gently than Raid.*

**TRADE:** You are making a long-term question into a personal grievance.

**TESS:** One of the patients on that manifest patched my suit after Black Drift. She said if I ruined the cuff again she'd charge me double. Apparently I have a personal grievance with having her die before she can collect.

**RAID:** This hearing has consequences beyond your affection for one patient.

**TESS:** Yes. I brought an entire ship to the hearing. I'm aware.

*Raid has the attendant register the loss of Gate-line authority. Abby doesn't intervene. She knows doing so would imply Tess needs a superior captain to exercise a choice that is hers.*

*OUTSIDE THE VAULT. The air smells of hot metal. Tess stops at the threshold and appears to be counting her fingers.*

**ABBY:** Have you lost something?

**TESS:** My posting. I just spent a remarkably unpleasant ten minutes losing it.

**ABBY:** I meant why are you looking at your hands?

**TESS:** They were shaking. I'm checking if they've stopped.

*Abby catches Tess's wrist. She has known those hands since they were children.*

**ABBY:** You should have warned me.

**TESS:** What were you going to do, put your hand up and ask for a family recess?

**ABBY:** I could have prepared the captains.

**TESS:** We have thirty minutes between windows. If you can get seven of them to turn their guns aside, I'll get the skiffs through. Don't make them yours to order. Ask them.

**ABBY:** I know the difference.

**TESS:** You know it when you're calm. You get very commanding when someone frightens you.

*Abby lets go of her wrist.*

**ABBY:** I was frightened in there.

**TESS:** So was I. There's something we can agree on.

*They watch a maintenance crew hurry past with an empty trolley, heading to prepare Gravefire's departure.*

**ABBY:** They'll take your berth next.

**TESS:** Probably. The crew's going to be furious. They were finally getting used to having a place where every other day didn't involve bargaining for cooling water.

**ABBY:** They could find another captain.

**TESS:** They could. I wouldn't blame them.

**ABBY:** You would be unbearable about it for a month.

**TESS:** Two months. At least.

*Abby laughs despite herself, then stops.*

**ABBY:** Seven captains is not a vote I can win by standing in front of them and being right.

**TESS:** Good. You're learning. For years you've been so good at getting people to follow you that you forget sometimes they want to stay exactly where they are.

**ABBY:** They're risking the housing their crews worked for.

**TESS:** Yes. And they'll remember you asked them. Not that you were particularly persuasive.

*Abby straightens the repaired cuff of Tess's flight suit. Tess lets her. Then they go toward different ships.*

### SCENE 5 — THE FIRST OPENING (P08–P10)

*VEIL MARKET — HANGAR. Sunlash is ready. Two working freighters hold grain for Auric. Their captains have been watching the Gate measurements and one another for so long that irritation has replaced anxiety.*

**FREIGHTER CAPTAIN:** Are you the one who said this opening was safe?

**NAOMI:** No. I'm the one who said we could use it if you listened.

**FREIGHTER CAPTAIN:** That's not how it was repeated to me.

**NAOMI:** I'll take that up with my publicity department. It's a very small department.

**SECOND:** She is her publicity department.

**NAOMI:** And as you can see, I do an appalling job.

*Naomi goes through the approach once more: Sunlash leads, food freighters staggered, no sudden burns near the anchor marker. The physics are measurable and unforgiving. These aren't doors opened by magic; they're volumes whose reciprocal-field tolerances are known for short periods.*

**FREIGHTER CAPTAIN:** Can I follow you exactly?

**NAOMI:** Follow the published fixes and your own instruments. If you put your ship where I was thirty seconds ago, you'll be late to a very different piece of space.

**SECOND:** First window settling.

**NAOMI:** Then let's stop talking about it.

*They depart. Sunlash takes the exposed line. One freighter drifts too close to an anchor marker and clips it at low relative velocity. The mast buckles. No hull breach, no instant explosion; the ship remains under control but will need real repairs.*

**SECOND:** Freighter Two struck the marker. Mast damage. Their pilot wants to turn back.

**NAOMI:** Tell them not to. The geometry they're in will carry them out if they keep their present burn. A rescue turn puts them across the other freighter's bow.

**FREIGHTER CAPTAIN:** I can't keep my mast upright.

**NAOMI:** You don't need to. You need to keep your ship upright.

**FREIGHTER CAPTAIN:** Is that supposed to be comforting?

**NAOMI:** It's supposed to be an instruction.

*The grain ships reach Auric. Workers ready a receiving team and then have to revise their arrangement because the damaged mast blocks the normal access side.*

**HALEV:** Can it unload?

**DOCK SUPERVISOR:** Yes. Slowly. The mast's in the way, and I won't put anyone underneath it until it's braced.

**HALEV:** Then start on the undamaged ship. Tell the queue exactly which grain is available tonight.

**DOCK SUPERVISOR:** They'll complain we promised more.

**HALEV:** We did promise more. They should complain. That doesn't mean we tell them the rest has arrived before it can be unloaded.

*Somebody calls the second window from another station. Naomi looks toward the Gate again. The first crossing has ended well enough to create work, which is the best version of success she's prepared to trust.*

### SCENE 6 — SEVEN SEPARATE ANSWERS (P11–P12)

*SHARDFALL — SERVICE COMMUNICATIONS BAY. Trade learns its loyalist replacements cannot arrive before the second window. Abby has a communications station and a narrow interval to contact seven captains. She is not issuing Confederacy orders, no matter how much easier that would be.*

**EMISSARY:** The loyalist relief force is still thirty minutes out. We cannot reposition before the medical window.

**TRADE:** Then instruct the existing captains to maintain their obstruction. The Gate cannot be left to whoever arrives with medicine and a persuasive story.

*Across the service bay, Abby prepares separate messages. Her officer stands ready to send a collective command.*

**OFFICER:** I can route it under your authority.

**ABBY:** That's the one thing you mustn't do. These ships don't belong to me.

**OFFICER:** They know you. Some of them would obey.

**ABBY:** Yes. And after tonight we'd be explaining why a favor became an order. Send requests. One to each captain.

*FIRST CALL.*

**CAPTAIN ONE:** Raid will freeze my berth.

**ABBY:** He may.

**CAPTAIN ONE:** That means my crew's families are out of heated quarters.

**ABBY:** I can offer repair time on Scythe's outer rail, while we have it. I cannot give you housing. I'm not going to pretend the two are the same.

**CAPTAIN ONE:** That's the worst offer I've had all week.

**ABBY:** Have you decided?

**CAPTAIN ONE:** Don't rush me. I'm trying to think of one worse.

*SECOND CALL.*

**CAPTAIN TWO:** You want us to rotate weapons off the medical lane. What happens if a loyalist fires from behind us?

**ABBY:** You keep the right to defend your crew. I'm asking you not to shoot at civilians or close the route. You decide when the line is no longer defensible.

**CAPTAIN TWO:** So we risk being branded traitors and you still won't promise protection.

**ABBY:** I can promise you accurate positions. I can promise I won't claim you joined my command. I cannot promise nobody will shoot at you.

*THIRD CALL. A captain refuses immediately.*

**CAPTAIN THREE:** No. I lost one berth following promises about where the supplies would go. My crew's not paying for your conscience.

**ABBY:** Understood.

*She closes the call. Her officer stares.*

**OFFICER:** You're not going to try him again?

**ABBY:** No. He said no. We're allowed to hear that.

*Another captain calls back; her crew has insisted on hearing the request rather than having their captain decide alone. Abby waits while unfamiliar voices argue in the background.*

**CAPTAIN FOUR:** We will rotate off the medical lane. We are not joining a new fleet. And I want that said while people are listening.

**ABBY:** I will say it.

**CAPTAIN FOUR:** Good. Because everyone seems to think whoever has the best timing chart gets to own the ships using it.

*By the window there are seven independent commitments. The refusals remain recorded. The captains begin shifting their weapon arcs at different times, each responsible for their own hull. Abby watches them without making a speech.*

### SCENE 7 — ASHFANG IN THE WAY (P13–P15)

*AURIC GATE — SECOND WINDOW. Gravefire leads medical freight and civilian skiffs. Its port radiator remains damaged; its cooling margin is limited and visible in crew work. Tess requests the overlap from Thunderbreak. Deka has the port bus measurements in front of her.*

**TESS:** Thunderbreak, Gravefire. Medical holds and skiffs in my wake. Request the overlap.

**DEKA:** Extending sixty klicks on your side. The Gate lane is yours.

**TESS:** I'll keep the skiffs inside it.

**DEKA:** You'll have to, Captain. My engineering officer is threatening to take my chair away if I warm the port bus another degree.

**TESS:** I'd listen to the engineer. They usually know where the fire starts.

*The seven captains rotate their guns away, not in a perfect coordinated display but in separate, occasionally awkward movements. A loyalist ship tries to recover the field. Ashfang Marauder, fuel-starved since its refusal at Tal Corvus, moves deliberately into the firing line. It is not joining Abby's flotilla; Billie has chosen this position herself.*

**ASHFANG OFFICER:** Our reserves won't support a fight.

**BILLIE:** Then don't start one.

**LOYALIST CAPTAIN:** Ashfang, clear the line.

**BILLIE:** The skiffs are behind me. You want to fire at them, you're going to have to go through this very expensive piece of machinery first.

**ASHFANG OFFICER:** We aren't particularly expensive anymore.

**BILLIE:** It cost me enough. I am not having an argument about market value while somebody is aiming weapons at our tail.

*The loyalist does not fire. Behind Ashfang, Gravefire shepherds the skiffs toward the actual measured field opening. One pilot starts to veer, frightened by the gun arcs.*

**TESS:** Medical Two, I see the turn. Stop it. You're not in trouble for being frightened. You will be in trouble if you put your patients in the shear because you want to get away from someone else's guns.

**PILOT:** I can't see the marker.

**TESS:** Then use my hull as your reference. Stay on the inside of my wake. Don't invent a new route because you don't like the view.

*At Auric, two convoys arrive, thirty minutes apart. The food freighter's damaged mast is still wrapped in a brace; unloading crews have not yet finished. On Thunderbreak, a draft public report arrives.*

**TACTICAL:** Both crossings clear. The food freighter needs a hull team. No weapons fire.

**DEKA:** Identify the seven Shard ships that moved.

**TACTICAL:** The public Fleet report says our escort broke the line.

**DEKA:** Our shields held. Their captains opened it.

**TACTICAL:** That's going to make an awkward correction.

**DEKA:** Good. The original is wrong. Awkwardness seems appropriate.

*She studies the track Ashfang held, the medics now at the receiving berth, and the damaged mast the civilian crews are trying to brace. No one has won the Gate. People have gotten through it.*

### NEW SCENE V6-B — SEVEN PEOPLE WHO DIDN'T ASK HER (AFTER SOURCE P15; THE CROSSING IS ALREADY COMPLETE)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — CIVILIAN CONFERENCE LINK. The two Gate windows have passed. All seven independent Shard captains who moved their ships did so without an order from Venn and without a promise from Fleet. Abby Saville appears on a secured civilian link that the Verge has agreed to relay. She has declined an official Fleet conference. Venn sits at the review table with no insignia in the camera frame except the one on her uniform. Abby is in a cramped working space with an off-duty crew member changing a filter behind her.*

**ABBY:** You said you had a question about the report. It'd better be a good one. Two of my captains are trying to get their docking rights back, and I promised I'd call them before the next meal.

**VENN:** I wanted to know whether the seven would consent to their individual decisions being documented by the review, under their own names or anonymously as they choose.

**ABBY:** Fleet's already written it up. I saw a copy. Apparently your shields defeated the blockade.

**VENN:** Deka challenged that account. She wrote that the captains opened the line.

**ABBY:** Good. It shouldn't have taken a lieutenant saying the obvious.

**VENN:** I agree.

**ABBY:** Do you? People on your side keep finding ways to make our choice sound like a useful consequence of your planning. We were hungry before we were helpful, Commodore. Some of those captains still believe the Triarch had the right to inspect the freight. They moved because the medical ships needed to get through. That doesn't make them members of your alliance.

**VENN:** I wasn't asking for membership.

**ABBY:** You were asking for names. They have families on Tal Corvus. A signature can take away a berth faster than a gunship can.

*Venn looks down at the review form. The first line asks for “witness identity.” She has never thought much about how quickly a form can become a weapon.*

**VENN:** Then I shouldn't have led with the names. Can the record distinguish each captain's action without identifying them until they've consented?

**ABBY:** Yes. If you don't turn their refusal to give a name into proof that they weren't there.

**VENN:** I can request that wording. Keating will have to approve it.

**ABBY:** You can't do it yourself?

**VENN:** I'm under a disciplinary finding. I can ask for preservation and testimony. I cannot order the Admiralty to accept either.

*Abby sits back, unexpectedly interested.*

**ABBY:** What's that like?

**VENN:** Frustrating.

**ABBY:** Good. You'll be familiar with most of my working life.

*Venn laughs before she can stop herself. Abby doesn't quite smile.*

**VENN:** Have I asked you to do something you can't safely do?

**ABBY:** Not yet. You've asked me to make people available to an institution that has spent years calling them raiders. Some of them were raiders. Some still are. They're not all suddenly innocent because they kept a medical skiff alive.

**VENN:** Nor should the record make them innocent. It should make what happened accurate.

**ABBY:** Accuracy's a start. What happens to the captain who says, yes, I moved the blockade, and then the Triarch freezes her berth? Does Fleet protect her?

**VENN:** I cannot promise that.

**ABBY:** Could you before?

**VENN:** I would have thought I could. I wasn't always right.

**ABBY:** You're learning slowly.

**VENN:** Consistently, I'm told.

*Behind Abby, the crew member has failed to seat the filter and curses quietly. Abby turns away from Venn for a moment, helps align the frame, and returns to the screen.*

**ABBY:** I'll ask the seven what they want documented. Don't call them directly. Don't tell them it would help their standing. I don't know if it will.

**VENN:** Agreed.

**ABBY:** And don't use this conversation to tell the Admiralty I'm cooperating with you.

**VENN:** What should I tell them?

**ABBY:** That you asked a question and didn't get everything you wanted. Perhaps that'll make your report sound real.

*The link ends. Venn opens the review form, deletes the assumption that Abby will provide witness names, and substitutes a consent process proposed by Abby. For once, a result of Venn's negotiation is the explicit preservation of someone else's right not to participate.*

### SCENE 8 — THE SECOND RETURN (P16)

*TAL CORVUS — SERVICE WARRENS. Tess, making a return inspection after the medical crossing, walks with a technician along a structural seam. The wet streak on the seam appears to repeat itself a fraction of a second after the first, although pressure and temperature measurements remain within ordinary tolerances. The instruments record both events without explaining them.*

**TECHNICIAN:** There wasn't anything here yesterday.

**TESS:** Yesterday you were spending most of your time hiding from the people who were supposed to be governing the place. Did you measure it then?

**TECHNICIAN:** No.

**TESS:** Then we know there wasn't a measurement. We don't know there wasn't a seep.

*He positions a sensor, repeats the observation. The second return is close enough to the first to trouble him.*

**TECHNICIAN:** It's the same temperature. Pressure's normal. No air loss. It doesn't look like a leak.

**TESS:** Then don't repair it until we've found out what repairing would do.

**TECHNICIAN:** I didn't touch it.

**TESS:** I saw your hand moving.

**TECHNICIAN:** That was my professional curiosity.
