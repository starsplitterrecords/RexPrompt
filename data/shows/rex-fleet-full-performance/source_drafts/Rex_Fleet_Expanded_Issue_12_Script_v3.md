# REX FLEET — EXPANDED DRAMATIC SCRIPT
## Source Issue 12 — Thirty Days

**Development draft v1. RF_I12_P01–P21. Ten atlas scenes. Accord is a provisional thirty-day operational trial, not permanent political union.**

### SCENE 1 — THE ROOM WITH THE LONG TABLE (P01–P03)

*BASTION SPIRE — RELIC COURT. The Gate is still under repair. Three political factions have agreed to discuss a shared route without agreeing that any one of them owns it. Dominion sits opposite Halev. Venn has a place at the Fleet side but no restored unlimited authority; Abby sits by choice, not as a subject captain.*

**DOMINION:** We can stabilize the Gate with a permanent Fleet schedule and verified patrol assignment.

**HALEV:** You can stabilize the passage. If the schedule requires Auric's permission to be revised every time someone on your side changes a command, that isn't the same as control over our traffic.

**DOMINION:** I didn't say you would lose control of your ports.

**HALEV:** You didn't say we would keep it either. That's why we're still talking.

*Jex enters with copies of the payments linking the market disturbances and Shatterforge vent sabotage. His evidence is careful and incomplete.*

**JEX:** I can connect the purchases. I can show the people paid to make the market angry, and the relays bought before the Shatterforge lock. I can't make an account number confess to being one particular person.

**DOMINION:** The ledgers are authenticated?

**JEX:** By more than me. Which was the point of releasing them. A very expensive point, I might add.

*Abby brings the replay of mixed escorts protecting the civilian lane.*

**ABBY:** You're calling it an emergency exception. How many times does something have to work before we stop calling it an exception?

**DOMINION:** It worked under exceptional conditions. Those captains aren't in the Fleet chain.

**ABBY:** Exactly. And they kept the lane open anyway. You don't have to command them to rely on what they agreed to do.

*Dominion considers that but refuses the premise that voluntary agreements cannot fail.*

**DOMINION:** And when one of your captains changes their mind during a battle?
*He doesn't wait for a response. The question has a specific incident behind it, one that he has carried through several hearings.*

**DOMINION:** I have a report on an escort officer who left formation to recover a civilian tug. Another ship had to expose itself to cover the gap. The tug was recovered. We were fortunate there was no second attack. If you had asked the families on the tug, they would have wanted that officer to turn. If you'd asked the civilians behind the screen, they might have wanted the ship to stay.

**HALEV:** We don't know what the families behind the screen would have said.

**DOMINION:** That's precisely my concern. The people making these decisions can't canvass the people they're going to endanger. They need a structure they trust when the time to think has run out.

**ABBY:** I trust the captains who stood with me. That doesn't mean I know what every one of them will do tomorrow.

**DOMINION:** Then on what basis can I put Fleet ships beside them?

*Abby considers the question. It deserves a better answer than indignation.*

**ABBY:** You ask what each captain can promise, and you put the promise where the next captain can inspect it. No one gets to discover in the middle of a crossing that a Shard captain agreed to defend a different lane from the one you were counting on. And if a captain refuses, we don't write them into the formation anyway.

**DOMINION:** That's a schedule, not a chain of command.

**ABBY:** Yes. That's why it might work between us.

*Selene makes a note to distinguish a binding mutual notification from an assumed order. Dominion does not concede the argument, but he lets the proposed language go forward for examination.*

**ABBY:** Then the other people on the line deserve to know it. That's why we need a procedure we can inspect, not a promise about how good everyone is.
*Halev has been listening to the argument about command as if it were a dispute over seating. He lays the damaged freight manifest on the table and turns it to show the unresolved shipments.*

**HALEV:** In the last three months, I have signed emergency orders to house people in places they didn't own, ration food people had already bought and ask captains to protect convoys they weren't obligated to escort. Every signature was supposed to solve an immediate problem. Each one left somebody owing somebody else something they hadn't agreed to.

**DOMINION:** Government cannot operate without emergency authority.

**HALEV:** I know. I used it. I'm asking what keeps the emergency from becoming the ordinary way we govern. These people will still have to buy fuel and heat their homes thirty days from now.

**JEX:** Some of them will still be trying to collect payment from me. Since we're naming the injured parties, I'd appreciate a little precision.

*Halev glances at him. He is almost grateful for the interruption.*

**HALEV:** If we can't explain to a dock worker who is responsible when something goes wrong, this agreement is too clever to survive its first accident.

*Dominion turns to Selene and asks for a version of the clause that specifies who can suspend a crossing, who must notify the other partners, and where a civilian can contest the result. The negotiation begins to concern an actual procedure instead of a contest between histories.*

### SCENE 2 — BILLIE'S OBJECTION (P04–P06)

*The draft Accord leaves a broad clause allowing Fleet to assume unified control during a corridor emergency. Billie Rusk arrives uninvited and finds the clause before anyone can usher her away.*

**BILLIE:** You've built a beautiful agreement. I particularly like the part where Fleet gets to decide when the emergency is serious enough to own everything.

**VENN:** That language was intended for immediate rescue coordination.

**BILLIE:** Then why does it say command? And whose rescue are we coordinating when the person giving the order can also declare what counts as danger?

**DOMINION:** You are not a party to these negotiations.

**BILLIE:** I noticed. My ship has spent months being told where it may travel by people who never asked me to sit at their table. I've come to offer the table a familiar experience.

*Abby watches her oldest friend challenge the clause without claiming the moral high ground.*

**ABBY:** Billie, what do you want it to say?

**BILLIE:** That a ship's captain can protect civilians without becoming somebody else's property for thirty days. That the route doesn't disappear because one faction is angry. That if Fleet wants a Shard ship to move, Fleet asks, and the captain has to answer for whatever they decide.

**DOMINION:** A chain of command cannot operate on requests.

**BILLIE:** Then don't call three different chains one chain. They aren't. They didn't become one because we had a frightening evening.
*Halev looks at the clause. He has spent months resisting a Fleet permanent presence, yet the evacuation proved he needs ships he does not control.*

**HALEV:** If one captain sees a civilian collision risk and has to wait for three authorities to agree, we're going to kill somebody with perfect courtesy.

**BILLIE:** Then don't make them wait. Give a captain the power to protect people in immediate danger. Make them tell everyone else what they did and why. Don't give the captain the power to claim every other ship afterward.

**DOMINION:** Emergency authority without a clear limit can be abused.

**BILLIE:** Yes. You may have noticed I have some experience with that.

*No one can tell whether she means her own orders or those she obeyed. Tess, who knows both histories, doesn't rescue her from the silence.*

**VENN:** What you're proposing puts a heavy responsibility on whoever recognizes the danger first.

**BILLIE:** It already does. You can pretend the rule will keep them safe from making a terrible choice, or you can make certain the choice is recorded and answerable afterward.

**SELENE:** That would require preserving decisions made by other factions even when the original authority disputed them.

**HALEV:** It would also require my council to admit that a Verge pilot can be wrong, and that Fleet might save us without being entitled to run us.

**JEX:** A clause requiring everyone to admit they may be wrong. I expect it to be the shortest agreement in human history.

*For the first time, Dominion laughs outright. It surprises everyone, including him. Then the room returns to the language, which has become harder to evade precisely because the joke was true.*

*The clause is revised into independently confirmed timing, agreed common civilian protection procedures, no unilateral custodial seizure, and separate command over each force. Billie leaves a Shard coin on the amended page rather than sign. No one pretends she has joined the Accord.*

**TESS:** You could stay for the signing.

**BILLIE:** I've done enough damage to contracts this year.
*She looks at the coin on the amended page. The room expects a signature, a handshake or a new allegiance. She gives them none of those things.*

**TESS:** You could stay long enough to hear what you've changed.

**BILLIE:** I'd rather hear about it from the pilots after they've flown under it. A signed piece of paper can be very agreeable to everyone who never has to maneuver a ship.

**ABBY:** Are you going to obstruct the trial?

*Billie turns toward her. They have said far worse things to each other during this season; both seem to remember them.*

**BILLIE:** No. But I'm not going to swear loyalty to an agreement I helped argue with. My crew hasn't consented to it. If they want to join an escort, they can tell me, and I'll decide whether Ashfang can safely cover them.

**ABBY:** That's all I was asking.

**BILLIE:** It wasn't. You were asking whether I can be trusted to stay out of the way. I don't know if you can trust me. I know I wouldn't have trusted myself six months ago.

*Abby wants to say something that would make the moment easier. Instead she lets Billie leave. Tess picks up the coin only long enough to move it clear of the signing instruments; she places it back where Billie left it, a record of an objection rather than membership.*

*For a second it sounds like her old humor. Tess doesn't laugh, but she keeps the coin where it is.*

### SCENE 3 — AGREEMENTS YOU CAN TEST (P07–P08)

*Selene reads back the operational terms; the officials hear their own compromises in the plain language of someone who will archive them.*

**SELENE:** Shared measured windows. Separate ship command. Mixed escorts by independent consent. No unilateral transfer of civilian witnesses. No permanent assumption of port authority. Thirty days, then review.

**DOMINION:** And a provision allowing any party to withdraw if the measurements cease to support safe transit.

**JIA:** That's essential. A political signature cannot make an unstable field stable.

**HALEV:** Nor can a safe field force a captain to be cooperative.

*Venn and Abby sign the bounded working terms, not a universal peace.*

**VENN:** I don't know whether our governments can live with this.

**ABBY:** Neither do I. We can find out without asking the civilians to wait for us to like each other.

*They turn toward practical preparations rather than declaring a new era.*

### SCENE 4 — THREE CLOCKS (P09–P13)

*Auric Gate at dusk. Three factions independently verify the same reciprocal-field timing. The first Accord convoy has real freight, a worried pilot, and a schedule that can still fail.*

**SECOND:** Verge timing agrees. Fleet return is within tolerance. Shard return arrived a fraction late.

**NAOMI:** Late enough to matter?

**SECOND:** Not yet. It is the first time three people have agreed on an error rather than which one of them caused it.

*On Thunderbreak, Venn reviews task-group objectives while Deka commands the maneuver.*

**VENN:** All three confirmations received. Deka, the lane is yours to cover.

**DEKA:** Understood. I'd like our shield to survive this agreement. Engineering, don't let anyone borrow its power for a demonstration.

*On Sunlash, a mixed-escort pilot congratulates a Shard vessel on joining civilized traffic.*

**NAOMI:** Don't be too friendly. Give them proper spacing or you'll both spend the next hour explaining how we managed to collide during a peace trial.

*On Gravefire, Tess hears a young Shard pilot blame Fleet's clock for his own mistake.*

**TESS:** Check your local reference before you start a war over somebody else's reading. Your clock is five seconds behind because you forgot to reset it after maintenance.

**PILOT:** I thought it was Fleet drift.

**TESS:** Of course you did. It's a very economical explanation. Unfortunately the clock is yours.

*The pilot corrects it. The convoy passes through in a working formation whose vessels remain politically and visually distinct.*

### SCENE 5 — THIRTY DAYS IS TIME ENOUGH (P14–P15)

*SHARDFALL CITADEL. The Triarch watches the first crossing. The three masks study different liabilities: Trade sees one Gate vulnerable to another bottleneck; Fear sees thirty days of time to undermine confidence; Raid sees the cost of spending force too soon.*

**TRADE MASK:** They are running a single certified route on borrowed cooperation. One delayed shipment will put the market back on its knees.

**FEAR MASK:** They have taught civilians to trust copied clocks. Copies can disagree. People can be shown the difference.

**RAID MASK:** Let them demonstrate the schedule until it becomes valuable. Then we'll know precisely what they cannot afford to lose.

*Billie remains outside the Accord. She hears the convoy has crossed without incident and stares at the Shard coin's missing place in her pocket. She cannot decide whether to be pleased or worried. She settles for checking Ashfang's fuel count.*

### SCENE 6 — THE ARCHIVE DOESN'T AGREE (P16)

*Relic Court analysis desk. Selene compares Fleet, Verge, and Shard records of the same crossing. The timestamps agree; the explanations don't.*

**SELENE:** One record calls it disciplined Fleet restraint. Another calls it Verge navigation. The third says independent Shard captains kept the guns off the lane.

**JIA:** All three describe actions that happened.

**SELENE:** Yes. None is a complete account of why they happened.

**JIA:** Then record the measurements separately from the motives. We can verify the clocks. We can ask people what they intended. We can't calibrate an intention.
*Selene closes the first version of the archive and looks at a blank comparative record. It bothers her that the blank space is not a failure of scholarship but a truthful description of the evidence.*

**SELENE:** When I was younger, I believed the work was to recover the right version of events. You find enough original records, you identify the corrections, and eventually the noise goes away.

**JIA:** Has it?

**SELENE:** No. It has accumulated more very well documented reasons to disagree.

**JIA:** That sounds like progress.

**SELENE:** It's an irritating kind of progress. People expect history to tell them what happened. They don't want to be told three people acted for different reasons and that those reasons are part of what happened.

*Jia studies the instrument logs.*

**JIA:** I can't explain the corridor's echoes. But now I can tell another scientist which measurements to repeat if they want to disagree with me. If the next result proves I was wrong, we still have learned something. Does your record allow that?

**SELENE:** It will have to. Otherwise the next government will decide which motive was proper and erase the others before anyone has time to object.

**JIA:** I like your profession better when it sounds like a laboratory.

**SELENE:** And I like yours better when it remembers who was aboard the ships.

*They return to the records. Neither has won the conversation, and both have made the other person's work slightly harder and more useful.*

*Selene begins a new comparative record, uncomfortable but accurate. The historical explanation remains unsettled.*

### SCENE 7 — WHAT JEX KEEPS (P17)

*Auric governor hall. Naomi finds Jex looking at his old private route token. He has rebuilt part of his trade business around publicly verifiable timing rather than exclusive access.*

**NAOMI:** I hear you're charging for people to make sense of free route data now.

**JEX:** Expertise remains stubbornly billable.

**NAOMI:** And the private refuge routes?

**JEX:** Still private when their disclosure would hurt the people using them. Transparency is not the same thing as leaving every door unlocked.

**NAOMI:** You haven't heard from Tali.

**JEX:** No. I keep expecting to. I don't know whether that means I'm hopeful or incapable of learning.

**NAOMI:** You can be both.
*Jex turns the token over. Its worn edge has been polished by a habit of his thumb, not by any special material. Naomi has been careful not to ask to see what it encodes. She knows it is the one place where his new enthusiasm for public verification has a boundary.*

**JEX:** I used to be the person who could find somebody. You gave me a name and a debt and I'd tell you which berth to knock on. I was very good at it.

**NAOMI:** You still are.

**JEX:** I haven't found my own sister.

**NAOMI:** Your routes can tell you where a ship went. They can't tell you what a person did after they stopped answering.

**JEX:** That's not much help.

**NAOMI:** I wasn't trying to help. I was trying not to lie to you.

*He almost smiles. They have been disagreeing long enough for the distinction to matter.*

**JEX:** Her last message was about moving families. Not about herself. I keep rereading it as if she left me an instruction I haven't understood.

**NAOMI:** She may have been busy doing exactly what she said she was doing.

**JEX:** Yes.

*He puts the token away and asks about tomorrow's cargo. Naomi gives him the actual inventory; they return to work without pretending the conversation has ended the wait.*

*She has come to ask him about tomorrow's freighters. She lets him put away the token before discussing business.*

### SCENE 8 — AT GRAVEFIRE'S BERTH (P18)

*A child waits outside Gravefire while a parent finishes paperwork. Tess, still recovering from her forearm wound and with her ship awaiting radiator repairs, recognizes the look of somebody worried about whether a door will open.*

**CHILD:** They said five minutes.

**TESS:** People who work in arrival offices are very optimistic about minutes.

**CHILD:** Do they always come back?

*Tess considers the question carefully.*

**TESS:** I can't promise you that about every person. I can tell you your parent is in that office, and the person at the desk knows you're waiting. Would it help if we sat where they can see us?

*The child nods. Tess shifts a crate to make space, using the uninjured arm. They wait together without a grand lesson about the Accord.*

### SCENE 9 — USEFUL (P19–P20)

*Next morning. The second convoy assembles with fewer arguments. On Thunderbreak, Naomi's link opens to Venn.*

**NAOMI:** You remember what I said before you left for the tribunal?

**VENN:** You told me to come back with something Auric could use.

**NAOMI:** Yes. I thought you might come back with a repair crew and a very long speech about it.

**VENN:** I've sent the crews. Would you prefer the speech now?

**NAOMI:** No. Please don't spoil it.
*Naomi leaves the channel open while a freight worker calls to ask whether the borrowed repair team knows how to service an old Verge coupling. Venn hears the question.*

**VENN:** We sent people who can repair it. Not people who can redesign it into a Fleet coupling.

**NAOMI:** That's more reassuring than you know.

**VENN:** I suspect I know a little more than I did before the tribunal.

*Naomi is quiet. She remembers how angry she was when Venn first left Auric and how easily it would have been to accept a neat apology in place of the repair teams.*

**NAOMI:** I can't promise I won't be furious with you the next time Fleet takes a useful thing away. Or that I won't blame you for some of it even when it's not your order.

**VENN:** I wouldn't ask you to promise that.

**NAOMI:** Good. I still don't like having to ask your officers whether I'm allowed to use equipment to fix my own station.

**VENN:** Then ask the people who own it. They should be told what the loan actually allows. I've learned that a favor with a hidden command in it isn't much of a favor.

*Naomi looks away from the screen to the cargo approaching the gate. For the first time the question is not whether Venn will be the officer responsible for the next crisis, but whether the procedure can survive them both getting angry again.*

*Venn smiles. They return to the transit figures, both a little less formal than they were at the beginning.*

### SCENE 10 — A ROAD WITH OTHER PEOPLE ON IT (P21)

*The second convoy moves through Auric Gate. Thunderbreak, Sunlash and Gravefire protect their different sectors, and unrelated civilian traffic goes about its business. The route works for the moment, without healing the damage, settling the factions' pasts, or resolving the mystery of the corridors. It belongs to the people who use it as much as to those who protect it.*

*On a freighter a worker checks a freight latch, and another brings a child to the window to watch a ship drift clear of the lane. There is no speech. The convoy proceeds.*
