### SCENE 1 — THE SHUTTER (P01)

*VEIL MARKET — LOWER CAVERN. A ration shutter jams halfway open. The people nearest it are pushed against the counter by the crowd behind them. Jex, attempting to get from one side to the other, loses his footing.*

**JEX:** There's a second distribution point at the east passage! There is no need to turn this one into a contest!

**WORKER:** The east passage closed last night!

**JEX:** I was not told that.*The worker gives him a look. He is young, with a bandaged thumb and a plain work vest that has been patched twice. Jex knows his face from shipping transactions but cannot find his name.*

**WORKER:** We closed it after the heater failed. The door started sticking when the pipes contracted. Everybody who's queued for rations knows.

**JEX:** I had a verified distribution notice from four hours ago.

**WORKER:** Then you had the last person who knew about it four hours ago.

*The shutter drops another finger's width. The nearest people begin pushing harder. A woman near the front raises a small child overhead so he can breathe. Jex tries to step between the counter and the crowd, slipping on spilled grain.*

**NAOMI:** Stop pushing! You're crushing the front row!

*Her voice carries because she sounds angry rather than official. She points to three adults near the edge.*

**NAOMI:** You three, take two steps back and make a lane. Don't look at me; look at your neighbors. Help them move.

*A man hesitates, then starts pushing the crowd outward instead of forward. Others follow. It takes several ugly minutes before there's space to pull Jex clear.*

**JEX:** I assume nobody is putting that on the morning incident report.

**WORKER:** I already told dispatch your name. They asked whether you had a permit to operate the door.

**JEX:** This is why I avoid public service.

*The worker's laugh surprises both of them. He returns to the shutter. Jex remembers that he still does not know the man's name, and asks.*

**WORKER:** You can call me the man who got his grain yesterday.

**JEX:** Fair enough. I ought to find out why you only got it yesterday.

**WORKER:** Nobody tells the people who charge for the directions when the door's been welded shut.

*Two workers pull Jex free. One holds him against the wall until the crowd moves back.*

**JEX:** Thank you. That was very nearly expensive.

**WORKER:** For you, maybe.

**NAOMI:** You all right?

**JEX:** I have discovered how unpopular an out-of-date schedule can make a man.

**NAOMI:** It would help if the schedules were less expensive.

*She reaches behind the shutter to help release the jammed mechanism. Jex does not try to take credit for it.*

### SCENE 2 — ONE HUNDRED (P02–P03)

*AURIC FREEPORT. Wallace is moving three columns of expected supplies around an inventory board. Halev wants to authorize distribution; Naomi has just returned from Veil Market.*

**WALLACE:** A hundred crates were promised. A hundred is what I've already committed to clinics and ration halls. I cannot distribute a hundred crates we do not have.

**HALEV:** Where's the ship?

**NAOMI:** Outside the closed window. Nobody's flying an unverified reciprocal-field opening because we're impatient.

**WALLACE:** People are getting impatient because they're hungry.

**NAOMI:** And they'll be hungrier if we put the freighter through shear and spread it across the Rift.

**HALEV:** When can we verify another opening?

**NAOMI:** Jex has the live timing changes. I have the last public bulletin. They're not the same.

**WALLACE:** Naturally.

**HALEV:** Ask him.

**NAOMI:** I already have. He asked what we intended to pay.

*Halev stops writing.*

**HALEV:** Tell him we'll pay the pilots who use the information. I'll discuss his invoice when the food is here.

### NEW SCENE V5-A — THE NAME THAT STILL OPENS DOORS (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P03; NO FLEET RESOURCE TRANSFER)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — COMMUNICATIONS BOOTH. Venn has been permitted routine civilian correspondence during her restriction. On the other side of an ordinary voice link sits a licensed surveyor who once maintained Fleet corridor instruments and now works privately. The surveyor, an older woman whose work jacket has paint on one cuff, has answered while repairing a thermal test stand. Her name is not entered into the public script record; her profession is.*

**SURVEYOR:** I recognize the voice. I was hoping the caller identification was wrong.

**VENN:** It's good to hear you too.

**SURVEYOR:** Last time I heard that voice, somebody had put a cruiser on my calibration schedule without asking me.

**VENN:** It was a small cruiser.

**SURVEYOR:** It blocked my loading bay for a week.

**VENN:** Five days.

**SURVEYOR:** Your people counted the days. My people counted the missed wages. We had different instruments.

*Venn smiles. The surveyor does not entirely mean to be unfriendly.*

**VENN:** I wanted to ask whether you could give Auric's maintenance office an independent assessment of their damaged sensor couplings. They'd contract you directly.

**SURVEYOR:** I heard about the Tal Corvus arrivals. What makes you think I'm qualified to repair a shelter heating system?

**VENN:** I don't. I'm asking about the sensors that tell them whether a new corridor fitting is safe. A freighter can arrive with food, but the port has to know which equipment can receive it.

**SURVEYOR:** So you need a survey, not a repair.

**VENN:** Yes.

**SURVEYOR:** Then why did you tell their office I could do both?

*Venn opens the draft she sent. She sees the phrasing. “Independent assessment and repair plan.” A phrase she'd thought safely general.*

**VENN:** I used the wrong words.

**SURVEYOR:** You used the words that would make the person hiring me think I'd solve both problems. That's not quite the same thing.

**VENN:** Fair criticism.

**SURVEYOR:** You've never said that to me before. Are you in trouble?

**VENN:** A certain amount.

**SURVEYOR:** Good. I was starting to think the whole institution was immune.

*The surveyor sets down the fitting she's been holding.*

**SURVEYOR:** I'll talk to Auric. But I won't take a commission if they can't pay the crew. I'm not rich enough to replace the shipping bond, and I'm not taking a Fleet promise written by somebody who can't authorize it.

**VENN:** That's fair.

**SURVEYOR:** And I won't inspect a military screen while a civilian calls it a port survey. If there are guns involved, you tell me now.

**VENN:** There are armed vessels in the region. I'm not asking you to evaluate their positions. If the job becomes one that needs military authorization, walk away.

**SURVEYOR:** I'll decide that myself.

**VENN:** You should.

*Silence. The surveyor looks at the call header.*

**SURVEYOR:** You're not asking me to do it because of Kaldr, are you? Because of the grain ship?

**VENN:** I would have asked for help whether or not we'd been there.

**SURVEYOR:** That's not an answer.

**VENN:** No. I'm asking because we were there. I thought getting the passengers through was the end of my responsibility. It wasn't. And the people who have to live with the aftermath don't particularly want to hear me explain that.

**SURVEYOR:** They've got a point.

**VENN:** Often.

**SURVEYOR:** All right. Send me their public specification, not your Admiralty version of it. I'll quote against what Auric actually needs. If they reject the price, I want you to stay out of it.

**VENN:** Agreed.

**SURVEYOR:** One thing. You remember telling the Chief at Kaldr that I'd always make room for Fleet?

**VENN:** I remember.

**SURVEYOR:** I didn't make room for Fleet. I made room for the wounded people you were carrying. There's a difference. It would be pleasant if you remembered it this time.

*The call ends. Venn removes the loose reference to “repair plan” from the request and sends a corrected public specification to Auric. The surveyor may accept a civilian contract; she has not promised a repair, lent a Fleet instrument, or been put under anybody's command.*

### SCENE 3 — WHAT A CLOCK COSTS (P04–P05)

*JEX'S PRIVATE VAULT. Three different live opening corrections are visible on a simple instrument record. Jex has kept the verified updates to sell. Naomi recognizes one shift she has already seen in the field.*

**NAOMI:** You're not selling a route. You're selling the difference between a safe crossing and a collision with the boundary.

**JEX:** I prefer to think I'm selling the work of people who measure that difference.

**NAOMI:** And the ship whose food is sitting on the wrong side of it?

**JEX:** They could have bought the update.

**NAOMI:** Nobody told them the old one was wrong.

*He opens his mouth, then closes it. There's no defense that doesn't sound like a price.*

**JEX:** If I open the current clock, Billie can read it. Pike can read it. Every fool with a gun and a receiver can read it.

**NAOMI:** Yes.

**JEX:** You've become an unhelpful conversationalist.*Naomi does not answer immediately. She is studying the three corrections and the intervals between them. A faint discrepancy has the look of an instrument carried outside its calibration range. She circles one observation.*

**NAOMI:** Who supplied this one?

**JEX:** An independent survey crew. Their ship is competent, their debt is modest, and I believe their captain is only lying to one spouse at the moment.

**NAOMI:** You trust it?

**JEX:** I trust it enough to put my name on the bulletin. That's a considerably more expensive form of trust than the one I ordinarily sell.

**NAOMI:** There'll be days when the correction is wrong. If the field changes faster than they report it, people will blame you.

**JEX:** People already blame me for things I haven't done. At least this time I'll have the satisfaction of knowing I did something.

*He opens the private subscriber list. Pilots who used to pay for early corrections now receive the same public update as crews who could never afford them. Jex's hand hovers over the confirmation.*

**NAOMI:** You can keep the transaction record. We aren't asking you to burn every useful thing you've built.

**JEX:** I don't think you understand what happens when a broker gives up being the only man who knows where the road goes.

**NAOMI:** He may have to start being useful in another way.

*Jex confirms. One subscriber immediately messages to complain that the secret he bought is no longer secret. Jex starts to type an answer, deletes it, and returns to the public data.*

**NAOMI:** You know what I think. I came to see what you think when you can't hide behind a price.

*Jex transfers the verified current updates to a public bulletin, including corrections as they arrive.*

**JEX:** Keep your pilots checking measurements. If the field changes, this paper will become a lie before I've finished publishing it.

**NAOMI:** Good. Put that on the notice too.

**JEX:** It's remarkable how much more you ask when I'm giving things away.

*The bulletin becomes available to the civilian freighter. Elsewhere, Ashfang receives the same timing.*

### SCENE 4 — ASHFANG HAS NO FUEL (P06–P10)

*ASHFANG MARAUDER. The Triarch has cut Billie's fuel deliveries because she refused to burn independent Shard fields. Her officer has been stretching the remaining stores and no longer bothers to pretend the figures are adequate.*

**OFFICER:** Enough to reach the next staging point. Not enough to fight there and come back.

**BILLIE:** Then we don't fight there.

**OFFICER:** A hundred crates on a Verge relief freighter. We've got their crossing time.

**BILLIE:** That's food for somebody.

**OFFICER:** So are the stores we don't have. You told Trade to keep their tankers.

*Billie stares at the timing bulletin Jex opened. She chooses an interdiction she knows Abby would reject.*

**BILLIE:** We take the supply rack. Quick and out. Keep the weapons off the cabins.

*In the Rift, Ashfang fires first. The aft rack tears loose. Twenty-eight of the hundred promised crates are destroyed, not captured. The freighter struggles to maintain course.*

**FREIGHTER PILOT:** We're losing the rack! Stop firing! This is food!

*Billie's officer watches a cloud of ruined cargo move away from the ship.*

**OFFICER:** We didn't get any of it.

**BILLIE:** I can see that.

**OFFICER:** The rest could still feed us if we catch the freighter.

*Gravefire enters on its already damaged port side. Tess places its heat-weakened radiator between Ashfang and the freighter.*

**TESS:** Billie, don't make me explain the people inside that hull to you.

**BILLIE:** Move. We need the remaining freight.

**TESS:** So do the people it belongs to.

**BILLIE:** You haven't missed a meal yet.

**TESS:** My crew has. I can still tell the difference between a meal and somebody else's supper.

*Gravefire's cooling warning climbs. Tess is not bluffing about what one more shot would do.*

**OFFICER:** Captain, her port section is barely holding together.

**BILLIE:** I know. She put it right where I'd have to hit it.

**OFFICER:** We can turn her.

**BILLIE:** You can stop suggesting I shoot Tess. Break off.*The officer obeys. Ashfang rolls away and the freighter moves toward its escort, still venting debris from the destroyed rack. For a while Billie listens to the ship rather than the crew. A bearing sounds wrong after the turn. She asks for engineering.*

**ENGINEERING:** Same starboard coupling. It's been running hot since Black Drift. We can't repair it with what we've got.

**BILLIE:** And the fuel?

**ENGINEERING:** Enough to get home if you don't change your mind about leaving.

*She looks at the retreating freighter. Tess has not opened another channel. The absence is worse than an argument.*

**OFFICER:** We could ask the captain to transfer a few medical cases. Gravefire would escort them. Tess might agree.

**BILLIE:** After we shot twenty-eight crates into space? Would you agree if you were her?

**OFFICER:** No.

**BILLIE:** Then don't make me ask her for something I haven't earned.

*The officer leaves to tell the galley the expected food hasn't arrived. Billie can hear the conversation from her station; the cook asks whether to cut the next meal again. She nearly calls the officer back to make the request anyway. Instead she opens the list of independent Shard growers the Triarch wanted destroyed. She has to decide whom she's willing to ask for help without pointing a gun first.*

*Ashfang withdraws with no supplies, a damaged reputation, and less fuel than before.*

### SCENE 5 — THE OTHER SIDE OF THE CLOCK (P11–P12)

*VEIL MARKET SERVICE CAVERN. Naomi follows a worker's chalk arrow toward a ration door that is actually open, ignoring an older official marker. A loader confronts Jex while workers tally the destroyed delivery.*

**LOADER:** Twenty-eight.

**JEX:** I heard.

**LOADER:** You heard someone say a number. You didn't open the broken crates to find out what was inside.

**JEX:** Was there anything salvageable?

**LOADER:** You could make soup out of the labels.

**JEX:** I'll send a recovery crew.

**LOADER:** With what? The clock you opened told every ship where we were. Ashfang read it as well as anybody.

**JEX:** So did the freighter. So did the escorts who came to help.

**LOADER:** You always have another side to it.

**JEX:** Usually three. That's how I make a living.

**LOADER:** I had a cart, Jex. They had guns. You're welcome to find the third side.

*Jex picks up a ruined strap, then leaves it where it is.*

**LOADER:** My sister gets her allotment every fourth crossing. She asks me for an extra bag of grain. I've spent a month telling her no. Now I get to explain why we lost twenty-eight bags' worth before anyone got to eat them.

**JEX:** I didn't tell Billie to attack.

**LOADER:** I know. If I thought you had, we wouldn't be talking.

**JEX:** Then why ask me to close the clock?

**LOADER:** I didn't ask you. Don't close it. My sister's still waiting. But if you've got information about who's coming to take her grain next time, I wouldn't mind hearing it before the shooting starts.

*Jex looks at the bulletin. He continues posting corrections. Tali's private token stays out of public view.*

### NEW SCENE V5-B — THE COST ON THE OTHER SIDE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P12; CIVILIAN CONTRACT DISCUSSION)

*AURIC FREEPORT — SMALL PROCUREMENT OFFICE / FLEET ANNEX — SUPERVISED LINK. Sera Dain has taken Naomi's place on a call with Venn. The independent surveyor's quotation lies between them. A civilian clerk from Auric sits beside Sera with a live inventory that contains an unpleasant number of empty entries. Outside the office, a ration cart stops and starts as workers reassign the remaining delivery.*

**SERA:** I can authorize the inspection. I cannot authorize the equipment the inspector hasn't identified yet. Your surveyor seems disappointed by that.

**VENN:** She's usually disappointed when people ask her to certify something they've already decided to buy.

**SERA:** Then she and I may get on very well. She wants two independent readings and a local escort. That's doable. She wants permission to keep the raw recordings for her insurance. That's not.

**VENN:** Why not?

**SERA:** Because the recordings show where we berth damaged ships and how long we leave the outer dock exposed. The equipment might be civilian; the information can still put people in danger.

*Venn starts to object and realizes she's about to use the same argument Naomi made earlier.*

**VENN:** She needs enough to document her calculation. Could she keep anonymized equipment readings and have Auric retain the positions?

**SERA:** That's exactly what my clerk suggested before you called.

**CLERK:** I suggested it more politely.

**SERA:** She did. I was in a less receptive mood then.

**VENN:** I'm relieved to have arrived during your receptive one.

**SERA:** Don't become accustomed to it. There's another problem. The survey team would need two hours of warmth and a clear working bay. Two hours we currently use to process emergency arrivals. I can move them, but the people in that bay have already moved twice.

**VENN:** I could ask Fleet to provide a temporary working platform.

**SERA:** Could you?

**VENN:** No.

**SERA:** I'm glad we've dispensed with that in under a sentence.

*The clerk runs the numbers. A small adjustment appears on her screen.*

**CLERK:** If the survey happens after the incoming relief freight clears, we only need one bay for one hour. It costs us a shift of inspection backlog, not sleeping space.

**SERA:** Assuming the freight clears. It was attacked this morning. Our promised hundred crates are now seventy-two.

**VENN:** I saw the preliminary report.

**SERA:** Did you see what the missing twenty-eight means? They were supposed to restock two clinics. A captain chose to shoot off an equipment rack, and the people who ordered her food deliveries stopped will still eat before we do.

**VENN:** I don't have an answer that makes it smaller.

**SERA:** Good. I'm becoming tired of people with excellent answers and no crates.

*Venn thinks for a moment. She chooses not to invoke a colleague.*

**VENN:** Then don't schedule the survey yet. If the cargo berth and your crews are free after the next run, you can bring her in. If not, defer it. She can prepare from the redacted specifications. Neither I nor the surveyor gets to decide whether that hour is worth more than your arrivals work.

**CLERK:** That means we can obtain a price for the test without committing the shift.

**SERA:** I see why Commodore Venn became popular with her engineers. She can still be persuaded to accept the obvious after several minutes.

**VENN:** I was considerably worse when I had a ship.

**SERA:** That's what Naomi tells me.

*The clerk smiles despite an exhausted face. Sera notices and softens.*

**SERA:** I'll authorize a conditional quotation, subject to local control of the evidence. If this works, it helps us certify equipment. If it fails, we will have spent an hour discovering an old sensor is old. Please don't have Fleet issue a celebration for either outcome.

**VENN:** I won't.

**SERA:** And Commodore? The surveyor is now dealing with my office. You don't need to remain in the middle of every message.

**VENN:** Understood.

*The line closes. For the first time Venn has helped arrange something without being the person who authorizes it. The conditional contract has a clear civilian owner and an equally clear right to refuse.*

### SCENE 6 — WHAT THE CAPTAINS CAN WITHHOLD (P13–P14)

*THUNDERBREAK — COMMAND. The cruiser has orders to offer a limited defensive screen for the next freight escort. Deka sits in the captain's station; the port armor and shield distribution are still being nursed through repairs. A tactical officer has Gravefire marked by hull return and by an old Shard hostile designation.*

**OFFICER:** Admiralty approved a defensive screen for this freight escort. No offensive release.

**DEKA:** That's more than I expected.

**OFFICER:** I'd celebrate, but the next line says we can't interfere with Shard command decisions.

**DEKA:** We weren't planning to.

**OFFICER:** Banks is holding that line with a damaged port radiator.

**DEKA:** I can see it. She's keeping her hot side away from the freighter and doing our job with half a cooling circuit.

**OFFICER:** Gravefire still shows Shard hostile.

**DEKA:** Keep the designation. Give her civilian overlap and cover her exposed side.

**OFFICER:** We could ask for confirmation through the Admiralty chain. If the screen is misinterpreted as an alliance, the report comes back to you.

**DEKA:** It probably will. We can wait for a better report or we can cover a real ship. How long until the freighter reaches the dangerous part?

**OFFICER:** Eleven minutes.

**DEKA:** Then we should probably spend ten of them doing something useful.

*The officer checks the shield bus and opens a channel to Gravefire. Deka does not call the Shard captain a subordinate or ask her to change her route.*

**DEKA:** Gravefire, Thunderbreak. We can hold overlap along your exposed port approach. Your crossing order stays yours.

**TESS (COMMS):** That's reassuring. I'm sure the next Admiralty letter will be equally friendly.

**DEKA:** I wouldn't make plans around it.

**TESS:** Keep the shield on the freight, then. I can take care of my ship.

**DEKA:** That's why I'm offering it.

*On Scythe of Saville, Abby has the report of Billie's attack in front of her. A crew officer has already prepared to remove Ashfang's access to their shared escort information. He hasn't executed the change.*

**OFFICER:** If you cut her off, Billie will say you've left Ashfang blind.

**ABBY:** She wasn't blind when she fired at the relief freighter. She knew exactly what was on it.

**OFFICER:** She also knew the crew's fuel allocation had been cut. Her people are hungry.

**ABBY:** So are the people who needed those crates.

*Abby walks to the observation pane. From here the Shard ships are still a working fleet of independent captains, not an army she can order home.*

**OFFICER:** You've known her longer than most of us have been alive. Are you sure you want this recorded as a sanction?

**ABBY:** I remember the first time she lent me an engine part without asking what she was getting back. I also remember the last time she did it. It wasn't all that long ago.

**OFFICER:** Then call her.

**ABBY:** If I call, she'll talk me into remembering everything except what happened to that freighter. I'm not ready for that conversation.

**OFFICER:** She'll say you chose Fleet over her.

**ABBY:** I chose not to give her the timing of the next civilian shipment. That should be a distinction she'd understand.

*She signs the restriction. The officer hesitates.*

**ABBY:** She can fly wherever she chooses. She still commands Ashfang. I am not taking her ship, or the people who trust her. I'm withholding something that depends on trusting her myself.

**OFFICER:** Will you tell her?

**ABBY:** Yes. Not through a notice she can show her crew and make a martyr out of. I'll tell her when she answers my call.

*The call is placed. It rings. Abby sits waiting, but Billie does not answer. The unanswered call stays in the log. That unanswered call is an injury of its own.*

### SCENE 7 — SEVENTY-TWO (P15–P17)

*Auric freight hall. The ship arrives with seventy-two crates. Sera insists the board keep the missing twenty-eight visible as a debt the station cannot fulfill.*

**SERA:** Don't write 'hundred received' because the manifest still says a hundred. We have seventy-two on the dock.

**WALLACE:** I know how to count crates.

**SERA:** I wasn't speaking to your arithmetic. I was speaking to the very persuasive way people edit their own expectations.

**HALEV:** How much do we cut each allotment?

**WALLACE:** The clinics get their original share. Everyone else gets less. The people who already had very little get less as well.

**HALEV:** Put the numbers where they can see them.

*A family at the distribution bay voluntarily breaks a small allotment to share with a resident whose household missed registration. The boy from Luminara passes a bag across the counter.*

**BOY:** We can come back tomorrow, can't we?

**RESIDENT:** They didn't say we'd have more tomorrow.

**BOY:** No. I suppose they didn't.*His mother reaches for the bag, then stops. The boy had offered their food without permission and knows it. He waits for the reprimand. It doesn't come.*

**MOTHER:** How much did you give away?

**BOY:** Not everything. I kept the part you said was for breakfast.

**MOTHER:** That's considerate of you. I'm not sure the breakfast you saved is enough for breakfast.

**BOY:** There are two of us. We can share.

*The resident comes back with a chipped cup of something warm.*

**RESIDENT:** It's broth. Not much of it. My neighbor had some vegetables and made a pot.

**MOTHER:** You needn't repay us.

**RESIDENT:** I wasn't repaying you. I was hoping you knew where to find another cup.

*The three talk about which of the heaters near the berth is still working. The boy's account of a malfunctioning one is unhelpfully precise, and the women cannot help laughing. Through the broad glazing the city still looks impossibly wealthy. No one at the counter calls it hypocrisy; they pass the broth around and plan another walk to the working heater.*

*Halev sees the exchange. He does not turn it into a speech; he goes to find the next freighter.*

### NEW SCENE V5-C — A PRIVATE CHAIN OF COMMAND (AFTER SOURCE P17; BEFORE THE SECOND RELIEF CONVOY)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — VISITING ROOM. Dominion has come without an audience or ceremonial escort. A security officer remains outside the door. He places a list of outgoing civilian correspondence on the table. Venn does not touch it.*

**DOMINION:** You've been busy.

**VENN:** I've been confined to two rooms for weeks. The alternative was to become very good at cards.

**DOMINION:** Kerr. An independent surveyor. Auric's elected officials. One retired civilian contractor who used to have Fleet work. Did you think no one would notice?

**VENN:** I expected you to notice. I wasn't certain you'd take the time to visit.

**DOMINION:** You're making private approaches to people responsible for a region in which Fleet vessels are currently engaged. Your tribunal finding remains in force.

**VENN:** I have issued no orders. Nobody has used Fleet authority on my request. The contractor can walk away, and Auric decides whether to employ her.

**DOMINION:** And when someone says yes because your name is Ella Venn rather than because the work is sound?

*That lands. Venn doesn't conceal it.*

**VENN:** Then I've built a chain of command I can pretend isn't one.

**DOMINION:** You understand why that concerns me.

**VENN:** I do.

**DOMINION:** Yet you continue.

**VENN:** Because Auric needs equipment and reliable measurements. The public route exists. The cargo losses exist. I can leave the institution's private arguments to you and still ask a licensed person whether she wants a job.

**DOMINION:** You once commanded a cruiser. Now you sound like a hiring agent.

**VENN:** I've had a few difficult conversations about the difference.

**DOMINION:** If what you wanted was a hearing about refugee supplies, you could submit one. Instead you've chosen to cultivate the people who will testify when Fleet asks for continued access.

**VENN:** Some of them already dislike me. I find that reassuring.

**DOMINION:** Don't confuse being disliked with being harmless.

*Dominion slides a second page toward her. It is a disciplinary accounting of *Thunderbreak*'s defensive support for *Gravefire*, recorded under Deka's command.*

**DOMINION:** Your successor covered a Shard ship classified as hostile. Under restricted defensive orders. We are reviewing the judgment.

**VENN:** I know what Deka did. I don't know why she judged it necessary. You should ask her.

**DOMINION:** You haven't spoken to her?

**VENN:** No. I won't make my reputation an extra officer on her bridge.

**DOMINION:** Your restraint is new.

**VENN:** My circumstances are new. I'd like to think I'm learning from them.

**DOMINION:** I won't promise to forgive her for making a defensible choice. Or condemn her for one. Her tasking has limits, whether or not you agree with them.

**VENN:** Then evaluate the limits. Not me.

*Dominion closes the file. The room holds an uncomfortable truce.*

**DOMINION:** I'm placing your external technical correspondence under declared review. You may send public specifications and legal requests. Not operational positions, no tasking, no informal requests to Fleet personnel. Kerr will receive the record. If you cross that line, you'll lose even this room.

**VENN:** You're giving me a boundary instead of silence.

**DOMINION:** I'm giving myself a reason to stop this before people begin taking orders from an officer who has been removed from command.

**VENN:** Those are the same boundary, Admiral. We disagree about what it's for.

*Dominion stands.*

**DOMINION:** I always thought taking *Thunderbreak* from you would mean you'd have fewer opportunities to cause trouble.

**VENN:** So did I.

*He goes. Venn spends the rest of the evening preparing a written list of her civilian contacts and exactly what each has been asked to do. Several entries contain only “declined.” She leaves them in. It's the first record of her influence she has ever made that openly records when influence failed.*

### SCENE 8 — THE NEXT SHIP (P18–P20)

*The next relief convoy approaches. Naomi leads with Sunlash, Tess brings Gravefire despite the limitation of its damaged cooling system, and a Fleet escort holds to the agreed defensive role.*
