# REX FLEET — VENN WITHOUT THUNDERBREAK

## Full dramatic performances, Issues 4–6 (v7)

**Nine complete new scenes, presented in story order.** The scenes are integrated into the complete Issue 4 v3, Issue 5 v4 and Issue 6 v4 development scripts. Unapproved writing proposals; they neither remove the tribunal finding nor take command from Deka.


### NEW SCENE V4-A — THE WRONG KIND OF SIGNATURE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P05; VENN HAS NO COMMAND)

*FLEET DISCIPLINARY TRANSIT — REVIEW CABIN. The cabin has been made comfortable in the efficient way that makes it impossible to forget whose cabin it is. A desk, a narrow sleeping berth, a working comms panel and a door that opens only when the escort officer authorizes it. The ship hasn't left Fleet jurisdiction. Commodore Ella Venn is reading a civilian freight-repair estimate on an ordinary portable screen. She has circled an item and written the word “why” beside it in pencil on paper.*

*Admiral Soren Kerr appears on a supervised connection. He doesn't bother with a greeting.*

**KERR:** Tell me that's not the report from Auric I think it is.

**VENN:** That depends which report you think it is.

**KERR:** The one you asked an Admiralty records clerk to retrieve under your retired access privileges.

**VENN:** I haven't retired.

**KERR:** Precisely. You have no retired access privileges. You also have no active command privileges. You're managing to occupy an impressively small legal space.

**VENN:** It was a publicly tendered estimate for thermal couplings. The clerk sent me the wrong copy. I told him so.

**KERR:** After circling the part number.

**VENN:** He'd already sent it. Was I supposed to close my eyes?

*Kerr considers her. The old irritation between them is familiar enough not to require anger.*

**KERR:** What are you trying to do, Ella?

**VENN:** Auric needs people who can verify an improvised corridor coupling without turning the job into a Fleet installation. I'd like the name of the technician who did the independent surveys before Kaldr reopened.

**KERR:** You know her name.

**VENN:** I know her *former* name. She retired eight years ago. She may not want to speak to me. I'm hoping you know whether she still does that work.

**KERR:** You want me to introduce a suspended officer to somebody who may then be seen arranging independent equipment for Auric while a Fleet screen holds Tal Corvus.

**VENN:** When you put it like that, it sounds needlessly suspicious.

**KERR:** How would you put it?

**VENN:** A woman looking for a mechanic.

**KERR:** A woman who used to move heavy cruisers by saying a sentence and now needs a mechanic rather badly.

*Venn is amused despite herself. Kerr doesn't let the amusement settle.*

**KERR:** I cannot obtain contacts through the Admiralty directory for you. I won't pretend the request came from me. And I certainly won't make it look as if you're still assigning work on behalf of Fleet.

**VENN:** I didn't ask you to pretend.

**KERR:** You didn't have to. That was once a useful thing about serving with you.

*Venn puts the pencil down.*

**VENN:** You're right. I'm sorry. I thought if I gave you the smallest possible request, it wouldn't feel like a request at all.

**KERR:** It's quite a request when you know the person on the other end can lose a career for helping.

**VENN:** Have you lost yours?

**KERR:** Not yet. You're making it a strenuous afternoon.

*From the cabin corridor comes the modest clatter of a meal trolley. Venn looks at the untouched tray waiting at the hatch.*

**VENN:** There's a public registry of civilian survey licenses. I can use that without you.

**KERR:** You can. Whoever answers can refuse you without a Fleet admiral standing behind your shoulder.

**VENN:** And if nobody answers?

**KERR:** Then you discover what other people have to put up with when they aren't you.

*He is about to close the connection, but stops.*

**KERR:** I hear Deka has the third-window screen.

**VENN:** She does.

**KERR:** You could write her a note.

**VENN:** About what?

**KERR:** That's what I wanted to find out.

**VENN:** She knows how to fly that ship. I won't send her a suggestion disguised as encouragement.

**KERR:** Good. That would have been the second wrong kind of signature today.

*He disconnects. Venn turns to the public directory. She composes a message without rank or Fleet insignia. It takes her longer than any order she's issued in years. The recipient is a licensed civilian surveyor, not an Admiralty officer. She leaves the first sentence unwritten for a full minute before beginning.*

### NEW SCENE V4-B — AN OFFER WITH A HANDLE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P10; BEFORE THE THIRD WINDOW OPENS)

*The supervised civilian link is delayed by the same traffic emergency everyone is trying to navigate. Venn waits while Auric's connection drops twice. When Captain Naomi Sol finally appears, she is in a freight operations passage carrying two handheld instruments. An arriving dockhand asks her for a docking number. She gives him one without looking away from Venn.*

**NAOMI:** I have eight minutes before we bring Sunlash into position. If this is a speech about shared purposes, I can send you the one I made last month. Nobody listened to that either.

**VENN:** I can make it shorter. I found a civilian calibration service familiar with old reciprocal-field couplings. They're willing to quote for your damaged arrivals equipment.

**NAOMI:** Quote to whom?

**VENN:** Auric's maintenance office.

**NAOMI:** Good. I was afraid you were going to say Fleet.

**VENN:** They want access to the original sensor measurements. Not the whole operational picture. Just the physical readings.

**NAOMI:** Will their people see our port registrations? Our cargo contracts? Where we put the refugees afterward?

**VENN:** I asked for measurements. They asked for the full bundle. I haven't answered.

**NAOMI:** Then answer no.

**VENN:** It's more complicated. They need to know which instruments are lying to each other.

**NAOMI:** They can have sensor types, tolerances, and timestamps. They don't need the names of the families sleeping beside them.

**VENN:** I could have asked that.

**NAOMI:** You could have. You know what you sound like, Commodore? Somebody who's never had to persuade a maintenance crew to let her take a look without giving her the keys to the entire building.

**VENN:** For a long time the maintenance crews worked for me.

**NAOMI:** Exactly.

*Naomi shifts the instruments under her arm and steps out of the way of an arriving trolley of blankets. She doesn't ask Venn whether the room on the other side of the screen is a detention cabin. Venn realizes that she has noticed.*

**VENN:** They aren't my people now. If this service comes, Auric will employ them, supervise them, and decide what information to provide. No Fleet installation. No authority over your traffic.

**NAOMI:** Why did you go looking for them?

**VENN:** Because you told me to come back with something useful.

**NAOMI:** I didn't think you'd take that as an assignment.

**VENN:** Old habits.

**NAOMI:** You asked about *useful*. Useful would be three working heat exchangers, seventy new berths, and a freighter that stops arriving missing pieces. What you've found is a person who may be able to tell us why our old equipment fails. That's a beginning, not a gift.

**VENN:** I know the difference.

**NAOMI:** Do you? The last time we spoke, you described every limitation Fleet had. You were very accurate. I spent the next week trying to heat people with your accuracy.

*Venn receives the remark without trying to improve its presentation.*

**VENN:** I can send Auric the contact and the limitations. You can decide whether to call.

**NAOMI:** Send it to Sera. She'll find the person who actually owns the equipment. If they're allowed to say no, I'll believe this is different.

**VENN:** I won't contact Deka about the window.

*Naomi looks up sharply.*

**NAOMI:** Why would you?

**VENN:** I thought you might wonder.

**NAOMI:** Erin doesn't need you to tell her where the shields go. She needs somebody to make sure she won't be punished for keeping them there. Can you do *that*?

**VENN:** No. Not from here.

**NAOMI:** All right. That's a real answer.

*There is a call from Sunlash. Naomi turns toward it.*

**NAOMI:** I've got a departure. Send the contact. If you're tempted to attach a letter explaining your intentions, don't. Just send the number.

**VENN:** Captain Sol?

**NAOMI:** What?

**VENN:** Good crossing.

**NAOMI:** I'll try to make it boring.

*Naomi breaks the connection. Venn sends a single civilian contact and the narrow specifications. The message contains no request for favorable publicity, no demand for acknowledgement, and no Fleet tasking. The civilian contractor has made no commitment and no equipment has moved. Outside the review cabin, the third-window operation proceeds without her.*

### NEW SCENE V4-C — THE PART OF THE RECORD SHE DOESN'T OWN (AFTER SOURCE P31; BEFORE THE LATER DEKA INQUIRY)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — RECORDS CONFERENCE ROOM. The Tal Corvus skiffs have reached Auric. Their supplies haven't miraculously multiplied. The older incident report on Venn's own rescue remains in the Fleet case files. Admiral Janet Keating has brought that file in person. Kerr is present as an observer. Venn has been granted an interview, not a command consultation.*

**KEATING:** You requested preservation of the escort's live recording, the civilian tug's pressure log, and three non-Fleet observations from the departure mouth.

**VENN:** Yes.

**KEATING:** You'll understand why that looked like an attempt to build Lieutenant Deka a defense before anyone had even questioned her.

**VENN:** It would be a defense if I knew what happened. I don't. We have a Fleet summary with a large blank where the formation changed.

**KEATING:** The blank is because part of the tactical track remains restricted.

**VENN:** Then preserve it under restriction. I'm not asking you to send it to Auric. I'm asking you not to decide what the evidence means before we can put the different witnesses in the same room.

**KEATING:** You want to put a Shard pilot in a Fleet disciplinary proceeding.

**VENN:** I want the pilot who was there to be available to the independent record. Whether the Fleet accepts testimony is your decision, and one you'll have to defend.

**KEATING:** You've become very fond of saying which decisions are mine.

**VENN:** I haven't got many left.

*Kerr almost smiles. Keating hears him thinking about it.*

**KEATING:** Your own tribunal file is still open on appeal. The record shows you chose civilian rescue over a supply convoy, and then refused the segregation order. You've objected to how those decisions were characterized.

**VENN:** I've objected to the suggestion that the supplies weren't real, or that the people weren't. Both mattered.

**KEATING:** Then you'll have no objection to attaching the complete account of the supplies Auric lost. Not only your bridge log, not only the survivors who thanked you. The invoices, the failed heat allotments, the medical shortages.

*Venn is silent for the first time. She glances toward Kerr. He gives her no assistance.*

**VENN:** That record will be read as an argument against me.

**KEATING:** It is an argument against part of what you did. I thought you preferred complete records.

**VENN:** I do. I don't particularly enjoy them.

**KEATING:** Neither do I.

*Keating turns the file around. Venn can see the early estimates, still imperfect, and the names of Auric workers who have had to sign for depleted stock.*

**VENN:** Include them. And the return readings from the passenger hull. Not as competing tallies. As the two consequences of one order.

**KEATING:** If the civilian witnesses consent, they can submit their own records. They don't become Fleet informants by doing so. I'll authorize the preservation notice. I won't promise access or a favorable finding.

**VENN:** I didn't come for a finding.

**KEATING:** You're very difficult to believe when you say that.

**KERR:** She has been consistent about being difficult.

**KEATING:** Consistency is not the same as good judgment, Admiral.

**KERR:** I'm beginning to notice.

*Keating collects the folder. Before she leaves, she stops at the door.*

**KEATING:** One more thing. You will not contact Deka or her junior escort to shape their testimony. If they want counsel, they will have counsel. Not a former commander with a long memory for what her officers owe her.

**VENN:** Agreed.

**KEATING:** You said that quickly.

**VENN:** Because I was tempted to do exactly that.

*Keating studies her, startled by the admission. Then she leaves.*

**KERR:** I thought you might ask me to call Erin.

**VENN:** She deserves a room where nobody is waiting for her to agree with me.

**KERR:** You know Dominion won't thank you for expanding the record.

**VENN:** I wasn't hoping for thanks. I was hoping he would have to read it.

*The signed preservation order goes forward. The later inquiry will take its own course. Venn has influenced what can be examined, but neither Deka's decision nor Deka's testimony belongs to her.*

### NEW SCENE V5-A — THE NAME THAT STILL OPENS DOORS (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P03; NO FLEET RESOURCE TRANSFER)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — COMMUNICATIONS BOOTH. Venn has been permitted routine civilian correspondence during her restriction. On the other side of an ordinary voice link sits a licensed surveyor who once maintained Fleet corridor instruments and now works privately. The surveyor, an older woman whose work jacket has paint on one cuff, has answered while repairing a thermal test stand. Her name is not entered into the public script record; her profession is.*

**SURVEYOR:** I recognize the voice. I was hoping the caller identification was wrong.

**VENN:** It's good to hear you too.

**SURVEYOR:** Last time I heard that voice, somebody had put a cruiser on my calibration schedule without asking me.

**VENN:** It was a small cruiser.

**SURVEYOR:** It blocked my loading bay for a week.

**VENN:** Five days.

**SURVEYOR:** Your people counted the days. My people counted the missed wages. We had different instruments.

*Venn smiles. The surveyor does not entirely mean to be unfriendly.*

**VENN:** I wanted to ask whether you could give Auric's maintenance office an independent assessment of their damaged sensor couplings. They'd contract you directly.

**SURVEYOR:** I heard about the Tal Corvus arrivals. What makes you think I'm qualified to repair a shelter heating system?

**VENN:** I don't. I'm asking about the sensors that tell them whether a new corridor fitting is safe. A freighter can arrive with food, but the port has to know which equipment can receive it.

**SURVEYOR:** So you need a survey, not a repair.

**VENN:** Yes.

**SURVEYOR:** Then why did you tell their office I could do both?

*Venn opens the draft she sent. She sees the phrasing. “Independent assessment and repair plan.” A phrase she'd thought safely general.*

**VENN:** I used the wrong words.

**SURVEYOR:** You used the words that would make the person hiring me think I'd solve both problems. That's not quite the same thing.

**VENN:** Fair criticism.

**SURVEYOR:** You've never said that to me before. Are you in trouble?

**VENN:** A certain amount.

**SURVEYOR:** Good. I was starting to think the whole institution was immune.

*The surveyor sets down the fitting she's been holding.*

**SURVEYOR:** I'll talk to Auric. But I won't take a commission if they can't pay the crew. I'm not rich enough to replace the shipping bond, and I'm not taking a Fleet promise written by somebody who can't authorize it.

**VENN:** That's fair.

**SURVEYOR:** And I won't inspect a military screen while a civilian calls it a port survey. If there are guns involved, you tell me now.

**VENN:** There are armed vessels in the region. I'm not asking you to evaluate their positions. If the job becomes one that needs military authorization, walk away.

**SURVEYOR:** I'll decide that myself.

**VENN:** You should.

*Silence. The surveyor looks at the call header.*

**SURVEYOR:** You're not asking me to do it because of Kaldr, are you? Because of the grain ship?

**VENN:** I would have asked for help whether or not we'd been there.

**SURVEYOR:** That's not an answer.

**VENN:** No. I'm asking because we were there. I thought getting the passengers through was the end of my responsibility. It wasn't. And the people who have to live with the aftermath don't particularly want to hear me explain that.

**SURVEYOR:** They've got a point.

**VENN:** Often.

**SURVEYOR:** All right. Send me their public specification, not your Admiralty version of it. I'll quote against what Auric actually needs. If they reject the price, I want you to stay out of it.

**VENN:** Agreed.

**SURVEYOR:** One thing. You remember telling the Chief at Kaldr that I'd always make room for Fleet?

**VENN:** I remember.

**SURVEYOR:** I didn't make room for Fleet. I made room for the wounded people you were carrying. There's a difference. It would be pleasant if you remembered it this time.

*The call ends. Venn removes the loose reference to “repair plan” from the request and sends a corrected public specification to Auric. The surveyor may accept a civilian contract; she has not promised a repair, lent a Fleet instrument, or been put under anybody's command.*

### NEW SCENE V5-B — THE COST ON THE OTHER SIDE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P12; CIVILIAN CONTRACT DISCUSSION)

*AURIC FREEPORT — SMALL PROCUREMENT OFFICE / FLEET ANNEX — SUPERVISED LINK. Sera Dain has taken Naomi's place on a call with Venn. The independent surveyor's quotation lies between them. A civilian clerk from Auric sits beside Sera with a live inventory that contains an unpleasant number of empty entries. Outside the office, a ration cart stops and starts as workers reassign the remaining delivery.*

**SERA:** I can authorize the inspection. I cannot authorize the equipment the inspector hasn't identified yet. Your surveyor seems disappointed by that.

**VENN:** She's usually disappointed when people ask her to certify something they've already decided to buy.

**SERA:** Then she and I may get on very well. She wants two independent readings and a local escort. That's doable. She wants permission to keep the raw recordings for her insurance. That's not.

**VENN:** Why not?

**SERA:** Because the recordings show where we berth damaged ships and how long we leave the outer dock exposed. The equipment might be civilian; the information can still put people in danger.

*Venn starts to object and realizes she's about to use the same argument Naomi made earlier.*

**VENN:** She needs enough to document her calculation. Could she keep anonymized equipment readings and have Auric retain the positions?

**SERA:** That's exactly what my clerk suggested before you called.

**CLERK:** I suggested it more politely.

**SERA:** She did. I was in a less receptive mood then.

**VENN:** I'm relieved to have arrived during your receptive one.

**SERA:** Don't become accustomed to it. There's another problem. The survey team would need two hours of warmth and a clear working bay. Two hours we currently use to process emergency arrivals. I can move them, but the people in that bay have already moved twice.

**VENN:** I could ask Fleet to provide a temporary working platform.

**SERA:** Could you?

**VENN:** No.

**SERA:** I'm glad we've dispensed with that in under a sentence.

*The clerk runs the numbers. A small adjustment appears on her screen.*

**CLERK:** If the survey happens after the incoming relief freight clears, we only need one bay for one hour. It costs us a shift of inspection backlog, not sleeping space.

**SERA:** Assuming the freight clears. It was attacked this morning. Our promised hundred crates are now seventy-two.

**VENN:** I saw the preliminary report.

**SERA:** Did you see what the missing twenty-eight means? They were supposed to restock two clinics. A captain chose to shoot off an equipment rack, and the people who ordered her food deliveries stopped will still eat before we do.

**VENN:** I don't have an answer that makes it smaller.

**SERA:** Good. I'm becoming tired of people with excellent answers and no crates.

*Venn thinks for a moment. She chooses not to invoke a colleague.*

**VENN:** Then don't schedule the survey yet. If the cargo berth and your crews are free after the next run, you can bring her in. If not, defer it. She can prepare from the redacted specifications. Neither I nor the surveyor gets to decide whether that hour is worth more than your arrivals work.

**CLERK:** That means we can obtain a price for the test without committing the shift.

**SERA:** I see why Commodore Venn became popular with her engineers. She can still be persuaded to accept the obvious after several minutes.

**VENN:** I was considerably worse when I had a ship.

**SERA:** That's what Naomi tells me.

*The clerk smiles despite an exhausted face. Sera notices and softens.*

**SERA:** I'll authorize a conditional quotation, subject to local control of the evidence. If this works, it helps us certify equipment. If it fails, we will have spent an hour discovering an old sensor is old. Please don't have Fleet issue a celebration for either outcome.

**VENN:** I won't.

**SERA:** And Commodore? The surveyor is now dealing with my office. You don't need to remain in the middle of every message.

**VENN:** Understood.

*The line closes. For the first time Venn has helped arrange something without being the person who authorizes it. The conditional contract has a clear civilian owner and an equally clear right to refuse.*

### NEW SCENE V5-C — A PRIVATE CHAIN OF COMMAND (AFTER SOURCE P17; BEFORE THE SECOND RELIEF CONVOY)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — VISITING ROOM. Dominion has come without an audience or ceremonial escort. A security officer remains outside the door. He places a list of outgoing civilian correspondence on the table. Venn does not touch it.*

**DOMINION:** You've been busy.

**VENN:** I've been confined to two rooms for weeks. The alternative was to become very good at cards.

**DOMINION:** Kerr. An independent surveyor. Auric's elected officials. One retired civilian contractor who used to have Fleet work. Did you think no one would notice?

**VENN:** I expected you to notice. I wasn't certain you'd take the time to visit.

**DOMINION:** You're making private approaches to people responsible for a region in which Fleet vessels are currently engaged. Your tribunal finding remains in force.

**VENN:** I have issued no orders. Nobody has used Fleet authority on my request. The contractor can walk away, and Auric decides whether to employ her.

**DOMINION:** And when someone says yes because your name is Ella Venn rather than because the work is sound?

*That lands. Venn doesn't conceal it.*

**VENN:** Then I've built a chain of command I can pretend isn't one.

**DOMINION:** You understand why that concerns me.

**VENN:** I do.

**DOMINION:** Yet you continue.

**VENN:** Because Auric needs equipment and reliable measurements. The public route exists. The cargo losses exist. I can leave the institution's private arguments to you and still ask a licensed person whether she wants a job.

**DOMINION:** You once commanded a cruiser. Now you sound like a hiring agent.

**VENN:** I've had a few difficult conversations about the difference.

**DOMINION:** If what you wanted was a hearing about refugee supplies, you could submit one. Instead you've chosen to cultivate the people who will testify when Fleet asks for continued access.

**VENN:** Some of them already dislike me. I find that reassuring.

**DOMINION:** Don't confuse being disliked with being harmless.

*Dominion slides a second page toward her. It is a disciplinary accounting of *Thunderbreak*'s defensive support for *Gravefire*, recorded under Deka's command.*

**DOMINION:** Your successor covered a Shard ship classified as hostile. Under restricted defensive orders. We are reviewing the judgment.

**VENN:** I know what Deka did. I don't know why she judged it necessary. You should ask her.

**DOMINION:** You haven't spoken to her?

**VENN:** No. I won't make my reputation an extra officer on her bridge.

**DOMINION:** Your restraint is new.

**VENN:** My circumstances are new. I'd like to think I'm learning from them.

**DOMINION:** I won't promise to forgive her for making a defensible choice. Or condemn her for one. Her tasking has limits, whether or not you agree with them.

**VENN:** Then evaluate the limits. Not me.

*Dominion closes the file. The room holds an uncomfortable truce.*

**DOMINION:** I'm placing your external technical correspondence under declared review. You may send public specifications and legal requests. Not operational positions, no tasking, no informal requests to Fleet personnel. Kerr will receive the record. If you cross that line, you'll lose even this room.

**VENN:** You're giving me a boundary instead of silence.

**DOMINION:** I'm giving myself a reason to stop this before people begin taking orders from an officer who has been removed from command.

**VENN:** Those are the same boundary, Admiral. We disagree about what it's for.

*Dominion stands.*

**DOMINION:** I always thought taking *Thunderbreak* from you would mean you'd have fewer opportunities to cause trouble.

**VENN:** So did I.

*He goes. Venn spends the rest of the evening preparing a written list of her civilian contacts and exactly what each has been asked to do. Several entries contain only “declined.” She leaves them in. It's the first record of her influence she has ever made that openly records when influence failed.*

### NEW SCENE V6-A — THE PART YOU CAN MEASURE (INTERCUT AFTER SOURCE P03; NO CHANGE TO THE TWO-WINDOW PLAN)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — DOCUMENT ROOM. Two civil Gate windows have already been publicly announced by Jex and Auric. The Shard pickets and each captain's choices remain entirely outside Venn's control. Keating has agreed to let Venn attend a review of what Fleet's verification instruments are permitted to disclose to a civilian crossing authority. Kerr is there because he was willing to sign his name to the request. A technician has brought the relevant technical records, not a tactical display.*

**KEATING:** Before we begin, nobody in this room is reopening the order for the Gate screen. Deka has the assigned vessel. The civilian authorities have the windows they intend to use.

**VENN:** I wasn't going to challenge the crossing plan.

**KEATING:** You challenged two orders in one afternoon. My confidence in your intentions is not quite as robust as it once was.

**KERR:** We might save time by treating every sentence here as a proposal, not an order.

**KEATING:** That is precisely what I was going to suggest, Admiral. Without the air of discovery.

*The technician opens a recorded test. Two successive field readings disagree within a tolerable range; the verified transit boundary remains unchanged.*

**TECHNICIAN:** What the civilians asked for is the instrument uncertainty and the test method. What the division asked me to release is a single certification mark. If I give them only the mark, they cannot tell when their older equipment has drifted.

**KEATING:** If you release the raw Fleet recordings, you may reveal where our survey vessels stand and what their instruments can observe.

**VENN:** Then remove the ship positions. Publish the tolerance band and the method used to arrive at it. Auric can compare against its own independent sensors.

**TECHNICIAN:** We can do that. It won't tell them where tonight's pickets are, and it won't tell anyone the full Fleet field track.

**KERR:** Would it let a raider improve their timing?

**TECHNICIAN:** A raider can already read Jex's public updates. They might learn how little faith to put in an old sensor. The same is true of a freighter captain. The information has no conscience.

**KEATING:** A cheerful way to describe what you're proposing.

**TECHNICIAN:** I repair instruments, Admiral. The people who buy them supply the cheer.

*Venn studies the tolerance band. The technician has marked a discrepancy in an instrument that was tested under load, not while a ship was in transit.*

**VENN:** How long to validate this for civilian release?

**TECHNICIAN:** Long enough that the first crossing will be over. Probably the second too, if nobody asks me to rewrite every sentence for a committee.

**VENN:** Then don't pretend we're helping tonight's pilots. We're helping the next convoy determine whether the numbers they receive deserve to be believed.

**KEATING:** You are suggesting that Fleet admit its instruments have limits.

**VENN:** They've always had limits. We've been unusually fortunate that civilians haven't had the authority to read them.

**KERR:** That is not entirely fair.

**VENN:** No. Neither is a certificate without a test method.

*Keating turns to the technician.*

**KEATING:** Draft the stripped technical method and the uncertainty limits. Release it after validation. No vessel positions, no tactical observations, no names of informants or private crossings. Route the draft through the ordinary safety review, not Venn's correspondence file.

**TECHNICIAN:** Understood.

**VENN:** Thank you.

**KEATING:** I haven't given you a favor. I've authorized a defensible public standard.

**VENN:** I think I'm beginning to understand why those are different.

**KEATING:** Good. Then perhaps we won't have to repeat this conversation when you find something else you want.

*On the way out, Kerr pauses beside Venn.*

**KERR:** You could have asked her to release the whole recording.

**VENN:** And spent another month arguing about why she couldn't. I used to have a ship to fill that month with work. I need to be more selective now.

**KERR:** That sounds dangerously like patience.

**VENN:** Don't tell anyone. They'll think I'm ill.

*The after-action technical release proceeds through a valid review. It cannot alter tonight's two crossings, which still depend on Halev's decision, Naomi's flying, Tess's ship, Deka's shields, and the independent Shard captains.*

### NEW SCENE V6-B — SEVEN PEOPLE WHO DIDN'T ASK HER (AFTER SOURCE P15; THE CROSSING IS ALREADY COMPLETE)

*FLEET REVIEW ANNEX — CIVILIAN CONFERENCE LINK. The two Gate windows have passed. All seven independent Shard captains who moved their ships did so without an order from Venn and without a promise from Fleet. Abby Saville appears on a secured civilian link that the Verge has agreed to relay. She has declined an official Fleet conference. Venn sits at the review table with no insignia in the camera frame except the one on her uniform. Abby is in a cramped working space with an off-duty crew member changing a filter behind her.*

**ABBY:** You said you had a question about the report. It'd better be a good one. Two of my captains are trying to get their docking rights back, and I promised I'd call them before the next meal.

**VENN:** I wanted to know whether the seven would consent to their individual decisions being documented by the review, under their own names or anonymously as they choose.

**ABBY:** Fleet's already written it up. I saw a copy. Apparently your shields defeated the blockade.

**VENN:** Deka challenged that account. She wrote that the captains opened the line.

**ABBY:** Good. It shouldn't have taken a lieutenant saying the obvious.

**VENN:** I agree.

**ABBY:** Do you? People on your side keep finding ways to make our choice sound like a useful consequence of your planning. We were hungry before we were helpful, Commodore. Some of those captains still believe the Triarch had the right to inspect the freight. They moved because the medical ships needed to get through. That doesn't make them members of your alliance.

**VENN:** I wasn't asking for membership.

**ABBY:** You were asking for names. They have families on Tal Corvus. A signature can take away a berth faster than a gunship can.

*Venn looks down at the review form. The first line asks for “witness identity.” She has never thought much about how quickly a form can become a weapon.*
