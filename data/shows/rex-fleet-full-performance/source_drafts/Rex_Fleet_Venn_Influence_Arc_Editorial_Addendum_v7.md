# Rex Fleet — Venn without *Thunderbreak*: Issues 4–6 v7 development addendum

**Status:** Proposed character-performance scenes. Not canon, not approved for comic production, not integrated into RexPrompt page recipes. Original source pages remain intact.

## Editorial premise

Venn's disciplinary removal takes away her ship and the assumption that her judgment is a standing order. It does not remove her experience, reputation, social obligations, access to approved correspondence, or ability to make introductions. Her early attempts at influence are flawed: she asks an old colleague for a favor that would endanger that colleague; proposes a technical engagement with too-broad access; and assumes a civilian need can be resolved by finding a capable person. The arc makes her learn to distinguish a useful introduction from disguised command. Her methods become more effective as she yields control to those who can legitimately refuse. That is a change in behavior, not a secret strategy that explains everyone else's victories.

## Dramatic progression

1. **Loss of command does not equal loss of identity.** Venn's first instinct is to solve a problem by finding a name in a directory. Kerr's refusal exposes the real burden of being asked by a famous officer to do something unofficial.
2. **Influence produces a bill someone else must pay.** Naomi and Sera challenge Venn about access, staffing, data ownership, heat, and the difference between finding an expert and repairing a broken system.
3. **Her name can itself be coercive.** Dominion identifies the threat of a parallel chain of command without treating her as a villain. Venn concedes the danger and accepts documented limits.
4. **She learns to bargain for procedures instead of obedience.** Keating gets a stronger evidence record, not a political victory for Venn; a sensor-method review produces a bounded public standard, not tactical data; Abby's captains retain consent.
5. **Her return is a limited accommodation, not vindication.** The institution needs an officer who can convene mixed observations, but does not return *Thunderbreak* or erase the tribunal. Deka earns and keeps command.

## Non-negotiable continuity conditions

- Venn does **not** direct Deka, the junior escort, the Tal Corvus rescue, the Issue 5 defensive overlap, the Issue 6 Gate crossing or any of the seven Shard captains while under the disciplinary restriction.
- Deka is *Thunderbreak*'s commanding officer throughout Issues 4–6 and retains direct command of crew, navigation, weapons and engineering when Venn arrives in Issue 7.
- Venn holds Commodore rank but is suspended from ship command. Her permitted correspondence, records requests and supervised interviews are distinct from operational authority. The exact legal conditions require final RexPrompt reconciliation.
- Naomi, Halev, Sera, Abby, Tess and civilian specialists make their own decisions. No one owes Venn a yes, and a yes does not create Fleet ownership or jurisdiction.
- The surveyor's work is a proposed, civilian-funded, contingent inspection, **not** a completed repair or the source of the already successful evacuation. It cannot erase *Gravefire*'s damaged port radiator, food losses, shelter shortages or previous hull damage.
- Source Issue 6 already makes Jex's timing data public. The new Fleet measurement-method review is narrower, de-identified, and prospective; it does not replace or enable that release.
- The seven captains make independent decisions before Venn contacts Abby. Deka's report correction remains hers.
- The later Deka inquiry is chronologically **after** the Tal Corvus rescue concludes. The final page insertion and issue length remain unsettled.
- Venn's temporary Issue 7 appointment is for a defined corridor Fleet task-group coordination function. It is not reinstatement to *Thunderbreak*, a command of civilians or Shards, or rescission of the tribunal finding.

## Editorial questions still open

- Is Venn's authorized civilian correspondence and location in the Fleet review annex consistent with the tribunal/detention mechanics in the assembled page scripts? The dramatic draft proposes supervised contact; confirm before canonization.
- Would a retired civilian surveyor realistically have the claimed historical relationship with Venn, and do the public sensor data and equipment exist in established faction/technology inventories? The contact is intentionally unnamed; accept or replace the detail through canon review.
- Are the records-preservation process and consent protections enforceable under the actual Fleet/Verge rules? Treat them as negotiated proposals, not established new universal law.
- Where should the later Deka inquiry sit in the expanded comic sequence so it does not interrupt the third-window rescue?
- Is the proposed scope of Venn's Issue 7 appointment operationally coherent with the live *Thunderbreak* chain of command? Preserve Deka's last word on ship handling.

**Creative performance test:** These scenes should be read as dramatic encounters, not used as a list of plot facts to compress back into dialogue summaries. Kerr, Naomi, Sera, Keating, Abby and Dominion each have grounded reasons to resist Venn. The stories move because she learns from the resistance, not because she eventually persuades everybody.
