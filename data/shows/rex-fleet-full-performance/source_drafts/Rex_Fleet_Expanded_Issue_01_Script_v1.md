# REX FLEET — EXPANDED DRAMATIC SCRIPT
## Source Issue 01: The Green Corridor

**Development draft v1 · 12 dramatically continuous scenes · No fixed comic-page count · Not canon or production-approved.**

This script is a creative expansion of the live Issue 1 numbered-page recipes, not a transcription and not an assembled final RexPrompt unit. All additions to background behavior and dialogue are proposals. Faction causality, cargo accounting, route physics, ownership and character knowledge follow the existing pages. The labels `RF_I01_P##` show which source pages a scene covers. Page and panel breakdown comes *after* performance review.

### SCENE 1 — THE BAG (RF_I01_P01)

*LUMINARA FIELDS — AGRICULTURAL FREIGHT DOCK — MORNING*

*The fields do not end so much as surrender to an arrangement of silos, loading arms, rail platforms, and an outbound passenger gate. Everything is maintained, but almost nothing is new. A long Verge grain freighter receives a harvest that its growers still own. Behind a guarded aisle, passengers are boarding the same vessel.*

*A dock worker points out two stacks of bins to a younger loader.*

**DOCK WORKER:** Those stay here. The sealed ones go to Auric.

**LOADER:** They're both sealed.

**DOCK WORKER:** Then look at where they're standing.

*The worker gives an exasperated little wave toward the reserve stacks. It is not the first time they've had this conversation.*

*A mother arrives with a boy, two bags, and a compact moving bundle that is just slightly too heavy for comfort. The boy walks backward, trying to look at the machinery without losing sight of her.*

**MOTHER:** Forward. You have spent your entire life seeing this from the road.

**BOY:** Never from this side.

**MOTHER:** You'll see it through the window in a minute. Mind the bag.

*He bumps into a luggage scale. Nothing breaks. The dock worker reaches out automatically to steady the scale and then the child.*

**DOCK WORKER:** Leaving us, are you?

**MOTHER:** We thought we'd let another planet put up with him for a while.

**BOY:** I'm not difficult.

**MOTHER:** He remembers everything anybody says. That's the difficult part.

**DOCK WORKER:** Auric, then? They say the rooms are warmer there.

**MOTHER:** That's one of the things they say.

**BOY:** And there's work. She checked three times.

**MOTHER:** I checked four. Keep hold of your ticket.

*The boy checks a pocket he has already checked repeatedly. The ticket is there. The mother has one hand on the bulky bundle and the other on her own bag, and can't easily check his pocket for him. She does anyway.*

**BOY:** Are we ever coming back?

*The mother looks toward the fields. Machines are moving in straight, dependable lines through grain taller than the boy.*

**MOTHER:** We'll have to find out whether we like where we're going first.

**BOY:** Can we come back if we don't?

**MOTHER:** Yes.

*She has answered too quickly, and he hears it.*

**BOY:** Even with the crossings?

**MOTHER:** The escorts know the passage. It's shorter now.

**BOY:** The Shards know that too.

**MOTHER:** They know plenty. It doesn't mean we're staying here forever because of them.

*The dock worker looks up from the loads. There is nothing useful to say to that.*

**MOTHER:** Your bag.

**BOY:** I've got it.

**MOTHER:** Good. Let's find out whether Auric looks as rich from the inside.

*At the passenger viewport, the boy watches the freight loaders uncouple. The separation between reserved food and export freight is evident in the stacked holds. So is the fact that people and grain are traveling together because there's only so much ship to go around.*

*The mother settles the moving bundle onto a seat. The boy presses close to the glazing. Beyond the fields, the escort vessels hold their places, one local and one very much Fleet.*

**BOY:** That's Thunderbreak?

**MOTHER:** So they tell me.

**BOY:** It's enormous.

**MOTHER:** Good.

*The word comes out more fervently than she intended. She sits beside him and fastens the strap across their luggage.*

### SCENE 2 — THE WRONG SORT OF ACCURACY (RF_I01_P02–P03)

*KALDR RIFT — VERIFIED GREEN CORRIDOR EXIT, THEN SLOW APPROACH*

*The fast crossing is more measurement than spectacle: ships riding a verified reciprocal-field inversion, a narrow corridor of agreed timing, the steady checks that keep a route real. At the exit the convoy reduces speed before the Kaldr wreck field. No one is fighting at corridor speed. Naomi Sol has both hands near actual flight controls; Second is watching drift estimates.*

**SECOND:** Last beacon agrees across the line.

**NAOMI:** All of it?

**SECOND:** The important part.

**NAOMI:** You're going to make me ask.

**SECOND:** One marker is six milliseconds late against our local return. Same error as the last run. It's not growing.

**NAOMI:** So the important part agrees. And the part that might someday kill us is being polite.

**SECOND:** I wasn't going to phrase it that way.

**NAOMI:** That's why you're not captain yet. Hold the deceleration until the exit reference. Then bring the grain ship down before the wreck field.

*Second starts to relay it. A Fleet channel opens. The message carries an instruction rather than a request.*

**SECOND:** Thunderbreak wants us behind their screen.

**NAOMI:** They have a lovely screen. They can keep it. We're leading the approach.

**SECOND:** They say they'll take the exposed flank.

**NAOMI:** That part's sensible. Give them room aft. We control the order at the exit.

**SECOND:** They'll say you're being territorial.

**NAOMI:** I am being territorial. It is our territory. Transmit politely.

**SECOND:** How politely?

**NAOMI:** I'll know when I hear it.

**SECOND (transmitting):** Captain Sol will retain the lead through the slow approach. We request Fleet protection aft and along our exposed quarter. With appreciation.

**NAOMI:** That's ghastly.

**SECOND:** You said politely.

*Naomi permits the smallest smile. Then the smile goes. In the wreck field, two contacts are moving too well to be debris.*

**NAOMI:** That return behind the derelict. Show me the last three sweeps.

*Second brings up the prior observations. Nothing flashes a convenient diagnosis. The vectors are subtle, timed to the grain freighter's slower exit.*

**SECOND:** We have two of them.

**NAOMI:** They didn't come here looking for a ship. They're waiting for this ship.

**SECOND:** Everybody knows the new crossing opens today.

**NAOMI:** Not everybody knows our exit speed to within a few seconds.

*She checks the grain ship's position, then the old cruiser well aft.*

**SECOND:** Fleet is repeating the instruction to fall back.

**NAOMI:** Oh? Not asking this time? Tell them we know this approach better than they do. They can kiss our flank.

*Second keys the channel.*

**SECOND:** Captain Sol says she's staying in the lead. Please protect us from the rear.

*Naomi looks over.*

**NAOMI:** You left something out.

**SECOND:** With appreciation.

*Two cutters appear from wreck cover, angled not for the escorts but for the freight collars.*

**SECOND:** Both accelerating. They're not after us.

**NAOMI:** Cargo. Warn the master. Seal every inner access now. No one goes out to look at the noise.

**SECOND:** They knew the position.

**NAOMI:** Yes. And knowing how doesn't change what we do in the next thirty seconds.

*Naomi holds the lead as the grain ship alters speed behind her. The cutters' approach is geometrically credible and deeply unwelcome.*

### SCENE 3 — THE SCRAPE (RF_I01_P04)

*THUNDERBREAK — COMMAND / KALDR SLOW APPROACH*

*Thunderbreak is an old heavy cruiser that has been maintained by people who know which fittings are original. The bridge is large, practical and untheatrical. Fleet navy, service white, restrained gold. One tactical officer tracks the same two cutters Naomi sees.*

**TACTICAL:** Two Shard cutters, closing on starboard freight collars. Our aft offset is too wide to stop both.

**OFFICER:** We could burn to the grain ship.

**TACTICAL:** One of them would dock before we got there.

**OFFICER:** I said could. I didn't say we'd like the result.

*Venn studies the actual distances. The freighter continues moving. A broken derelict drifts near Thunderbreak's port-forward side, just off the raiders' approach geometry.*

**VENN:** What's the mass of that wreck section?

**TACTICAL:** Enough to make us regret touching it.

**VENN:** Does it still have propellant aboard?

**OFFICER:** None on the last survey. Structural returns are poor. And if I understand your expression, we need to have a talk about the paintwork.

**VENN:** Match its drift. Port-forward armor. We're going to persuade it to change lanes.

**TACTICAL:** We'll scrape the plating.

**VENN:** Yes.

**OFFICER:** A very expensive way to move something that doesn't belong to us.

**VENN:** It's in our way. I don't intend to keep it. Give me the clearance to the freighter.

*The officer checks. Venn waits for the calculation rather than pushing through it. Only when the trajectory is verified does she speak again.*

**OFFICER:** Safe separation from the grain ship if the wreck moves as modeled. We may have a problem with its outer truss.

**VENN:** We already have a problem with its outer truss. Damage control, stand by. Helm, take us in.

*Metal scrapes metal in a slow, awful contact. No heroic impact. Short sparks, a bounded gouge through Thunderbreak's port bow, and a modest lateral change to the derelict's drift. One cutter commits to a hard outward correction. The other, already inside the line, remains clear of the moving wreck.*

**TACTICAL:** One cutter has broken off. The inside one is still closing.

**OFFICER:** Damage control confirms port-forward plating compromised. Local only.

**VENN:** Good. Keep the bow out of the new debris. Don't give them the rest of the cruiser.

**TACTICAL:** Are we going after the second cutter?

**VENN:** Not if it means putting us through our own wreck field. Warn the freighter. We bought them a little time.

**OFFICER:** How much?

*Venn looks at the cutter's live vector.*

**VENN:** Not enough.

*The second cutter meets the freighter's starboard collar.*

### SCENE 4 — THE DOOR (RF_I01_P05)

*GRAIN FREIGHTER — STARBOARD CARGO ACCESS / SEALED PASSENGER BULKHEAD*

*The cutter docks pressure-tight to the cargo collar. The inner access is locked; its locking frame does not survive the raiders' tools. Air rushes only into the contained boarding vestibule, enough to whip loose straps and make cargo handlers grab for railings. Beyond the heavy bulkhead the passenger compartment still holds pressure.*

*A Verge guard herds passengers toward the refuge door. The mother has removed her boy's emergency helmet from a seat compartment and is struggling with its seal.*

**BOY:** You're pulling my ear.

**MOTHER:** Your ear will forgive me. Hold still.

**BOY:** I know how to do it.

**MOTHER:** Then be wonderfully knowledgeable and stop turning your head.

*The guard kneels to check the boy's fastener.*

**GUARD:** He's got the collar right. The seal's caught under his jacket.

**MOTHER:** Thank you.

**GUARD:** Everyone on this side. We close in ten.

*The mother glances down the cargo passage. Through the narrow aperture she sees the raiders moving toward grain clamps, not passengers. That isn't much comfort.*

**BOY:** Are they going to take our grain?

**MOTHER:** I don't know.

**BOY:** We don't own it.

**MOTHER:** That doesn't mean we don't eat it.

*On the cargo side a Shard raider catches a second boarder looking toward the passenger access.*

**RAIDER:** Cargo only. Leave the passengers. We take what we came for.

**SECOND RAIDER:** The collar seal's leaking.

**RAIDER:** Then work faster. I don't want to have to explain a dead passenger to anybody.

*The guard pulls the heavy pressure door shut. The latch takes an uncomfortable second to register; the guard watches the lock physically seat before reporting.*

**GUARD:** Passenger compartment sealed. Stay away from the inspection glass.

**BOY:** Is there going to be a fight?

*The mother wants to say no. She can't.*

**MOTHER:** Sit with me. If there is, you won't help it by watching.

*They sit together. The boy keeps the helmet on. The mother keeps a hand over one of his gloved hands, even after the metal sounds on the other side have moved away.*

### SCENE 5 — THE SHIP THEY HAVE (RF_I01_P06)

*KALDR RIFT — MINUTES AFTER BOARDING / THUNDERBREAK COMMAND / NAOMI'S COCKPIT*

*The boarding cutter withdraws with three intact, modestly sized grain containers. The second cutter covers it after maneuvering around the displaced wreck. The first cutter pulls badly at the damaged cargo collar; a separate, fourth remaining container loses its brace, splits, and sheds grain into vacuum. The passenger refuge remains sealed.*

**TACTICAL:** Three containers loaded onto the boarding cutter. They're separating.

**OFFICER:** Other cutter's coming around to cover. We could catch the loaded one if we burn now.

**VENN:** And leave what between the freighter and their friend?

**OFFICER:** Distance. More than I'd care to explain to Captain Sol.

**TACTICAL:** Another container has split. Cargo-side collar is coming apart.

*On the exterior feed, bright individual grains escape through a gap that ought not be there. Venn watches the raiders recede and keeps herself from following them on instinct.*

**VENN:** Then we stay. Protect the freighter.

**OFFICER:** They're going to get away.

**VENN:** Yes.

**OFFICER:** We have a pursuit solution.

**VENN:** We also have a civilian ship with a damaged collar in a wreck field. I'm choosing that one. You may write down that I disliked it.

*Naomi's voice cuts in, quick and unsentimental.*

**NAOMI (comms):** Passenger pressure holding. No missing souls on the freighter. The cargo master says he's lost the port-side brace and half his temper.

**VENN:** Three containers captured. A fourth damaged. The manifest stays Verge-owned. We'll attach our damage observations.

**NAOMI (comms):** Very kind. You've got a mark on your bow.

**VENN:** We noticed.

**NAOMI (comms):** Is it a problem?

**VENN:** Not one for you. I'm covering your aft quarter. You fly the approach.

*Naomi pauses, registering the answer. For once there's no argument over the chain of control.*

**NAOMI (comms):** Understood. Tell your helm to leave a little space when we pass the next wreck. My day's been long enough without your ship borrowing another piece of it.

*Venn permits herself half a breath of amusement after the channel closes. The convoy resumes, smaller in cargo, intact in passengers, with Thunderbreak's port-bow scrape plainly visible.*

### SCENE 6 — AURIC, AT NIGHT (RF_I01_P07–P08)

*AURIC BAZAAR — EVENING INTO NIGHT*

*Auric is spectacular. Its rings and civilized concourses have the confident beauty of a place prosperous enough to build public space for pleasure. Under that architecture, people are standing in lines with luggage, waiting for heat and a place to sleep. The Luminara freighter docks. All its passengers disembark alive.*

*The boy looks up so far his mother has to tug his sleeve to keep him moving.*

**BOY:** It's bigger than the pictures.

**MOTHER:** The pictures didn't have the line.

**BOY:** Maybe we're in the wrong line.

**MOTHER:** We've tried three. This is the one for people who want beds.

*Dock staff count remaining grain while laborers separate three empty container positions from one visibly ruptured unit. Naomi is standing with Venn at the manifest desk when Halev and Wallace arrive from the civic freight office.*

**NAOMI:** Three taken. One split after the cutter disconnected. Everyone who boarded at Luminara is here.

**WALLACE:** Good. Excellent. The dead would have made an ugly accounting problem.

*Naomi gives him a look. Wallace regrets the words immediately.*

**WALLACE:** That came out wrong. I'm glad they're alive. I'm also thinking about traders who paid for grain they won't receive. Both are true.

**HALEV:** Have the inspectors count what's physically docked. No promises based on what was loaded at Luminara.

**WALLACE:** And before the wholesalers start calling it a Fleet loss, remind them it is Verge grain.

**VENN:** Nobody aboard Thunderbreak is claiming ownership. Our damage record will follow the manifest.

**NAOMI:** I was going to say exactly that, but less elegantly.

**VENN:** I suspect you still might.

**NAOMI:** Depends what you do next.

*Halev's attention has moved to a registration queue near a heater. An older resident is sharing the warmest spot with a new arrival; both have coat collars turned up. The mother and boy wait a few paces behind.*

**HALEV:** How long have they been standing here?

**CIVIC WORKER:** New arrivals? About an hour. Housing asked us to send everyone to the same desk. They haven't sent a desk clerk.

**HALEV:** Then send someone from my office.

**WALLACE:** They are already doing freight compensation.

**HALEV:** They can spend the next hour doing this. It will probably be the better use of their time.

*Halev walks to the mother, not with a practiced speech but because she is next in the line.*

**HALEV:** You've got a berth assignment?

**MOTHER:** I've got three pieces of paper and a son who has been promised the best rooms in Auric.

**BOY:** I didn't say best.

**MOTHER:** Better. A very specific word.

**HALEV:** We have places to register tonight. I can't promise every room is ready.

**MOTHER:** We came because we'd heard there was work, and heat, and somewhere to live. I can wait for work. It's the other two I worry about.

*Halev looks toward the beautiful concourse, then at the unused area beyond a civilian meeting room.*

**HALEV:** Open the civic warming room. Seat families there until housing can make an actual plan.

**WALLACE:** Some of those rooms are booked tomorrow.

**HALEV:** Tomorrow the booking office can bring its complaint to me. Tonight the rooms are warm.

**WALLACE:** I'll put that in writing before the office does it for me.

*The boy looks up at the governor after the group begins to disperse.*

**BOY:** Are the rooms really warmer?

**HALEV:** The room you're going to tonight is. I haven't seen the one you'll have next week.

**BOY:** That's what my mother said.

**HALEV:** Then she's ahead of the government.

*The mother almost laughs. Her hand is still tight around the bag. Halev notices but doesn't remark on it.*

*As registration resumes, Wallace walks beside Halev.*

**WALLACE:** The markets at both ends will feel this loss. A permanent fast route could make it up in weeks. If traders still trust it.

**HALEV:** People will come faster, too.

**WALLACE:** That's how a successful port usually works.

**HALEV:** Then we should find out what kind of port we can afford to be.

*They leave under lights beautiful enough to conceal the cold only from someone who hasn't been standing in it.*

### SCENE 7 — WHAT JEX SOLD (RF_I01_P09)

*AURIC BAZAAR — DOCK-SIDE BROKER TABLE, LATE NIGHT*

*Jex Marrin occupies the edge of a busy trade passage, where people negotiate freight beside food stalls that are closing for the night. He has a small physical route token under his thumb and a merchant's angry message in front of him. He reads it twice, not because the words are unclear, but because he is considering how not to answer them.*

*Billie Rusk arrives without waiting to be welcomed. Her bright hair and Shard flightwear are unmistakable. She helps herself to a chair that belongs to the neighboring table and tips it toward Jex.*

**JEX:** That's not my chair.

**BILLIE:** I know. Yours looks uncomfortable.

**JEX:** It's the only reason anyone leaves.

**BILLIE:** That and the prices.

*She eyes the token. Jex covers it with a hand.*

**JEX:** I gave you a crossing time. I didn't promise the freighter would come back in pieces.

**BILLIE:** It came back in one piece. An important piece is missing a few expensive things, but that's a different complaint.

**JEX:** Three stolen containers. A fourth torn open. Two merchants are already delaying their next departures.

**BILLIE:** Venn put a wreck across our approach. We took the three intact. Do you want me to apologize for not stealing the broken one?

**JEX:** I'd like you to recognize that merchants don't enjoy having their ships disassembled.

**BILLIE:** Neither do I. Tess is probably going to make me spend an entire shift fixing Ashfang's collar. I'll send you the bill.

**JEX:** You can send it. My waste chute accepts almost anything.

*Billie smiles. It's an old exchange. Her smile fades when he brings up the traders again.*

**BILLIE:** We brought food home. The families at Black Drift can eat tonight. When was the last time your merchants lost sleep because a Shard household had an empty table?

**JEX:** Last night, I suspect. They're frightened enough to avoid the crossing, and they were just starting to trust it.

**BILLIE:** Trust Fleet, you mean.

**JEX:** Trust the route. Very different thing, and I'd like to keep them different. If the merchant ships stop coming, you won't have grain to take, and I won't have grain to negotiate over. There's a little tragedy for both our professions.

*Billie watches two workers carry a ruined freight strap toward repair.*

**BILLIE:** You want us dependent on the next shipment.

**JEX:** I want there to be a next shipment. After that I expect to fight with you about it, possibly over a nicer table.

**BILLIE:** You keep talking as if you get to make the plan.

**JEX:** I have persuaded more than one captain to listen to a plan.

**BILLIE:** You persuaded me to listen to a crossing time. And I listened. Don't start confusing favors with the wheel of my ship.

*Jex turns the token over in his palm.*

**JEX:** No, I suppose that would be dangerous.

**BILLIE:** It already is. But you're still going to give me the next one, aren't you?

**JEX:** The next what?

**BILLIE:** Crossing.

**JEX:** You do like testing people.

**BILLIE:** No, Jex. I like knowing them.

*For a moment the two are quiet. Then Billie puts the borrowed chair back exactly where she found it, to Jex's surprise.*

**BILLIE:** Tell the merchants we left their passengers alone.

**JEX:** I will. They may even find that comforting.

**BILLIE:** They should. It was a choice.

*She goes. Jex begins writing a reply to the merchant. He erases his first sentence. A second message arrives before he's found a better one.*

### SCENE 8 — THREE CONTAINERS (RF_I01_P10–P12)

*BLACK DRIFT — WORKING SHARD REPAIR YARD, THEN ASHFANG'S BAY*

*The captured containers have arrived intact. Workers distribute grain into household allotments while welders inspect the damaged cargo collar of Ashfang Marauder. Families wait with crates and handcarts, neither grateful supplicants nor a faceless crowd. Everyone has something else to do after they collect the food.*

*Tessa Banks, sleeves rolled above a repaired flightwear underlayer, presses a tool against an ugly stretch of the collar frame. The tool gives a noise she clearly dislikes.*

**TESS:** Well, that's worse than it looked when it was on fire.

**BILLIE:** It wasn't on fire.

**TESS:** Then your crew should stop describing it that way. Makes the repairs sound more exciting than they are.

**BILLIE:** Can you patch it?

**TESS:** Yes. Can you stop hitting things with it?

**BILLIE:** I'll take that as a yes.

*Abby arrives from the ration point. She has been carrying a crate herself. A worker hurries toward her before she can set it down.*

**WORKER:** The families want the last container opened now. The collar crew wants the lift under the pressure frame. I have one lift and two people who both think I'm doing my job badly.

**ABBY:** Are they wrong?

**WORKER:** Yes.

**ABBY:** Then you're having a better morning than I am. Use the lift for the grain while the weld cools. Brace the pressure frame where it is. Tess signs it off before we load it again.

**TESS:** And if somebody tries to hurry me, they can explain to Billie why her next docking collar will be made of optimism.

**BILLIE:** I prefer metal.

**TESS:** You can tell me that when you stop scraping it off on other ships.

*The worker gets the lift moving. Billie watches a parent divide a small grain allotment into two bags and give one to an older neighbor.*

**BILLIE:** Three containers past a Fleet cruiser. That counts for something.

**ABBY:** It does.

**BILLIE:** That's not how you say it when you're pleased.

**ABBY:** I am pleased. I'm also looking at how long it has to last.

**BILLIE:** People are eating tonight.

**ABBY:** And two traders delayed their next runs. People will need to eat next week.

**BILLIE:** There'll be another crossing.

**TESS:** Assuming we finish the collar before our next birthdays.

**BILLIE:** You spend more time insulting it than fixing it.

**TESS:** It's a very old collar. It needs encouragement.

*Much later, the three share a small meal on an overturned work crate just beyond the repair bay. Tess keeps a seal brace in her lap. Billie is hungry enough to eat before sitting down. Abby is reviewing the delayed merchant departures.*

**BILLIE:** You're doing it again.

**ABBY:** Doing what?

**BILLIE:** Turning dinner into an examination.

**ABBY:** Two traders just pushed their crossings back. I don't like the pattern.

**BILLIE:** I don't like that they can decide when we eat.

**TESS:** Neither do the traders. They'd rather have us buy the food and leave their ships alone. A sentimental lot.

**ABBY:** What if the food came here without us intercepting it?

*Billie stops chewing.*

**BILLIE:** You mean buy it.

**ABBY:** Buy some. Trade what we can. Arrange delivery. Something that leaves the ships in one piece.

**BILLIE:** And ask Fleet's permission to use the road while we do it?

**ABBY:** They don't own the whole road.

**BILLIE:** They act like they do. They'll send one cruiser today, three tomorrow. Then we'll be paying to pass the place where our people used to live.

**ABBY:** If every crossing ends with wreckage, there won't be a road worth taking.

**BILLIE:** It's easy to talk about stopping raids when you've got food on your plate.

*The words land harder than Billie expected. The food came from the very raid they're discussing. Abby doesn't answer immediately.*

**ABBY:** I went hungry with you. Don't make me prove it again.

**BILLIE:** I didn't mean—

**ABBY:** I know. But you said it.

*Tess slides the brace into the space between them with a decisive clack.*

**TESS:** Would one of you hold this before I decide it's personally offended me?

*Abby takes one end. Billie takes the other. The familiar division of work returns without solving anything.*

**BILLIE:** You really think merchants will sail here willingly?

**ABBY:** I think we ought to find out before there aren't any left.

**BILLIE:** Suppose they're willing only because Fleet's got guns on them.

**ABBY:** Then it's not what I'm asking for.

**TESS:** You two can invent a new civilization when I get this frame shut. Right now, lift.

*They lift together. A waiting family passes beyond the repair rail with an allotment that will feed them for a little while. Ashfang's collar remains partly disassembled.*

### SCENE 9 — THREE DIFFERENT HUNGERS (RF_I01_P13)

*BLACK DRIFT — SILENT TRIARCH CHAMBER*

*Three political authorities occupy their distinct established places, each masked differently. The chamber has worn flooring and practical working tables; no ceremony suggests magic. Abby stands where petitioning captains stand, a tablet of actual shipping losses in her hand. Captains are politically accountable but independently command their own ships.*

**RAID:** The standing grain order remains. Cutters take what the frontier carries.

**TRADE:** At the last crossing, they also damaged the vehicle carrying it.

**RAID:** One convoy. Three containers reached our people.

**TRADE:** Two merchants have suspended their departures. If the next two do the same, what exactly will our cutters take?

**FEAR:** The Core will keep sending guards. It has to. It calls that obligation a virtue. I call it an expense.

**ABBY:** The expense doesn't fall on one side. We have grain today and fewer ships willing to come tomorrow.

**RAID:** You're responsible for getting the next shipment home intact.

**ABBY:** I can plan an interception. I can't repair a merchant's confidence with a weapon.

**FEAR:** Confidence is an easily changed feeling.

**ABBY:** So is fear. Neither puts grain on a table without a ship delivering it.

*The Trade Mask turns toward Abby, attentive. Raid does not.*

**TRADE:** What do you propose?

**ABBY:** Stop treating the freighter as a disposable container. The ship is what brings the next cargo. If you want supplies, you need people willing to send them.

**RAID:** Your captains already know how to take cargo without breaking ships.

**ABBY:** Billie's cutter took an unsound approach because Fleet moved the wreck. It can happen again. There are limits to what the crew can control.

**RAID:** Then they should learn to manage the limits.

**FEAR:** A visible risk can be useful. The Core should not be allowed to believe protection is free.

**TRADE:** Nor should merchants be allowed to believe this market is dead.

*The three masks are not speaking for one mind. Abby sees that and takes the opening without overstating it.*

**ABBY:** Then let us keep the crossings usable. I'll bring you food where I can. But if a ship breaks, calling it a lesson won't fill the holds.

*Trade studies her. Raid makes no concession. Fear looks toward the visible tally of crossings that have been deferred.*

**RAID:** The order is unchanged.

**ABBY:** Understood.

*She leaves without pretending she has won. Behind her, the three authorities continue disagreeing about what the same shortage means.*

### SCENE 10 — THE COST OF GETTING WHAT YOU WANT (RF_I01_P14–P15)

*AURIC BAZAAR — CIVIC ROUTE OFFICE — NEXT MORNING*

*The office overlooks a beautifully maintained transport ring. The room is not lavish: workers have improvised extra desks for arrival manifests and heating figures. Halev is standing over an allotment plan. Naomi has just returned from inspecting the Green Corridor approach. Wallace brings a stack of projected merchant departures.*

**WALLACE:** The growers think regular fast crossings could make last night's loss back in weeks. That's not an exaggeration. We can finally put perishable goods on the other side before they become fertilizer.

**NAOMI:** Assuming the markers hold and nobody treats the slow approach like the fast segment.

**WALLACE:** You're constitutionally unable to be happy about good news.

**NAOMI:** I like good news. I simply prefer it after the measurements.

**HALEV:** How many new arrivals can we house if regular runs begin?

*Wallace looks at the heat figures. He has prepared commercial projections, not beds.*

**WALLACE:** That's a separate budget.

**HALEV:** Unfortunately, the arrivals won't separate themselves by budget.

**WALLACE:** More freight means more work. More work helps us build housing.

**HALEV:** Eventually. The boy who came from Luminara wanted to know where he'd sleep last night. We gave him a public room. That's what we actually have today.

**WALLACE:** We can't refuse trade because someone is cold.

**HALEV:** Nor can we declare trade a success because someone can afford a warmer room when it arrives.

**NAOMI:** There's another question. Fleet wants a standing escort assignment. If they maintain the high-speed markers, they'll have a legitimate operational stake in what happens on the route.

**WALLACE:** We need their cruisers.

**NAOMI:** I know. I was on the other end of the approach yesterday. I just don't need them deciding which Verge freighter gets to dock first.

**HALEV:** We'll propose separate responsibilities. Fleet maintains and verifies what it can measure; Fleet commands its own patrol ships. Verge pilots control the slow approaches. Civilian ports keep cargo and crossing order.

**WALLACE:** Dominion will ask for a lasting commitment.

**HALEV:** Of course he will. If I were building routes for a living, I'd ask for it too.

**NAOMI:** Then put the expiry and review on the page. Don't make a one-way obligation because we're relieved someone brought the repair tools.

*Halev starts revising the conditions. Wallace stays, because he sees the opportunity more clearly than he dislikes the burden.*

**WALLACE:** I'll support it. I just want it understood that every day of delay is another day we pay the old price of distance.

**HALEV:** Put that in the council report. Alongside the heat allotment.

**WALLACE:** The council will hate that.

**HALEV:** I know. Let's give them two things they need to read.

**NAOMI:** And make sure the escort agreement is written before any captain mistakes a favor for a jurisdiction.

*She gathers the route measurements and leaves for her ship. Halev watches the work outside: freight unloading, a cold line outside the warming room, a new route promising more of both.*

### SCENE 11 — REACH AND RESOLVE (RF_I01_P16–P17)

*CORE ADMIRALTY — AUTHORIZATION CHAMBER*

*Dominion and Rhyne occupy a bright, mature Fleet institution. Venn presents the verified Kaldr figures without theatrical flourish. The gouge on Thunderbreak's port bow is visible in a practical damage projection. Venn doesn't conceal it.*

**RHYNE:** That's a serious-looking abrasion for a ship that wasn't meant to touch anything.

**VENN:** It is mostly an insult to the paintwork. The structural inspection is less offended.

**RHYNE:** You say that now. The yard will want an entire week to explain how offended it is.

**DOMINION:** The maneuver kept a cutter off the freighter.

**VENN:** One cutter. The other boarded. Three containers were taken, and a different one was lost when the fitting split. The civilian passengers arrived alive.

**DOMINION:** Good. Tell us about the route.

*Venn transfers to the measured reciprocal-field and low-speed exit data.*

**VENN:** The reblazed segment holds verification. Verge pilots have a reliable approach procedure. Auric offers crossing data and joint upkeep, provided port order and civilian freight remain under Verge control.

**RHYNE:** In other words, they want our cruiser but not our opinion.

**VENN:** They want our opinion about things we understand. They don't need a Fleet officer deciding a local berthing queue.

**DOMINION:** We can accept that distinction and still maintain authority over the patrol.

**RHYNE:** Today. Tomorrow every trader will assume our ship is there. The day after, the fastest route becomes the only route anyone can afford. Then we aren't offering protection; we're an essential part of their economy.

**DOMINION:** That is one of the reasons to restore reliable travel.

**RHYNE:** It's also one of the reasons people resent us.

*Dominion considers the argument instead of waving it away.*

**DOMINION:** Generations spent planning voyages by how many years they could afford to lose. People abandoned relatives they could not reach. Entire systems developed without knowing whether help was a lifetime away. We are finally able to restore a route that can be maintained. I will not pretend that is a minor responsibility because someone may someday dislike the manner in which we discharge it.

**VENN:** Nor should we pretend it's ours merely because we know how to maintain it.

*Dominion looks at her. The disagreement is genuine and not yet adversarial.*

**DOMINION:** I didn't propose annexing Auric, Commodore.

**VENN:** I know. I'm asking that the written order make it impossible for an eager lieutenant to think we did.

*Rhyne gives a reluctant, approving nod.*

**RHYNE:** At least that costs less than another cruiser.

**DOMINION:** You always calculate the cost of being needed. Have you calculated the cost of not being there?

**RHYNE:** Every time we don't have enough ships.

*That, too, is true. Dominion signs a provisional trial authorization, one that requires ongoing verification, beacon maintenance, and available patrol capacity.*

**DOMINION:** Trial authorization. Continue the field verification. Patrol the route when Thunderbreak is ready. No local port authority without consent.

**VENN:** Port-bow repairs first.

**DOMINION:** Naturally.

**RHYNE:** The yard will be delighted. You can have the other half of the discussion when they see what you did to their work.

**VENN:** I was hoping to delegate that part.

**DOMINION:** You may delegate the paperwork. Not the responsibility.

*Venn accepts the order. She believes in the mission and is alert to its cost. Those facts have not yet come into conflict.*

### SCENE 12 — THE NEXT CROSSING (RF_I01_P18)

*BLACK DRIFT — SHARD ASSEMBLY ORBIT AND DEPARTURE BAY*

*Ashfang's collar has been repaired enough to fly; the patched history remains visible. Nearby, Gravefire and Scythe of Saville remain separately commanded ships. Workers have started securing new provisions and replacement parts. Word of the continued Green Corridor trial has reached the dock.*

*Tess comes out of a work access with a tool in one hand and the look of someone who hasn't slept enough.*

**TESS:** The merchants are scheduling again. Gravefire's ready when you two are. Please don't tell me that means we're leaving immediately.

**BILLIE:** We could leave immediately.

**TESS:** I see. So you were listening only for an opportunity to be difficult.

**ABBY:** More crossings mean more chances to get food home. Keep the ships intact this time.

**BILLIE:** They also mean more Fleet guns. I don't like the idea that our next meal depends on how generously Dominion feels about putting a cruiser in our path.

**ABBY:** It already depends on how the merchants feel about risking their freighters. I'm trying to reduce the number of people whose moods can starve us.

**TESS:** Both of you could start by giving back the equipment you keep taking out of my bay.

**BILLIE:** Borrowing.

**TESS:** Borrowing has a return date. You seem to think it's a matter of archaeological discovery.

*Billie fishes a small fitting from her pocket, examines it, and hands it back.*

**TESS:** That's the one I specifically told you not to touch.

**BILLIE:** I looked after it.

**TESS:** You used it to pry something open.

**BILLIE:** It worked.

**TESS:** It did. Look at it now.

*Abby laughs, unexpectedly. Billie watches her, then catches it herself. They are still friends. No one has made the later decisions yet.*

**BILLIE:** You think a better arrangement is coming, Abby. I can see it on your face. You think if we behave nicely, someone will decide we deserve to eat.

**ABBY:** I think we have to stop measuring success by how much we can take from a ship before it breaks.

**BILLIE:** And I think someone ought to pay attention to why we started doing it.

*Neither is prepared to yield that point. Tess tucks the damaged fitting into a pocket.*

**TESS:** I would love to live long enough for this argument to become boring. Until then, Ashfang has a flight check and Gravefire needs her own crew back.

**ABBY:** You heard her. Ready your ships. Independently, before Billie tries to borrow one of those too.

**BILLIE:** Yours isn't my type.

**TESS:** Too many working parts?

*Billie throws a gloved hand at her. Tess steps easily aside. Work resumes across the bay. Outside, separate Shard vessels prepare for a newly reopened route that offers the Shards food and the Fleet influence—and leaves their captains free to make very different choices.*
