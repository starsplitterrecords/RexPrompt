## Core issue function
A municipal status/reconciliation review turns the household's accumulated life in New York into something that must now be defended.

The issue is not about a corporate-placement challenge. Its dramatic engine is:

**A household that has quietly become part of New York learns that its temporary status is under formal review, and Carrie and the Vikings must decide together how to answer that threat.**

## Relationship to Issue 5
Issue 5's neighborhood-memory material was deliberately given room before WAR COUNCIL. That work now pays off here.

Repeated civilians, merchants, neighbors, laundromat/park/library routines, and the Kin's quiet independent adaptation can begin converting from background texture into **evidence of actual life**.

Do not recap Issue 5 mechanically. Let Carrie discover that the household has more community ties and independent routines than she realized.

## Relationship to Issue 6
Issue 6 restores Bjorn's respect and meaning through the mixed-era temporal-refugee support group and his accidental re-emergence as a leader.

WAR COUNCIL should use that development rather than reset him to reactive bluster. When the review notice arrives, Bjorn's response is to gather people, listen, weigh threats, and lead.

He may still be intense and culturally alien, but the issue should demonstrate that his leadership now has modern relevance.

## Carrie arc
The key visual/emotional milestone is **Carrie joining the table**.

Earlier in the season, Carrie manages the Vikings as a case. In WAR COUNCIL, the threat is to a shared household and accumulated life. She eventually stops standing over the paperwork as the professional in charge and sits with them as part of the council.

This is not abandonment of professional competence. Her forms, deadlines, procedural knowledge, and strategy remain essential. The change is relational: she is now planning **with** them.

## Gunnar function
Gunnar should identify categories, contradictions, and what the review actually requires, but do not turn him back into a one-dimensional navigation/system decoder.

His useful insight in the preserved treatment is that the real threat is the mismatch between:

- their actual lived relationships, routines, obligations, and contribution; and
- what the municipal system knows how to count.

## Kin function
The Kin's earlier background adaptation becomes newly relevant here.

Carrie can discover, in small pieces, that they have:

- regular errands
- merchant relationships
- neighbor relationships
- parks / cultural routines
- household responsibilities
- recognizable places and people in the neighborhood

Do not turn this into a giant reveal speech. It should feel like accumulated facts Carrie simply never stopped to ask about.

## Visual grammar
Primary space: Bushwick studio and hallway.

The apartment table functions as:

- treaty table
- war table
- paperwork altar

Use tactile domestic specificity:

- brass key
- municipal envelopes/forms
- dishes / cutlery
- coffee
- smoke residue / ventilation problem if retained
- scrap metal / improvised household objects

The comedy comes from Bjorn treating mundane administrative preparation with complete war-council gravity while the actual strategy remains bureaucratic and communal rather than violent.

## End-state
By the end of WAR COUNCIL:

- the review threat is understood as real;
- Carrie and the Vikings are planning together;
- neighborhood ties and Kin routines are beginning to become evidentiary rather than merely atmospheric;
- Bjorn is functioning as a leader again rather than merely reacting to dependency;
- the household understands that belonging will have to be demonstrated to an institution that cannot directly see lived belonging;
- the story is positioned naturally for Issue 8's filing / audit / hearing-preparation escalation.
