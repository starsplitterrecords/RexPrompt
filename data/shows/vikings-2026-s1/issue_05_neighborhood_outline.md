# Vignette A — Bjorn's Coffee Guy
## Pages 1–6

Purpose: establish Bjorn's adaptation through repeated social ritual. The coffee itself is not the joke. The joke is that accurate repetition and remembered preference mean much more to Bjorn than the vendor realizes.

### Page 1 — The Morning Road
**Function:** Introduce the coffee cart as an already-busy neighborhood institution on a route Carrie, Bjorn and Gunnar are using after the transit adaptation of Iron Worm.

**Cast:** Bjorn, Gunnar, Carrie, Coffee Vendor, ordinary commuters.

**Beats:**
- Morning sidewalk traffic naturally compresses around a familiar coffee cart.
- Carrie stops as part of an ordinary routine; Bjorn and Gunnar watch how quickly the vendor handles people.
- Gunnar notices that the cart organizes the morning flow without any formal authority.
- Recovered line available: **"The coffee man controls the morning road."**
- Bjorn watches the exchange itself: order, payment, remembered shorthand, immediate handoff.
- End: vendor asks Bjorn what he wants. No awe at coffee, no caffeine joke.

**Final beat:** Bjorn decides by observing a normal customer's order rather than asking Carrie to translate the ritual.

---

### Page 2 — The First Cup
**Function:** Establish preference, not discovery.

**Cast:** Bjorn, Gunnar, Carrie, Coffee Vendor.

**Beats:**
- Bjorn gets a plain regular coffee and drinks it seriously.
- He decides whether he likes it without performing surprise at a hot beverage.
- Vendor offers or accidentally gives a sweeter/flavored alternative for comparison.
- Bjorn dislikes the alternate.
- The vendor accepts this as ordinary customer information and keeps working.
- Gunnar is more amused by Bjorn's certainty than by the drink.

**Final beat:** the vendor now has one piece of information about Bjorn: the drink he does not want.

---

### Page 3 — Again
**Function:** Time compression. Show that a second and third visit require less language.

**Cast:** Bjorn, Gunnar, Coffee Vendor.

**Beats:**
- Another morning. Same cart, different surrounding commuters.
- Bjorn starts to order; the vendor is already reaching for the regular setup.
- Gunnar notices the interaction before Bjorn does.
- The vendor asks only enough to confirm the choice.
- Bjorn pays correctly and moves with the line rather than blocking it.
- The exchange is faster than the first visit.

**Final beat:** Bjorn looks back once, registering that the vendor remembered.

---

### Page 4 — Enemy Coffee
**Function:** Preserve the strongest existing comic exchange while deepening recognition.

**Cast:** Bjorn, Gunnar, Coffee Vendor.

**Beats:**
- Vendor sees them coming and holds up two choices.
- Strong salvage:
  - **Coffee Vendor: "Regular, or the one you hated?"**
  - **Bjorn: "Regular."**
  - **Gunnar: "You have an enemy coffee."**
  - **Bjorn: "I chose it once."**
  - **Coffee Vendor: "That's how enemies start."**
- Gunnar laughs at Bjorn, not at the city.
- The vendor has already begun the correct cup.

**Final beat:** Bjorn accepts the cup with the gravity of a correctly remembered obligation.

---

### Page 5 — His Name
**Function:** Move from remembered order to remembered person.

**Cast:** Bjorn, Coffee Vendor; Carrie may enter late.

**Beats:**
- Another ordinary morning.
- The vendor addresses Bjorn by name before Bjorn says anything.
- Bjorn pauses. This matters.
- Do not make the vendor sentimental; he learned the name because Bjorn is there repeatedly and somebody said it.
- Bjorn asks a short confirming question or simply looks at him.
- Vendor keeps serving the line.

**Source mechanism:** recovered older fragment in which the merchant remembered Bjorn's name. Preserve the mechanism, not the obsolete "woman at the counter" identity.

**Final beat:** Bjorn leaves with the cup, visibly more affected by being remembered than by being served.

---

### Page 6 — The Bond
**Function:** Close the vignette by showing the mismatch in how Bjorn and Carrie quantify relationship.

**Cast:** Bjorn, Carrie, Gunnar.

**Beats:**
- Carrie catches up with them after the cart.
- Bjorn states, simply, that the coffee man knows him.
- Carrie reduces it to repetition.
- Strong recovered exchange to preserve if it still reads naturally in final dialogue:
  - **Carrie: "You ordered the same thing six times."**
  - **Bjorn: "Then the bond is proven."**
- Gunnar does not explain or mock the conclusion.
- They continue down the block.

**Final beat:** behind them, the Coffee Vendor is already serving the next person. To him, this was ordinary. To Bjorn, ordinary repetition is precisely what made it meaningful.

# Vignette B — Gunnar and Sal
## Pages 7–14

Purpose: Gunnar establishes a reliable merchant relationship and gradually incorporates the bodega into his working model of the neighborhood. The bodega is not a fantasy market. Sal succeeds because he is practical, consistent and uninterested in making Gunnar into an event.

### Page 7 — Provisions
**Function:** Give Gunnar a practical reason to return to the bodega on his own after Iron Worm has established that he can navigate nearby routes.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Carrie, Bjorn briefly; Sal by page end.

**Beats:**
- Household needs food that requires no hearth or elaborate cooking.
- Carrie gives Gunnar a short list and usable cash, not a tutorial in shopping.
- Bjorn has another task and does not accompany him.
- Gunnar knows where the bodega is.
- He enters with purpose.
- Sal recognizes him from earlier household purchases but does not make a ceremony of it.

**Historical source available but not mandatory verbatim:** the old "provisions / no cooking / cash or card / tribute" exchange. Mature production should keep the practical mismatch without regressing Gunnar into broad fantasy language.

**Final beat:** Sal asks the only question that matters to the transaction: what Gunnar needs.

---

### Page 8 — No Questions
**Function:** Establish why Gunnar respects Sal.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Sal.

**Beats:**
- Gunnar asks for durable, ready-to-eat food using concise practical descriptions.
- Sal translates these into ordinary bodega inventory immediately.
- He does not interrogate Gunnar's clothes, accent, history or household.
- Gunnar expects at least one unnecessary question and gets none.
- The transaction works.
- If old silver-coin material is retained later, it must not replace the current money continuity or undermine the coming Tally issue; the relationship is the important part.

**Recovered observation to preserve in spirit or caption form:** **"He did not ask questions. I respect this."**

**Final beat:** Gunnar leaves with exactly the useful food he came for.

---

### Page 9 — The Store Has a Shape
**Function:** Let Gunnar map a place because mapping is how he learns reliability.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Sal, bodega cat.

**Beats:**
- On a later visit Gunnar begins noting where useful categories are located.
- This is not tactical parody. It is practical memory: water, bread, dried meat, batteries, soap, counter.
- He notices which prepared food has been sitting too long.
- Old source option: a small X by the taquitos that have apparently been there since Tuesday.
- He notices the cat watching the store.
- Recovered line available: **"A worthy sentinel."**
- Sal catches him looking at the cat and does not care.

**Final beat:** Gunnar updates his small neighborhood map with the bodega as a dependable point, not merely a building.

---

### Page 10 — Sal
**Function:** Move the relationship from "bodega owner" to a named person.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Sal.

**Beats:**
- Gunnar returns enough times to hear another customer call the owner Sal, or Sal introduces himself during a payment problem.
- Gunnar uses the name once, correctly.
- Sal uses Gunnar's name in return.
- Neither comments on the milestone.
- Sal remembers one household preference or practical need without being asked.

**Final beat:** Gunnar leaves with the goods and the name. The map now contains a person, not just a location.

---

### Page 11 — Same
**Function:** Show repetition turning into shorthand.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Sal.

**Beats:**
- Gunnar arrives.
- Sal has already put one familiar useful item within reach.
- The transaction is shorter than Page 8.
- Gunnar catches that Sal anticipated the need.
- Sal treats this as ordinary merchant memory, not friendship.
- Gunnar quietly decides it counts anyway.

**Final beat:** only a few words were required.

---

### Page 12 — Reciprocity
**Function:** Gunnar decides that repeated useful exchange creates an obligation to give something back beyond payment.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Bjorn or Astrid briefly at home; Sal not required until end.

**Beats:**
- At home or on the fire escape, Gunnar carves a **small** simple token from scrap material.
- Keep this modest so it does not consume the later larger craftsmanship/carving story.
- Bjorn asks what it is for.
- Gunnar says it is for Sal or for the merchant.
- No speech about friendship.
- Gunnar brings it on the next supply run.

**Final beat:** the token disappears into Gunnar's pocket as he heads downstairs.

---

### Page 13 — The Token
**Function:** Pay off reciprocity without turning Sal sentimental.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Sal.

**Beats:**
- Normal purchase first.
- Gunnar places the small carved token near the register after payment.
- Sal notices.
- He does not ask what it means, praise it or perform gratitude.
- Gunnar waits half a beat for some response.
- Sal moves to the next customer or continues stocking.

**Recovered observation:** **"I have left him a small carved token. He has not acknowledged it."**

**Final beat:** Gunnar leaves, apparently satisfied.

---

### Page 14 — Respect
**Function:** Close the vignette by confirming that Sal's non-performance is precisely what Gunnar values.

**Cast:** Gunnar, Sal, bodega cat.

**Beats:**
- Later visit.
- The carved token is still present somewhere ordinary near the register, perhaps serving as a paperweight or tucked beside the till.
- Sal still does not mention it.
- The cat occupies its familiar position.
- Sal hands Gunnar the usual item.
- Their exchange is now almost entirely practical shorthand.

**Recovered observation to preserve as private narration if used:** **"He has not acknowledged it. I respect this also."**

**Final beat:** Gunnar exits; Sal calls the next customer. Relationship exists without declaration.

# Vignette C — Ordinary Rules
## Pages 15–21

Purpose: widen recognition beyond Bjorn and Gunnar without cashing the later full-independence payoff. The household encounters ordinary neighborhood rules, learns them once, and becomes easier to live around. Recognition becomes ambient.

### Page 15 — Laundry Day
**Function:** Introduce the laundromat as a recurring household place, not a "Vikings discover washing machine" set piece.

**Cast:** Carrie, Astrid, First Male Kin, Laundromat Regular; optional Gunnar for a brief opening beat only.

**Beats:**
- Carrie accompanies part of the household with laundry because this is still early integration.
- Astrid and First Male Kin already understand sorting by material and care more about protecting wool/leather/metal than Carrie expects.
- They keep armor and unsuitable materials out of the machines without being told.
- A Laundromat Regular with a red rolling cart makes one practical correction about local machine/table etiquette.
- They listen immediately.

**Old tutorial line ("So you don't smell like war") may be omitted unless a natural place appears. Mature version should foreground competence.**

**Final beat:** the correction is accepted without argument.

---

### Page 16 — Four Minutes
**Function:** Use machine unreliability rather than machine incomprehensibility as the joke.

**Cast:** Astrid, First Male Kin, Carrie, Laundromat Regular.

**Beats:**
- Washer/dryer display claims a small number of minutes remaining.
- They wait.
- The number barely changes or resets.
- Carrie knows this is normal and hates it too.
- First Male Kin treats the discrepancy as a simple factual defect.
- This becomes the origin of the later recurring line **"Machine lied again."**

**Final beat:** Laundromat Regular, without looking up, confirms that yes, that machine always lies.

---

### Page 17 — Once Is Enough
**Function:** Establish the issue's ordinary-rules mechanism.

**Cast:** Astrid, First Male Kin, Laundromat Regular.

**Beats:**
- Another laundry visit, compressed in time.
- The Viking household automatically follows the correction from Page 15.
- The regular notices but does not praise them.
- A new patron makes the same mistake they made previously.
- First Male Kin quietly shifts his own things out of the way rather than instructing the stranger.
- The household is becoming part of the room's normal behavior.

**Reserve the later witness line:** **"They folded chainmail once. I said no. They listened."** Do not use it as testimony here.

**Final beat:** the regular makes space for their basket without discussion.

---

### Page 18 — Machine Lied Again
**Function:** Repetition creates shared local humor.

**Cast:** First Male Kin, Laundromat Regular, Astrid.

**Beats:**
- Same unreliable machine.
- First Male Kin checks the display.
- Laundromat Regular sees the look before he speaks.
- **"Machine lied again."** becomes a natural shared complaint, not a Viking misunderstanding.
- The regular responds in kind.
- Astrid continues folding.

**Final beat:** everyone goes back to what they were doing. The joke belongs to the laundromat, not to the Vikings.

---

### Page 19 — Spicy Ones
**Function:** Add a second small neighborhood relationship without opening the full "separate lives" reveal.

**Cast:** Astrid, Upstairs Neighbor.

**Beats:**
- Hallway outside 5B.
- Astrid and the Upstairs Neighbor exchange packaged snacks as a recurring small ritual.
- Strong existing exchange available:
  - **Upstairs Neighbor: "You always bring the spicy ones."**
  - **Astrid: "You eat them."**
- Partial language and gesture are enough.
- Neither Carrie nor Bjorn needs to mediate, but this remains a tiny hallway relationship rather than a larger independent-life reveal.

**Final beat:** both continue in opposite directions without ceremony.

---

### Page 20 — Known Places
**Function:** Cross-cut the three vignette relationships and show recognition becoming distributed.

**Cast:** Bjorn, Coffee Vendor; Gunnar, Sal; Astrid/First Male Kin, Laundromat Regular; ordinary neighborhood people.

**Beats:**
- Coffee Vendor begins Bjorn's usual before Bjorn reaches the cart.
- Sal places Gunnar's familiar item on the counter.
- Laundromat Regular shifts the red cart to open the usual folding space.
- Upstairs Neighbor gives Astrid a casual greeting.
- No one treats any of this as notable.
- The Vikings also know what is expected at each place.

**Final beat:** each transaction takes fewer words than it once did.

---

### Page 21 — The Neighborhood Remembers
**Function:** Close on the issue's precise end state: not belonging declared, just familiarity accumulated.

**Cast:** ensemble in separate small glimpses; Carrie may be present in the final apartment beat but should not have a revelation.

**Beats:**
- Bodega counter: Gunnar's familiar item and the small carved token remain part of the ordinary counter landscape.
- Coffee cart: vendor serves the line; Bjorn receives his usual with minimal exchange.
- Laundromat: red cart, folding table, and household basket occupy familiar positions.
- Hallway: Astrid and Upstairs Neighbor pass with a routine greeting.
- Return to 5B: coffee, provisions, folded laundry and exchanged snack all enter the same household.
- Carrie sees the accumulation but reads it simply as a good day, not yet as evidence that her role is shrinking.

**Final beat:** exterior of the building/block at dusk. The Vikings are no longer visually the only story happening there. They are one recurring part of the neighborhood's ordinary rhythm.
