# Vikings 2026 — Issue 5: The Neighborhood
## Recovered source packet

Status: development source packet, not generation-authoritative until compiled into page recipes and registered in `data/shows.json`.

Placement intent: after **Issue 4 — The Iron Worm**, before the financial-legibility story.

Issue function: repeated ordinary contact begins turning the Vikings from conspicuous strangers into known neighborhood people. This issue is about recognition through routine, not full independence. Carrie is still important. The stronger later payoff—separate lives, library competence without her, and her realization that she has missed how much life is happening outside the case—is deliberately reserved for later Skaldic / Belonging material.

## Governing progression

**transaction -> repetition -> recognition -> relationship**

Do not frame these as "Viking discovers modern object" scenes. The durable comedy comes from two social systems successfully negotiating with each other through repetition.

# Vignette A — Bjorn's Coffee Guy

### Core purpose
Bjorn develops a favorite coffee vendor through repeated morning ritual. Recognition matters to him because remembered preference is evidence of a real social bond.

### Recovered fragments
- Gunnar: **"The coffee man controls the morning road."**
- Bjorn: **"The woman at the counter remembered my name."**
- Carrie: **"You ordered the same thing six times."**
- Bjorn: **"Then the bond is proven."**

The "woman at the counter" fragment preserves the mechanism, not the chosen recurring identity. Current direction is **Bjorn's favorite coffee guy**.

### Strong current-production material to salvage from the existing Skaldic Interface
- The vendor begins Bjorn's drink before Bjorn reaches the cart.
- Vendor: **"Regular, or the one you hated?"**
- Bjorn: **"Regular."**
- Gunnar: **"You have an enemy coffee."**
- Bjorn: **"I chose it once."**
- Vendor: **"That's how enemies start."**
- Bjorn notices that the vendor remembers.

### Intended evolution
1. First visit: Bjorn watches the order ritual and chooses by observing another customer.
2. Repeat visits: ordering compresses; fewer words are required.
3. Vendor anticipates Bjorn's usual order.
4. Bjorn recognizes this as reciprocal memory and therefore relationship.
5. Later issues can make the routine nearly wordless.

### Preserve
- Underplayed vendor.
- No "Viking discovers coffee" premise.
- Bjorn takes repeated exchange seriously without making a speech about friendship.
- Humor comes from how much social meaning Bjorn assigns to accurate repetition.

### Reserve for later
- Hearing-witness-style lines such as **"He knows the line now."**
- Any explicit proof-of-belonging testimony.
- Full "Carrie realizes they function without her" payoff.

# Vignette B — Gunnar and Sal

### Core purpose
Gunnar identifies a reliable merchant, tests the relationship, and gradually turns the bodega into part of his working map of the neighborhood.

### Recovered fragments
Exact observation:
> "The bodega man accepted three silver coins for the dried meat. He did not ask questions. I respect this. I have left him a small carved token. He has not acknowledged it. I respect this also."

Recovered exchange:
- Gunnar: **"We have need of provisions. The bureaucratic siege has begun. My jarl requires sustenance that does not require a hearth to prepare. We cannot risk a fire."**
- Sal: **"Right. No cooking. Got it. Cash or card?"**
- Gunnar: **"Tribute."**

Other recovered pieces:
- Five old MTA tokens appear in one development version.
- Gunnar calls them **"the currency of the under-realm."**
- Sal's attitude is effectively: fare is fare.
- Gunnar begins mapping the bodega itself.
- He marks an X near old taquitos because they have apparently been there since Tuesday.
- Gunnar evaluates the bodega cat as **"a worthy sentinel."**
- Earlier connective-tissue caption: **"I have made a friend. He does not know we are friends yet."**

### Intended evolution
1. Gunnar identifies Sal as a reliable source of provisions.
2. The first transactions are mismatched in language but successful in practice.
3. Gunnar studies stock, prices, reliability, traffic, and the cat with the same seriousness he gives routes.
4. He leaves a carved token as reciprocity.
5. Sal does not make a ceremony of it.
6. Sal's refusal to make a thing out of Gunnar makes Gunnar respect him more.

### Important correction to current Skaldic material
The active Skaldic Interface currently assigns the recurring bodega recognition scene to **Bjorn**. The recovered relationship is stronger and more specific as **Gunnar + Sal**. When Neighborhood becomes production data, the bodega relationship should be reassigned to Gunnar rather than duplicated.

### Preserve
- Sal is practical, busy, and not impressed enough to become a sitcom sidekick.
- Gunnar's competence: he is establishing supply, not failing to understand commerce.
- Modern cash/tokens/silver may appear during the progression, but the joke cannot remain "Gunnar doesn't understand money."
- The carved token is reciprocity, not mystical behavior.

### Reserve for later
Potential neighborhood/hearing witness fragments:
- **"They come in every Tuesday. She gets the crackers. He counts the coins wrong but less wrong now. They live here."**
- **"They come in. They pay. Mostly. The tall one stopped blocking the chips."**

Those are payoff evidence after repetition has accumulated, not early-vignette dialogue.

# Vignette C — Laundry / Ordinary Rules

### Core purpose
Show adaptation through small repeated rules that nobody needs to explain twice. The household begins accumulating people who recognize their habits.

### Recovered early laundry fragments
- Carrie: **"You come here once a week."**
- Gunnar: **"Why?"**
- Carrie: **"So you don't smell like war."**
- Gunnar: **"...That is fair."**

This is older/tutorial-style material. Use only if needed; the mature version should rely less on Carrie teaching basic life skills.

### Stronger later fragments
- Laundromat witness concept: **"They folded chainmail once. I said no. They listened."**
- Current Skaldic imagery: a male Kin member folds household laundry at a plastic table while watching television with regular patrons.
- Current recurring line: **"Machine lied again."**
- The Kin return with folded laundry as an ordinary household contribution.
- Upstairs-neighbor fragment: **"She brought me snacks. Too many, but nice."**

### Intended mechanism
**correction once -> behavior changes -> recognition accumulates**

The laundromat person does not become a teacher or comic foil. The meaningful fact is that a rule was stated once, the Vikings learned it, and subsequent encounters become ordinary.

### Possible third-vignette weave
Laundry can share space with two or three tiny neighborhood observations rather than carrying a single six-page machine joke:
- laundromat routine
- hallway snack exchange
- familiar local greeting
- returning home with clean clothes / groceries / coffee

### Preserve
- Kin agency.
- Practical learning.
- No full "everyone now has independent separate lives" reveal yet.
- Recognition remains ambient.
