## Issue function

Spring provisioning forces a direct comparison between Viking seasonal responsibility and New York's distributed food system. Bjorn and Gunnar correctly begin thinking about winter in spring; the city answers with continuous supply rather than household stores. When they try to supplement that system by growing food, public-land rules block the obvious solution. A spring storm then reverses the frame: the Vikings understand an immediate material danger better than the people around them and prepare without drama.

The issue does not solve Bjorn's provider crisis. It proves his competence is still real, then leaves him with the harder problem: ordinary provision depends on work he does not yet have.

## Locked character logic

### Bjorn
He is not anti-modern and does not reject the supply chain. He recognizes it as a strong system. His discomfort is that provision has been separated from his own labor and responsibility.

### Gunnar
He follows causal chains. "From where?" is practical curiosity, not paranoia. He notices the storm first from immediate physical signs.

### Carrie
She initially thinks winter planning in April is excessive, then accepts the agricultural logic. She gives real lawful alternatives when possible rather than merely saying no. She does not pretend community-garden access solves the current season.

### Astrid and the Kin
During the storm they show the same household competence established earlier. Bjorn does not command helpless dependents; everyone already knows how to secure a household against weather.
